# Chapter Generation Instructions

Write the requested curriculum chapter as usable study material. Shared standards are in [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md).

## Inputs and Coverage

Use the official syllabus for the boundary, the curriculum entry and any shared topic context for coverage, PYQs for depth and application, and authoritative references for facts. Address the assigned scope and explain the reasoning behind it. Synthesize explanations in your own words. Link related chapters rather than reteaching their full content.

## Required Output

- Identify the chapter, paper, curriculum entry, scope and actual prerequisites.
- Orient the reader to the topic and explain the content in a coherent, beginner-accessible sequence.
- Link key factual claims to sources and provide a short source list with titles, publishers, URLs and access dates.

Choose headings and teaching devices to suit the subject. State the learning outcome near the start, either in the opening information or a short learning-outcomes section; do not duplicate it in both. Glossaries, comparison tables and worked examples are useful when they aid understanding, not mandatory sections. Merge foundations into the explanation when that reads better.

## Shared Structure and Navigation

Maintain a recognisable structure across the curriculum without imposing an identical teaching skeleton on every topic or paper.

- Near the start, identify **Paper**, **Curriculum entry**, **Scope**, **Prerequisites** and **Learning outcome(s)** using those labels where applicable. Preserve meaningful authorship, revision and source dates; distinguish authored material from placeholders. A split part may use the chapter overview for shared context, but must identify its own scope and actual prerequisites.
- Orient the learner before developing the content. The body may follow a chronology, mechanism, derivation, map sequence, institutional comparison or other subject-appropriate organisation. Do not add empty sections or duplicate material merely to match another chapter.
- Use **Common confusions** as the shared label when collecting misconceptions or common mistakes. A separate section is optional when the distinctions are already taught clearly in context.
- Close teaching files with **Revision and next steps**, **Update watch**, then **Sources**, in that order. The revision section may reuse an existing readiness check, recap or retrieval prompts; include the next chapter/part or explain the appropriate route. These are unscored study prompts, not a newly generated formal assessment.
- Use consistent opening and closing labels while retaining useful subject-specific headings and numbering in the body. When renaming headings, preserve existing deep links with anchor aliases or update the affected references. Keep chapter filenames and curriculum numbering unchanged.
- For split chapters, retain the overview and ordered part links, identify `Part x of y`, and link between parts. Each teaching part needs its own relevant closing sections. A short overview may link to the parts' revision, update and source sections instead of repeating them.

When adjusting existing chapters, prefer small metadata, heading, navigation and maintenance edits. Reuse useful existing material, retain substantive explanations, worked examples, diagrams and source caveats, and avoid wholesale restructuring unless requested.

Use visual techniques when they help explain the subject: for example, maps, timelines, images, diagrams, flowcharts or simple ASCII illustrations. Suitable visuals may be sourced from the internet or created within the chapter. Exercise judgment about their form, placement and frequency; no visual quota or fixed format is required. Integrate visuals with the explanation and make labels legible. For externally sourced visuals, check accuracy and relevance, credit and link the original source, record the access date, and respect reuse terms.

For numerical or technical material, demonstrate the reasoning through worked examples where needed. For conceptual material, use concrete cases, contrasts and counterexamples. Let the subject determine emphasis: chronology and causation in history, institutional authority in polity, spatial processes in geography, measurement and mechanisms in economy, and so on. The shared opening and closing structure does not require an identical body template or word target.

Worked reasoning, annotated examples and brief checks of understanding may be embedded in teaching. Formal practice sets, scored tests and test feedback remain separate on-demand tasks.

## Updates and Maintenance

Apply the date distinctions in AGENTS.md. Include a visible **Update watch** near the end of every authored teaching chapter or part, immediately before **Sources**. Chapter 012's watch illustrates the intended specificity; copy its function, not its climate-specific items or length.

- For changing material, use a concise table with **Item**, **Why it may change** and **Revisit trigger**. Identify the relevant source, issuing authority or source section so the reader knows where to verify it. Cover the chapter's actual changing claims, not a generic instruction to check current affairs.
- For stable material, a short statement that no routine factual update is needed is sufficient. Mention a correction or convention-change trigger only when relevant; do not invent dynamic facts to fill a table.
- Retain observation periods for historical examples. A newer report may add a comparison without making an older observation false. Separate changes to a rule, current status or forecast from revisions to the historical record.
- Preserve source access dates and factual-verification dates. An editorial consistency pass is not a fresh fact-check and must not be presented as one. Record unresolved source/key discrepancies explicitly and give a verification trigger rather than silently treating them as settled.
- Inline dates and existing dynamic flags may remain, but do not replace the end-of-chapter watch. Literal `[DYNAMIC: ...]` tags are optional; clear dates, sources and triggers are required where relevant.

## Chapter Size and Parts

Default to the filename in the curriculum. Split at conceptual seams when a chapter is too large for one sitting or would teach better as distinct units, rather than at an arbitrary word count.

Use `<chapter>-part-N.md`. Each part identifies `Part x of y`, its title, prerequisites and links to the other parts. Update the curriculum index to reach every part.

## Completion Check

Before finalising:

- Verify consequential claims against the cited sources, including qualifications and exceptions; omit or qualify unsupported specifics. Check that simplifications preserve the underlying mechanism.
- Match PYQ explanations to the actual stem and every relevant option or matching row. Do not invent reasoning to fit an answer key; flag unresolved discrepancies using the corpus caveats.
- Check calculations, units, directions and consistency across prose, tables and visuals. Ensure promised skills are actually taught with enough reasoning or examples for a beginner, and check scope, prerequisites and local links.
- Check the shared opening information, closing-section order, specific Update watch, preserved source dates, and chapter/part navigation. For consistency-only edits, confirm that substantive teaching content and assessment records have been retained.

Keep the prose focused and avoid repetition that adds no teaching value.

# Chapter Generation Instructions

Write the requested curriculum chapter as usable study material. Shared standards are in [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md).

## Inputs and Coverage

Use the official syllabus for the boundary, the curriculum entry and any shared topic context for coverage, PYQs for depth and application, and authoritative references for facts. Address the assigned scope and explain the reasoning behind it. Synthesize explanations in your own words. Link related chapters rather than reteaching their full content.

## Required Output

- Identify the chapter, paper, curriculum entry, scope and actual prerequisites.
- Orient the reader to the topic and explain the content in a coherent, beginner-accessible sequence.
- Link key factual claims to sources and provide a short source list with titles, publishers, URLs and access dates.

Choose headings and teaching devices to suit the subject. Learning outcomes, glossaries, comparison tables and worked examples are useful when they aid understanding, not mandatory sections. Merge foundations into the explanation when that reads better.

Use visual techniques when they help explain the subject: for example, maps, timelines, images, diagrams, flowcharts or simple ASCII illustrations. Suitable visuals may be sourced from the internet or created within the chapter. Exercise judgment about their form, placement and frequency; no visual quota or fixed format is required. Integrate visuals with the explanation and make labels legible. For externally sourced visuals, check accuracy and relevance, credit and link the original source, record the access date, and respect reuse terms.

For numerical or technical material, demonstrate the reasoning through worked examples where needed. For conceptual material, use concrete cases, contrasts and counterexamples. Let the subject determine emphasis: chronology and causation in history, institutional authority in polity, spatial processes in geography, measurement and mechanisms in economy, and so on. Do not force a common paper template or word target.

Worked reasoning, annotated examples and brief checks of understanding may be embedded in teaching. Formal practice sets, scored tests and test feedback remain separate on-demand tasks.

## Updates and Maintenance

Apply the date distinctions in AGENTS.md. For genuinely changing material, add a concise update note or table when it helps future maintenance, identifying what to recheck and when. Historical observations can remain useful examples; neither literal `[DYNAMIC: ...]` tags nor a separate update section is required.

## Chapter Size and Parts

Default to the filename in the curriculum. Split at conceptual seams when a chapter is too large for one sitting or would teach better as distinct units, rather than at an arbitrary word count.

Use `<chapter>-part-N.md`. Each part identifies `Part x of y`, its title, prerequisites and links to the other parts. Update the curriculum index to reach every part.

## Completion Check

Before finalising:

- Verify consequential claims against the cited sources, including qualifications and exceptions; omit or qualify unsupported specifics. Check that simplifications preserve the underlying mechanism.
- Match PYQ explanations to the actual stem and every relevant option or matching row. Do not invent reasoning to fit an answer key; flag unresolved discrepancies using the corpus caveats.
- Check calculations, units, directions and consistency across prose, tables and visuals. Ensure promised skills are actually taught with enough reasoning or examples for a beginner, and check scope, prerequisites and local links.

Keep the prose focused and avoid repetition that adds no teaching value.

# Curriculum Generation Instructions

Create a study plan and linked chapter index for the requested paper. Shared audience, source, corpus and repository rules are in [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md).

## Inputs and Evidence

Use the paper's official syllabus, populated year-wise PYQ files and a sourced understanding of the exam pattern. Establish the format, marks, duration, scoring and qualifying or merit role before designing the plan. Verify current rules against official material; state any pending update or corpus limitation.

Use recurring PYQ themes and question demands to inform depth, alongside conceptual importance and learning dependencies. Keep the entire syllabus covered regardless of past frequency. Numerical weightage is optional: include it only when useful, supported and clear about classification and time period. External analyses can supplement the corpus; there is no source-count quota, and incompatible categories must not be averaged or presented as an official blueprint.

## Required Output

- A concise paper orientation: relevant exam facts, how to use the plan, and a manageable starting point.
- A phased progression from foundations to subject coverage, application and revision, adapted to the paper.
- A consolidated navigation index near the beginning, after the title and brief status/orientation. Group all chapters by phase in learning order; link each chapter title directly to its exact file and add a “more detail” link to a stable anchor at its detailed curriculum entry. Include every split part when applicable. Generate this index as part of the initial curriculum, without requiring a follow-up request.
- One authoritative detailed chapter list that also serves as the file index. Entries identify scope, learning outcome, important dependencies, exam relevance and useful PYQ references. Related entries may share context rather than repeat it.
- Guidance on essential resources and practice habits, including what each recommended resource is for. Make optional resources distinguishable from essentials.

Plan necessary terminology and bridge material without writing the chapters themselves. Use actual dependencies rather than assuming every earlier chapter is a prerequisite. A phase map or baseline self-check is useful when it adds orientation; the consolidated navigation index should repeat only titles and links, while scope and other planning details remain authoritative in the detailed entries.

## Balanced chapter granularity and consistent planning detail

Design the chapter sequence so substantive chapters provide a broadly comparable unit of learning across the curriculum. Calibrate boundaries to conceptual coherence, syllabus breadth, prerequisite structure and the explanation/application needed by a beginner. Compare chapters within the paper as a whole: a chapter should not silently absorb several independent learning sequences while a neighbouring chapter covers only one small fact set. Comparable granularity does not require identical chapter lengths, word counts, reading time or topic importance. Orientation, bridge, integration and revision modules may be deliberately shorter when their purpose calls for it. Do not set a target chapter count or divide material mechanically to achieve one.

Before finalising the index, map every syllabus element to a teaching home, then group related material into teachable units. Split a chapter when it combines distinct processes, periods, institutions or reasoning tasks that can be learned independently, or is too broad to teach coherently in one unit. Merge proposed chapters when either is too slight to sustain a meaningful learning outcome, duplicates the same explanation, or is merely a list of examples belonging with a shared concept. Split at a conceptual or chronological seam; merge around a coherent question or mechanism. Preserve the full syllabus and its appropriate depth through either decision.

Keep planning detail consistent across substantive chapter entries. Each entry must make clear:

- **Teaching scope and outcome:** what the chapter explains and what the learner should be able to explain, compare, interpret or apply afterward.
- **Prerequisites:** only the concepts or skills actually needed before beginning; distinguish these from useful context or later applications.
- **Boundary and hand-off:** what this chapter owns, what belongs in a named neighbouring chapter or another paper, and what may be briefly recalled without reteaching. Every excluded syllabus topic must still have a clear home or an explicit scope explanation.
- **Exam use and evidence:** the relevant question demand and useful PYQ anchors, or a clear syllabus-led reason for coverage when direct anchors are sparse. A cross-topic question does not make every component part of each linked chapter.

The wording and layout may suit the paper, but avoid entries that provide detailed boundaries for some chapters and only a topic label for others. State representative examples or the intended breadth where lists could otherwise become exhaustive. Define shared foundations once, then apply them in later chapters. Keep a brief curriculum-wide ownership map for recurring overlaps when it helps authors see which chapter teaches a mechanism and which chapter applies it. The detailed chapter entries remain authoritative; the consolidated navigation index stays concise.

Review granularity across the whole sequence, not just one chapter at a time. Check for overloaded and underdeveloped entries, orphaned syllabus elements, repeated explanations, prerequisites that pull in an entire unrelated branch, and applications that rely on unstated specialist knowledge. If actual chapter authoring reveals an unsuitable boundary, update all affected entries, dependencies, coverage mappings, filenames and both index views together. Use linked parts only when a single coherent chapter still benefits from separate sittings; parts do not excuse an unclear syllabus boundary.

## Paper-Sensitive Design

Use the following considerations where relevant, rather than as a common template:

- **Prelims GS-I:** map, timeline and data literacy; conceptual foundations; early MCQ reading and sound elimination.
- **CSAT:** comprehension, arithmetic, reasoning and data interpretation, with a bridge for learners lacking school-mathematics confidence.
- **Mains GS:** answer-writing practice alongside subject learning; gradually build relevant interlinkages. For GS IV, introduce ethical vocabulary and concrete dilemmas before advanced theory.
- **Essay:** reading, idea development, argument and paragraph organisation before full essays; calibrate to the relevant paper pattern.
- **Compulsory languages:** develop the skills named in the respective syllabus. Hindi requires translation in both directions; fluency alone does not establish familiarity with the examination.
- **Other papers:** derive the plan from that paper's syllabus, pattern and prerequisite chain instead of borrowing a GS structure.

Recommend a learning sequence without rigid deadlines, chapter-count targets or word quotas. Include current-affairs work only to the extent relevant to the paper, with an achievable entry routine for a beginner.

## Completion Check

Check full syllabus coverage, a workable prerequisite sequence, supported exam claims and usable resource guidance. Review chapter breadth across the whole curriculum: substantive units should have coherent, broadly comparable learning scope; short support modules should be purposefully short; splits and merges should resolve overload, fragmentation or repetition without changing required coverage. Confirm each substantive entry consistently states its scope/outcome, true prerequisites, boundary/hand-off and exam use/evidence. Check that exclusions have named homes, overlaps assign a clear teaching owner, and applications have no hidden prerequisites. Ensure every planned chapter has an exact filename and index link, with authored content and placeholders clearly distinguished. Verify that the consolidated navigation index and detailed entries contain the same chapters/parts in the same order, and that every file link and detail anchor resolves. Keep both views in sync when chapters are added, renamed or split. Keep teaching content in the later chapter stage.

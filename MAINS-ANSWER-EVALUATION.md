# Mains Answer Evaluation Instructions

Method for evaluating subjective Mains General Studies answers on demand for GS I–IV. Repository rules live in `AGENTS.md`; tests follow `MAINS-TEST-GENERATION.md`. This file does not cover Essay or compulsory language papers.

GS I–III answers are 10 or 15 marks. GS IV answers are 10-mark theory sub-parts or 20-mark case studies; a case study is scored as one 20-mark item, with its sub-parts allocated marks that add up to 20.

## Purpose

- Evaluate an attempted Mains test, question by question, against the pre-written evaluator guide.
- Use the same rubric across all three test modes; interpret expectations according to the question and mode.
- Report exact rubric marks, a band, and a confidence level.
- Diagnose content, analysis, examples, structure, time, and word management, then prescribe a rewrite and retest plan.
- Record the evaluation in the test file so the attempt, rubric, and diagnosis stay together.

UPSC does not publish official marking schemes or model answers. This is a transparent simulated examiner standard, not a claim of official UPSC marking.

## Inputs

- The test file: questions, marks, word limits, mode, scope, and evaluator guide.
- The learner's answers, typed or readable scans or photographs.
- The answer language.
- Time started and finished, if reported.
- Word counts, if reported; otherwise estimate and label the estimate.
- Do not edit the questions, evaluator guide, marks, or the learner's answers.

## Validate the Attempt

- Match each answer to its question number and sub-part. If the order is unclear, match by content only when unambiguous; otherwise ask.
- In every mode, treat every question and sub-part as compulsory. A blank answer or blank sub-part scores zero. In a GS IV case study, score the missing sub-part at zero and keep the case total out of 20.
- If a handwritten answer is unreadable, do not guess. Mark the unreadable portion and state how it limits evaluation.
- If timing or word count is missing, complete the content evaluation and mark time or length analysis as "not measured".
- If an answer is written in a language the evaluator cannot handle reliably, stop and ask rather than score it.

## Rubric

Score every answer item on five dimensions. Use 0.5-mark increments.

| Dimension | Weight | What it tests |
|---|---:|---|
| Demand and scope | 20% | Answers the exact directive; stays within the question's scope; does not drift |
| Content accuracy and coverage | 30% | Correct facts and concepts; required dimensions covered; no major omissions |
| Analysis and argument | 20% | Explanation, causes, effects, comparisons, trade-offs, judgment; not mere listing |
| Examples and evidence | 10% | Relevant cases, data, reports, judgments, maps, or diagrams used accurately |
| Structure and communication | 20% | Logical introduction, body, and closure; prioritisation; clarity; word-limit discipline |

Dimension maxima:

| Marks | Demand | Content | Analysis | Examples | Structure |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 10 | 2 | 3 | 2 | 1 | 2 |
| 15 | 3 | 4.5 | 3 | 1.5 | 3 |
| 20 | 4 | 6 | 4 | 2 | 4 |

Use the 10-mark and 15-mark rows for GS I–III. Use the 10-mark and 20-mark rows for GS IV. Do not use the 15-mark row for GS IV.

### Dimension anchors

- **Demand and scope:** full marks for answering the exact directive, addressing all its parts, and excluding irrelevant material. Low marks for misreading the directive, answering only one side, or drifting off-topic.
- **Content:** full marks for accurate, relevant, well-prioritised content covering the core dimensions. Low marks for factual errors, missing core dimensions, or unsupported assertion.
- **Analysis:** full marks for explaining mechanisms, causes, consequences, comparisons, trade-offs, and a reasoned judgment. Low marks for description, lists, or conclusions without reasoning.
- **Examples:** full marks for specific, relevant, correctly used evidence integrated into the argument. Low marks for generic, invented, unrelated, or decorative examples.
- **Structure:** full marks for a clear organisation, sensible prioritisation, and a conclusion that follows from the answer. Low marks for rambling, repetition, poor paragraphing, or severe word-limit problems.

### Case-study anchors

For GS IV case studies, apply the same dimensions to the case:

- **Demand and scope:** addresses every sub-part, including the required decision; identifies the relevant stakeholders and ethical issues; does not drift into general theory.
- **Content:** uses the facts of the case accurately; applies relevant ethical principles, values, laws, and administrative norms; does not invent facts.
- **Analysis:** identifies dilemmas and conflicts, evaluates at least two realistic options, states consequences and trade-offs, and gives a reasoned recommendation.
- **Examples:** uses relevant thinkers, principles, rules, precedents, or comparable cases accurately; does not decorate the answer with quotations.
- **Structure:** organises the answer around the case, not a textbook essay; covers issues, stakeholders, options, decision, justification, implementation, and safeguards.

A case study that gives no decision or no justification for the decision cannot score above 50%, even if the theory is accurate.

## Bands

Assign a band after the dimension marks:

| Band | Percentage | Descriptor |
|---|---:|---|
| A | 80–100% | Fully answers the directive; accurate, multi-dimensional, analytical, well-evidenced, and coherent |
| B | 60–79% | Largely answers the directive; adequate coverage and analysis; some evidence; clear but uneven |
| C | 40–59% | Partly relevant; largely descriptive; thin analysis or evidence; noticeable structural gaps |
| D | 20–39% | Misreads the directive or has major gaps or errors; assertion-heavy; poorly organised |
| E | 0–19% | Irrelevant, blank, or fundamentally wrong |

Use the dimension total as the primary score. Use the band as a cross-check. If the total and band conflict, re-read the answer and resolve the conflict with evidence.

## Hard Caps

Apply the lowest applicable cap; do not stack caps.

- Off-directive or misread directive: maximum 50% of the question's marks.
- Pervasive factual error, or a wrong core premise: maximum 40%.
- Severe irrelevance or padding that displaces required content: maximum 50%.
- Blank answer: 0.
- Unsupported assertions earn no content marks; do not invent supporting evidence for the learner.
- A missing conclusion lowers the structure score; it is not an automatic cap.
- A severe word-limit violation lowers the structure score and may expose content omissions; it is not an automatic zero.
- Do not penalise a valid alternative interpretation merely because it differs from the evaluator guide.
- Do not award marks for length, keyword density, or ornate language.

No negative marking applies to Mains.

## Paper-Specific Expectations

- **GS I–III:** use 10-mark and 15-mark rows. Do not add case-study requirements.
- **GS IV:** use 10-mark theory and 20-mark case-study rows. Judge case studies on stakeholder identification, ethical issues, options, decision, justification, implementation, and safeguards.
- Do not penalise a GS IV answer for omitting a 15-mark style when the paper uses 10/20.

## Mode-Specific Expectations

The rubric weights do not change by mode.

- **Chapter-specific:** reward depth and accuracy within the chapter. Do not require cross-chapter integration; relevant links are allowed but not compulsory.
- **Covered so far:** reward integration across completed chapters. Penalise tunnel vision when earlier completed material is directly relevant.
- **Full syllabus:** reward breadth, prioritisation, and integration across the paper. Do not require every chapter to appear in one answer, but penalise missing relevant dimensions.

Do not compare scores across modes as if they were equivalent. Record the mode in the evaluation.

## Evaluation Procedure

1. Read the question or case study, its evaluator guide, and the answer.
2. Extract evidence without scoring: the answer's claims, dimensions, examples, structure, omissions, word count, and time.
3. Score the five dimensions for the answer item.
4. Apply the lowest applicable cap.
5. Assign the band and confidence.
6. Write answer-item-level strengths and weaknesses with evidence.
7. Aggregate the total, then write the overall diagnosis and plan.

### Blind and two-pass rule

- Score the content before reading any self-score or self-assessment from the learner. Time and word count are separate management data; they must not change content marks.
- Use two passes: an evidence pass and a scoring pass. If the two passes differ by more than 1.5 marks on a 10-mark answer, 2 marks on a 15-mark answer, or 2.5 marks on a 20-mark case study, re-evaluate. If the disagreement remains, report a score range instead of false precision.

### Confidence

- **High:** readable answer, complete evaluator guide, unambiguous question, no material uncertainty.
- **Medium:** some ambiguity, incomplete guide, estimated word count, or minor readability issues.
- **Low:** significant unreadable portions, missing answer sections, defective question or guide, or unresolved scoring disagreement.

Confidence is metadata. Do not use it to inflate or deflate marks.

- Keep the score summary auditable: list every answer item, show dimension marks where needed, and ensure caps and totals add up. For GS IV, list each 10-mark theory sub-part separately and each case study as one 20-mark row.
- Quote or point to specific parts of the answer for every strength and weakness.
- Do not give a holistic "feel" score. Evaluate question by question, then aggregate.
- Do not reduce the evaluation to a percentage or rank.

## Model Answers

- Do not provide a full model answer by default.
- Give missing value points and a high-scoring outline after the evaluation.
- If the learner separately asks for a model answer, label it illustrative and not official.
- Do not let a model answer become the only acceptable answer.

## Recording Rules

- Append under `## Evaluation`; if the section is missing, add it at the end of the test file.
- If an evaluation already exists, append `### Evaluation — ... — Attempt N`. Never overwrite an earlier attempt.
- Label a revised diagnosis as a revision and keep the original.
- Do not create a separate evaluation file unless the operator explicitly asks.
- Do not change the test, evaluator guide, marks, or answers.
- Update the coverage ledger if the paper maintains one.
- Do not commit; commits happen only when the operator asks.

## Boundaries

- Do not cover Essay or language papers here; they need separate rubrics.
- Do not generate tests here; use `MAINS-TEST-GENERATION.md`.
- Do not use the 15-mark row for GS IV, or the 20-mark case-study row for GS I–III.
- Do not let a GS IV case study pass without a decision and justification.
- Do not fabricate answers, quotations, facts, or evidence.
- Do not score unreadable or missing answers by guessing.
- Do not claim official UPSC marks, rank, or percentile.
- Do not apply negative marking.
- Do not change the rubric after seeing a weak answer. If the guide is defective, flag it and version the correction.

# Mains English Paper B curriculum and chapter index

Prepared on 24 November 2026 for UPSC CSE 2027. **Planning stage.** All 25 linked chapter files are placeholders awaiting teaching content. The plan develops written English from accessible foundations through the four syllabus skills and complete-paper practice.

## Paper orientation and source status

English is qualifying Paper B. The 2026 notification assigns 300 marks and three hours, with a minimum qualifying standard of 25%, equivalent to 75 marks. Qualification is a condition for the merit papers to be taken into account. The standard is matriculation or equivalent. Preparation therefore targets dependable reading and written accuracy across the paper. [UPSC 2026 notification, Appendix I, Section II(B) and Section III(B)](https://upsc.gov.in/uploads/2026/05/Civil-Services-Preliminary-Examination-2026-Notice-E.pdf).

The [official syllabus](Mains-English-Syllabus.md) covers comprehension, précis, usage and vocabulary, and short essays. Its broad aim is understanding discursive writing and expressing ideas clearly and correctly. The shared syllabus file also reproduces the Indian-language subsection. Its translation requirement belongs to the Indian-language paper. English preparation follows its own four skill areas.

The local [2013–2026 PYQ files](Mains-English-PYQS/) are all extraction placeholders. They supply no populated working question corpus. This curriculum uses the 2025 paper reproduced in a hosted scan as an observed task blueprint and labels its references by question number. Direct official 2025 and 2026 paper links returned retrieval errors during preparation. The official 2026 examination page was accessible but did not expose its question-paper contents through the available retrieval. Consequently, the 2025 layout is a dated preparation baseline, with the 2026 detailed layout awaiting verification. No historical topic-frequency analysis is claimed. ## Observed 2025 paper blueprint

| Component | Observed allocation | Preparation implication |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 short essay | 100 marks, one of four topics, about 600 words | Develop a relevant, organized complete composition |
| Q2 comprehension | 75 marks, five questions | Read for argument and provide clear passage-grounded answers |
| Q3 précis | 75 marks, about one-third of the passage length, own wording, no title | Select essentials, compress faithfully and obey presentation directions |
| Q4 usage and vocabulary | 25 marks across correction, missing words, verb forms and antonyms | Apply grammar and contextual vocabulary |
| Q5 usage and vocabulary | 25 marks across directed rewriting, sentence use, word choice and idioms | Preserve meaning and demonstrate precise usage |

Total observed allocation is 300 marks. The paper requires responses to all questions while allowing the stated internal choice in Q1. Its instructions emphasize English responses and prescribed lengths. These allocations and the title rule belong to the observed 2025 paper and must be rechecked before constructing a later paper's practice contract. ## Starting route and how to use the plan

Start with 001. A learner who already reads and writes comfortably can demonstrate the outcomes of 002–012 with existing paper exercises and focus on weaknesses found. Speaking fluency alone cannot establish readiness for précis, written transformations or timed composition. A beginner should build sentence control while starting short, accessible reading. Comprehension can begin after 002, 006 and 010 are usable.

The recommended progression moves from sentence meaning to connected prose, then précis and short essays. Relevant foundations may be revisited alongside these branches. No GS curriculum, specialist literary study or prior Essay Paper I preparation is required. Chapter exercises, diagnostics and tests will be written later on request or during chapter authoring, following the relevant repository rules.

## Detailed chapter entries

References to the 2025 paper below refer to the hosted scan linked above. They are external PYQ anchors because the local year file is an extraction placeholder. Each substantive entry identifies its scope, outcome, true prerequisites and a named boundary.

### Phase 1 — Orientation and language foundations

<a id="chapter-001"></a>

#### 001. [Paper orientation and a baseline route](001-paper-orientation-and-a-baseline-route.md)

**Teaching scope and outcome.** Explain the qualifying role, instructions, skill areas and the difference between everyday fluency and successful written performance. Plan a baseline sampling reading, compression, usage and short composition, with later routing based on observed errors.

**Prerequisites.** None. Introduce examination and grammar vocabulary when first used.

**Boundary and hand-off.** Owns orientation and routing. Actual baseline questions and scoring are later assessment work. Full-paper pacing belongs to 023 and diagnosis to 025.

**Exam use and evidence.** Notification and syllabus-led. The 2025 paper supplies examples of the range of written tasks, without establishing the learner's proficiency.

**Planned practice outcome.** Keep an initial writing sample and identify skills needing direct study. A fluent learner may shorten a bridge only after demonstrating its outcome.

<a id="chapter-002"></a>

#### 002. [Sentence structure and grammatical roles](002-sentence-structure-and-grammatical-roles.md)

**Teaching scope and outcome.** Words, phrases, clauses, subjects, objects, complements and basic word classes. Identify the finite verb, build a complete sentence and distinguish sentence fragments from valid subordinate clauses.

**Prerequisites.** 001.

**Boundary and hand-off.** Owns the grammatical vocabulary used later. Verb time and form belong to 003, agreement to 004 and clause combinations to 006. Introduce terminology through ordinary sentences.

**Exam use and evidence.** Syllabus-led bridge supporting usage and all written responses. 2025 Q4(a) illustrates why identifying a sentence's structure precedes correction.

**Planned practice outcome.** Explain the structure of simple sentences and repair incomplete constructions without changing intended meaning.

<a id="chapter-003"></a>

#### 003. [Verbs time aspect and modality](003-verbs-time-aspect-and-modality.md)

**Teaching scope and outcome.** Base, past, participle and auxiliary forms; present, past and future reference; simple, progressive and perfect aspect; infinitives and gerunds; possibility, obligation and ability. Connect choices to time and meaning.

**Prerequisites.** 002.

**Boundary and hand-off.** Owns verb form, tense, aspect and modal meaning. Agreement is in 004, passive construction in 007 and reporting-related tense shifts in 008. Revisit forms selectively when errors show a gap.

**Exam use and evidence.** 2025 Q4(c) tests verb forms, with related corrections in Q4(a). Core language control also supports comprehension and précis.

**Planned practice outcome.** Choose forms from context, explain the time relation and correct verb sequences.

<a id="chapter-004"></a>

#### 004. [Agreement nouns pronouns and determiners](004-agreement-nouns-pronouns-and-determiners.md)

**Teaching scope and outcome.** Subject–verb agreement, number, countability, articles, determiners, pronoun reference and possessives. Find the governing subject even when intervening words obscure it.

**Prerequisites.** 002 and basic verb forms from 003.

**Boundary and hand-off.** Owns agreement and reference. Prepositions and modifier placement belong to 005, clause agreement applications to 006 and integrated correction to 012.

**Exam use and evidence.** 2025 Q4(a) includes agreement and article demands, while Q5(a) includes number transformation. Coverage is also led by the usage syllabus.

**Planned practice outcome.** Correct agreement and article choices, and resolve ambiguous pronoun reference with minimal necessary changes.

<a id="chapter-005"></a>

#### 005. [Prepositions modifiers and word relationships](005-prepositions-modifiers-and-word-relationships.md)

**Teaching scope and outcome.** Prepositions of time, place, movement and common verb/adjective combinations; adjective and adverb function; comparison, quantity and modifier placement. Use context to distinguish plausible alternatives.

**Prerequisites.** 002–004.

**Boundary and hand-off.** Owns grammatical relationships within and between phrases. Clausal connectors belong to 006 and idiomatic lexical combinations to 011. Teach manageable groups with examples instead of an exhaustive exception catalogue.

**Exam use and evidence.** 2025 Q4(b) tests missing words in context. Q4(a) also illustrates errors in relationships between words.

**Planned practice outcome.** Supply appropriate words and explain the relationship expressed. Repair modifier choices or placement when meaning becomes unclear.

<a id="chapter-006"></a>

#### 006. [Clauses connections and punctuation](006-clauses-connections-and-punctuation.md)

**Teaching scope and outcome.** Main and subordinate clauses, coordination, condition, reason, contrast, relative clauses and parallel structure. Use punctuation to represent questions, quotations and sentence boundaries.

**Prerequisites.** 002–005.

**Boundary and hand-off.** Owns combining and separating clauses for clear meaning. Directed transformations are practised in 009 and question tags in 008. Punctuation rules should be tied to meaning and observed mistakes.

**Exam use and evidence.** 2025 Q5(a) includes directed combinations and Q4(a) includes punctuation-sensitive correction. The syllabus requires clear written expression.

**Planned practice outcome.** Combine and separate sentences accurately, preserving the logical relation and repairing fragments or run-on constructions.

### Phase 2 — Transformations vocabulary and usage

<a id="chapter-007"></a>

#### 007. [Active and passive constructions](007-active-and-passive-constructions.md)

**Teaching scope and outcome.** Agent, receiver, transitive verbs and tense-preserving passive forms. Explain when the agent is retained or omitted and how emphasis changes while the core event remains the same.

**Prerequisites.** 002, 003 and 004.

**Boundary and hand-off.** Owns voice transformations. 009 integrates them with other directed changes. Précis in 018 may use voice changes when they aid concise expression and retain meaning.

**Exam use and evidence.** 2025 Q5(a)(v) provides a voice-transformation anchor.

**Planned practice outcome.** Convert eligible constructions in both directions and check tense, agreement, participants and meaning.

<a id="chapter-008"></a>

#### 008. [Reporting speech questions and question tags](008-reporting-speech-questions-and-question-tags.md)

**Teaching scope and outcome.** Direct and indirect speech, reporting statements, questions and requests; pronoun, time and place reference; context-sensitive backshift; auxiliary choice and polarity in tags.

**Prerequisites.** 003, 004 and 006.

**Boundary and hand-off.** Owns reporting and question formation. Sentence combining belongs to 009. Explain cases where backshift is unnecessary instead of applying an automatic tense substitution.

**Exam use and evidence.** 2025 Q5(a)(i) and Q5(a)(viii) anchor tags and reported speech.

**Planned practice outcome.** Report a short utterance in its context, form questions correctly and supply a tag that agrees with its clause.

<a id="chapter-009"></a>

#### 009. [Meaning-preserving sentence transformations](009-meaning-preserving-sentence-transformations.md)

**Teaching scope and outcome.** Sentence synthesis, simple/compound/complex alternatives, affirmative and negative equivalents, degree and quantity changes, correlatives and specified constructions. Follow a direction while preserving scope, time and logical relationships.

**Prerequisites.** 006–008.

**Boundary and hand-off.** Owns directed transformation as an integrated task. It recalls voice and reporting rules through application. Correction of erroneous sentences belongs to 012.

**Exam use and evidence.** 2025 Q5(a) supplies combinations, specified connectors and other directed changes. Inspect the original wording before setting a later exercise.

**Planned practice outcome.** Transform sentences under explicit directions and explain a change that is grammatical but alters the meaning.

<a id="chapter-010"></a>

#### 010. [Vocabulary from context and word formation](010-vocabulary-from-context-and-word-formation.md)

**Teaching scope and outcome.** Context clues, roots and common affixes, word families, part of speech, register, synonyms and antonyms. Distinguish approximate similarity from a substitute that fits a particular sentence.

**Prerequisites.** 002 and 004. Reading work from 013 can run alongside this chapter.

**Boundary and hand-off.** Owns vocabulary learning and contextual choice. Idioms and confusable words belong to 011, and passage-specific interpretation to 015.

**Exam use and evidence.** 2025 Q4(d) anchors antonyms. The vocabulary syllabus also justifies broader contextual learning without predicting a fixed item list.

**Planned practice outcome.** Record words with meaning, grammatical use and a self-written example, and test a proposed synonym against its context.

<a id="chapter-011"></a>

#### 011. [Idioms confusable words and productive usage](011-idioms-confusable-words-and-productive-usage.md)

**Teaching scope and outcome.** Common idioms and phrasal verbs, confusing word pairs, spelling distinctions and collocations. Construct sentences that demonstrate the intended meaning and respect a direction to retain the given word form.

**Prerequisites.** 005 and 010.

**Boundary and hand-off.** Owns lexical combinations and productive vocabulary. 005 teaches grammatical prepositions, while 012 integrates choices into correction. Avoid memorizing rare expressions without usable context.

**Exam use and evidence.** 2025 Q5(b)–(d) cover sentence use, contextual alternatives and idioms. These are observed task types from one paper.

**Planned practice outcome.** Use an expression in an unambiguous original sentence and justify a choice between commonly confused alternatives.

<a id="chapter-012"></a>

#### 012. [Correction and integrated usage practice](012-correction-and-integrated-usage-practice.md)

**Teaching scope and outcome.** Locate an error, identify the relevant rule and make the minimum change needed. Integrate grammar, word choice and punctuation, distinguishing a required correction from an optional stylistic rewrite.

**Prerequisites.** 002–011, with return routes to the specific rule missed.

**Boundary and hand-off.** Owns integrated language exercises and an error log. Editing a sustained essay is in 022 and general learning diagnosis in 025. Multiple defensible forms require attention to context.

**Exam use and evidence.** 2025 Q4(a) expressly limits unnecessary changes. Q4 and Q5 provide the observed usage task range.

**Planned practice outcome.** Correct mixed sentences, complete contextual blanks and explain the source of each substantive correction.

### Phase 3 — Discursive reading and comprehension

<a id="chapter-013"></a>

#### 013. [Reading discursive prose and tracing an argument](013-reading-discursive-prose-and-tracing-an-argument.md)

**Teaching scope and outcome.** Main ideas, paragraph roles, claims, reasons, examples, qualifications, reference words and connectives. Recognize how a writer develops a serious discussion, with unfamiliar terms explained through context.

**Prerequisites.** 002, 006 and 010. Full completion of every transformation chapter is unnecessary.

**Boundary and hand-off.** Owns the shared reading foundation for comprehension and précis. Answering questions belongs to 014–016 and selecting a compressed whole-text account to 017.

**Exam use and evidence.** Official syllabus-led. 2025 Q2 uses an extended discussion of reading and argument, making paragraph relationships material.

**Planned practice outcome.** Map a passage's central claim and the function of each paragraph, distinguishing main support from illustration.

<a id="chapter-014"></a>

#### 014. [Retrieving information and answering the question](014-retrieving-information-and-answering-the-question.md)

**Teaching scope and outcome.** Read question words, identify what is requested, locate supporting material and combine relevant details. Distinguish a request for explanation, comparison, enumeration or an author's view.

**Prerequisites.** 013.

**Boundary and hand-off.** Owns literal retrieval and question-to-evidence mapping. Implication and contextual interpretation belong to 015, final response editing to 016.

**Exam use and evidence.** 2025 Q2(a)–(c) provide observation, comparison and reading-rule demands. Answers must be grounded in the passage.

**Planned practice outcome.** Match each question to relevant passage evidence and outline an answer covering all requested parts.

<a id="chapter-015"></a>

#### 015. [Inference tone and meaning in context](015-inference-tone-and-meaning-in-context.md)

**Teaching scope and outcome.** Supported implication, unstated links, attitude and tone, reference resolution, contextual vocabulary and figurative meaning. Separate the author's claim from the reader's outside knowledge or agreement.

**Prerequisites.** 013 and 014.

**Boundary and hand-off.** Owns interpretive comprehension. General word learning remains in 010, précis fidelity in 017–019. Supply any conceptual context needed before asking a beginner to analyse a passage.

**Exam use and evidence.** 2025 Q2(d)–(e) ask for meanings within the passage. Tone and inference are syllabus-led preparation, with no claimed annual frequency.

**Planned practice outcome.** Explain a phrase using nearby reasoning and distinguish a warranted inference from a plausible external assumption.

<a id="chapter-016"></a>

#### 016. [Clear concise comprehension responses](016-clear-concise-comprehension-responses.md)

**Teaching scope and outcome.** Turn evidence into a complete answer, paraphrase accurately, retain key qualifications and match response length to the demand. Review relevance, expression and coverage of each question part.

**Prerequisites.** 012, 014 and 015.

**Boundary and hand-off.** Owns finished comprehension responses. 018 teaches sustained compression of a passage and 022 essay-level editing. Follow any printed length instruction rather than an invented universal answer length.

**Exam use and evidence.** 2025 Q2 asks for clear, correct and concise answers. Its response demands guide this application stage.

**Planned practice outcome.** Draft and revise responses, checking each substantive claim against the passage and each sentence against the question.

### Phase 4 — Precis selection compression and execution

<a id="chapter-017"></a>

#### 017. [Precis reading and selecting the essential argument](017-precis-reading-and-selecting-the-essential-argument.md)

**Teaching scope and outcome.** Purpose of a précis, authorial stance, central thought, hierarchy of supporting ideas, structure and necessary qualifications. Distinguish an account of the whole argument from a collection of interesting sentences.

**Prerequisites.** 013. Comprehension skills from 014–015 are useful context.

**Boundary and hand-off.** Owns content selection for précis. 018 owns compression and paraphrase, 019 owns a complete précis under printed instructions. Preserve the writer's position without adding commentary.

**Exam use and evidence.** 2025 Q3 supplies the précis task. Its substantial passage makes selection and proportion central.

**Planned practice outcome.** Create a hierarchy of main and supporting points and justify retaining or omitting a detail.

<a id="chapter-018"></a>

#### 018. [Paraphrasing compression and fidelity](018-paraphrasing-compression-and-fidelity.md)

**Teaching scope and outcome.** Combine related ideas, replace extended expressions with concise equivalents, generalize examples appropriately and preserve causal, contrasting and conditional relations. Avoid mechanical synonym replacement and distortions of certainty.

**Prerequisites.** 003, 006, 010 and 017.

**Boundary and hand-off.** Owns the mechanisms of compression and own-word expression. Full drafting, counting and presentation belong to 019. Necessary technical terms may be retained when substitutes would be inaccurate.

**Exam use and evidence.** 2025 Q3 requires own-word compression. The syllabus makes précis a distinct skill alongside comprehension.

**Planned practice outcome.** Compress a paragraph in stages, compare it with the original and repair lost qualifications or altered meaning.

<a id="chapter-019"></a>

#### 019. [Complete precis word control and instruction audit](019-complete-precis-word-control-and-instruction-audit.md)

**Teaching scope and outcome.** Draft a coherent précis, check proportional coverage and fidelity, and edit toward the stipulated length. Work out a target from the printed instruction, count consistently and obey any title and presentation directions.

**Prerequisites.** 017 and 018, with language revision skills from 012.

**Boundary and hand-off.** Owns complete précis execution. 023 handles paper-wide time allocation. Title requirements must be read afresh for each paper, rather than inherited from a generic précis template.

**Exam use and evidence.** 2025 Q3 specifies approximate one-third compression and expressly excludes a title. This is an observed instruction requiring rechecking in later papers.

**Planned practice outcome.** Produce a complete précis under the actual prompt, then audit meaning, language, length and title compliance.

### Phase 5 — Short essays and clear composition

<a id="chapter-020"></a>

#### 020. [Short-essay interpretation and planning](020-short-essay-interpretation-and-planning.md)

**Teaching scope and outcome.** Interpret the topic, set manageable scope, choose a central position and organize relevant points. Develop examples from accessible knowledge and personal observation, checking factual assertions and avoiding memorized digressions.

**Prerequisites.** 013 and clear sentence skills from 002–006.

**Boundary and hand-off.** Owns the short-essay outline. Connected paragraphs belong to 021 and full drafting to 022. Essay Paper I's separate curriculum develops its own longer paper practice.

**Exam use and evidence.** 2025 Q1 offers concrete and reflective choices and an approximately 600-word essay. Topic choice requires a coherent response within the printed demand.

**Planned practice outcome.** Produce alternative outlines and select one whose main point, support and boundaries can be explained clearly.

<a id="chapter-021"></a>

#### 021. [Paragraph development and connected composition](021-paragraph-development-and-connected-composition.md)

**Teaching scope and outcome.** Paragraph unity, supporting reasons, accurate illustration, transitions, proportion, openings and endings. Connect explanation to examples and develop a coherent sequence in plain English.

**Prerequisites.** 006, 012 and 020.

**Boundary and hand-off.** Owns the development of the short composition from an outline. 022 integrates drafting and editing. Essay Paper I chapters may offer additional practice without becoming prerequisites.

**Exam use and evidence.** The short-essay syllabus and 2025 Q1 motivate sustained organization and clear expression.

**Planned practice outcome.** Develop an outline into linked paragraphs, with an introduction that establishes the subject and a conclusion supported by the discussion.

<a id="chapter-022"></a>

#### 022. [Complete short essays and revision](022-complete-short-essays-and-revision.md)

**Teaching scope and outcome.** Write a complete short essay, maintain relevance, select adequate detail and revise structure, sentence accuracy and word choice. Learn to approximate and check the required length using observed handwriting density.

**Prerequisites.** 020 and 021, with correction skills from 012.

**Boundary and hand-off.** Owns full short-essay performance. 023 adds whole-paper constraints and 025 supplies longer-term remediation. Length and topic rules come from the selected paper.

**Exam use and evidence.** 2025 Q1's printed essay instruction provides the observed practice boundary. Its general instructions require attention to length.

**Planned practice outcome.** Write and revise a complete response to a selected existing topic, preserving the outline and draft for diagnosis.

### Phase 6 — Paper practice and targeted revision

<a id="chapter-023"></a>

#### 023. [Instructions time allocation and booklet execution](023-instructions-time-allocation-and-booklet-execution.md)

**Teaching scope and outcome.** Read compulsory-task and internal-choice directions, question numbering and length requirements. Budget time for every component, record actual reading and handwriting pace and reserve checking time.

**Prerequisites.** 016, 019 and 022, plus integrated language skills from 012.

**Boundary and hand-off.** Owns examination execution. 024 owns the combined sitting and 025 subsequent improvement. Allocate time from observed performance instead of treating marks alone as a clock.

**Exam use and evidence.** 2025 front instructions and Q1–Q5 supply the paper-specific audit. The notification supplies the three-hour baseline.

**Planned practice outcome.** Create and trial a personal time budget, practise orderly numbering and check that no required task was accidentally omitted.

<a id="chapter-024"></a>

#### 024. [Full-paper integration and qualifying readiness](024-full-paper-integration-and-qualifying-readiness.md)

**Teaching scope and outcome.** Combine comprehension, précis, usage and short composition in a complete sitting. Track completion, instruction compliance, accuracy and deterioration under time pressure. Distinguish reported practice marks from an official result.

**Prerequisites.** 012, 016, 019, 022 and 023.

**Boundary and hand-off.** Owns integrated practice from an existing original paper. New tests and numerical evaluation require separately declared paper-specific methods and a request. 025 determines the repair route.

**Exam use and evidence.** 2025 original paper provides a usable observed blueprint. Future practice must follow the instructions of the chosen paper. Local year files remain unpopulated.

**Planned practice outcome.** Complete a paper, preserve responses and timing notes and identify which skill gaps threaten consistent qualification.

<a id="chapter-025"></a>

#### 025. [Targeted remediation revision and maintenance](025-targeted-remediation-revision-and-maintenance.md)

**Teaching scope and outcome.** Diagnose recurring errors from written evidence, prioritize the underlying skill and test the repair in a new context. Maintain reading, vocabulary and short writing without a rigid schedule or expanding word lists indefinitely.

**Prerequisites.** 001 and an available written attempt. A full sitting from 024 adds evidence when ready.

**Boundary and hand-off.** Owns continuing revision. Return to 002–009 for grammar, 010–012 for lexical control, 013–016 for reading, 017–019 for précis and 020–022 for composition.

**Exam use and evidence.** The four official skill areas organize revision. The 75/300 qualifying baseline guides the goal, with practice marking kept distinct from official decisions.

**Planned practice outcome.** Revise the weakest evidenced skill, compare an earlier and later attempt and update the study route from observable improvement.

A dictionary such as Cambridge Dictionary or Oxford Learner's Dictionaries can serve the lexical role. A school grammar already available to the learner can serve the reference role. These are resource choices, with no purchase or cover-to-cover completion prerequisite.

Begin with a short reading passage and a small amount of written response. Check several actual language choices from that response rather than collecting unrelated word lists. As confidence develops, alternate comprehension responses with précis selection and short-essay outlining. Introduce complete tasks after their component skills are usable.

For every précis, compare meaning with the original as well as checking language and length. Read title instructions afresh. For comprehension, trace each answer to its textual support. For vocabulary sentences, make the intended sense apparent without relying on an evaluator to infer it. For short essays, connect each paragraph to the topic and reserve time for revision.

Contemporary issues can supply accessible reading and essay examples. No dedicated current-affairs syllabus is required for this qualifying language plan. Technical subject knowledge should be explained when used in instructional passages.

## Optional support and routes for observed difficulty

If sentence control is weak, use additional school-level exercises for 002–006 before increasing passage complexity. If grammar exercises go well but writing remains unclear, revisit 013 and 020–022 with actual prose. If comprehension is accurate but précis fails, return to selection and compression in 017–018. A fluent reader can need substantial practice in the latter even when foundation exercises are easy.

CSAT comprehension or Essay Paper I writing may provide extra practice where the skill overlaps. The English paper's own instructions remain the basis for its practice. English literature optional, literary criticism and advanced linguistic theory are outside this qualifying-paper scope.

Progress to a full sitting after representative tasks in all four areas can be completed accurately. Once full attempts are available, plan time using actual performance and identify a dependable margin above the qualifying baseline. The size of that margin is a practical decision based on marking uncertainty and performance variation. No numerical practice result guarantees an official outcome.

## 1. Understand the paper and start small

GS I connects Indian culture and history, world history, society and geography. Preparation should develop the ability to explain a process, compare cases and support a judgement with relevant examples. Knowing names and dates is useful when it helps that explanation.

The rule baseline is the [UPSC CSE 2026 notification](https://www.upsc.gov.in/sites/default/files/Notif-CSP-2026-Engl-060226Rev.pdf), Appendix I, Sections II(B) and III(B):

| Feature | Preparation baseline |
|---|---|
| Identity and role | GS I is **Mains Paper II**, a descriptive paper carrying **250 marks** and counting towards merit. It is distinct from Prelims GS I and from qualifying language papers. |
| Duration | **Three hours**. |
| Merit context | The written merit papers total 1,750 marks; the personality test adds 275. UPSC retains discretion to set qualifying marks in merit papers. |
| Language | Questions are set in Hindi and English. Answers must follow the authorised medium; this curriculum uses English. Selecting Hindi for Paper A does not itself select the GS answer medium. |
| Expected work | Understanding, analysis and relevant, concise answers; specialised optional-subject study is not the GS standard. |

For practice planning, use the recent full-paper pattern recorded in the repository's [Mains test method](../MAINS-TEST-GENERATION.md): **20 compulsory questions, ten of 10 marks/150 words and ten of 15 marks/250 words**, totalling 250. Mains uses descriptive evaluation, not the Prelims one-third wrong-option deduction; irrelevant content still does not earn credit. The official archive lists the 2026 paper, but its PDF and the 2025 PDF could not be retrieved during this preparation. Accordingly, the detailed question split is a previously sourced repository baseline, not a fresh verification of the 2026 booklet. Check the 2027 notification, corrigenda and actual paper instructions before applying these rules.

**First study session:** read the short paper syllabus, open a past paper to see the kinds of tasks asked, and set up a timeline page, an atlas and a notebook for unfamiliar words. Begin with 001–002, then the map bridge in 003. Evidence literacy is included in 001. Do not attempt to answer an entire paper before learning the subjects. Once a topic is understood, use 004 to explain it in a short paragraph and then an answer outline. There is no fixed daily chapter target or deadline.

## 2. Evidence, priorities and limits

The working corpus is the [local 2013–2026 GS I collection](Mains-General-Studies-I-PYQS/). All year files contain extracted questions, with Anantam IAS source links, access date 11 November 2026 and warnings to compare exact wording with the official paper. These are **secondary transcriptions**, not certified official copies. They omit booklet instructions and per-question marks/word limits; earlier formats also differ. Use the original for timed full-paper practice, disputed wording and question subdivisions. The corpus has not been re-extracted or altered for this curriculum.

The following are qualitative interpretations of that corpus, not an official blueprint or a forecast:

- **Culture requires features plus context:** explain how evidence, patronage and social life illuminate art or literature. Compare [2014 Q2](Mains-General-Studies-I-PYQS/2014-Mains-General-Studies-I.md) on Gandhara, [2021 Q1](Mains-General-Studies-I-PYQS/2021-Mains-General-Studies-I.md) on Bhakti literature and [2024 Q11](Mains-General-Studies-I-PYQS/2024-Mains-General-Studies-I.md) on Chola achievements.
- **History requires causes, contributions and change:** connect colonial economic effects, mobilisation and nation-building rather than memorising isolated biographies. Anchors include [2014 Q6–8](Mains-General-Studies-I-PYQS/2014-Mains-General-Studies-I.md), [2021 Q3, Q11–12](Mains-General-Studies-I-PYQS/2021-Mains-General-Studies-I.md) and [2024 Q3, Q12–13](Mains-General-Studies-I-PYQS/2024-Mains-General-Studies-I.md). World history and post-independence history retain full coverage even when a particular year's paper offers few examples.
- **Society requires qualified reasoning:** explain continuity alongside change, differences within groups, and the relation between structures and lived experience. See [2018 Q8, Q18–20](Mains-General-Studies-I-PYQS/2018-Mains-General-Studies-I.md), [2021 Q20](Mains-General-Studies-I-PYQS/2021-Mains-General-Studies-I.md) and [2024 Q8–9, Q17–20](Mains-General-Studies-I-PYQS/2024-Mains-General-Studies-I.md).
- **Geography joins mechanism, location and consequence:** draw a process, locate examples, then explain human or environmental effects. See [2014 Q17–24](Mains-General-Studies-I-PYQS/2014-Mains-General-Studies-I.md), [2021 Q4, Q14–16](Mains-General-Studies-I-PYQS/2021-Mains-General-Studies-I.md) and [2024 Q4, Q6, Q14–16](Mains-General-Studies-I-PYQS/2024-Mains-General-Studies-I.md).

Some questions cross paper boundaries: for example, [2025 Q9 and Q15](Mains-General-Studies-I-PYQS/2025-Mains-General-Studies-I.md) concern civil-service ethos and spatial technology. Treat such items as applications of relevant GS I foundations after checking the original; they do not justify replacing the GS I syllabus with a full governance or technology course. A question's assertion is something to examine, not automatically a fact to repeat. No numerical topic weightage is inferred from this overlapping corpus.

One encountered wording concern needs care: [2015 Q2](Mains-General-Studies-I-PYQS/2015-Mains-General-Studies-I.md) uses “Mesolithic rock cut architecture.” Do not infer that prehistoric rock paintings and excavated architectural monuments are the same category. The original wording needs checking before exact quotation or assessment; this plan keeps rock art and rock-cut architecture distinct and leaves the corpus unchanged.

## 3. Learning route and chapter boundaries

The reorganisation separates large historical sequences and physical processes, and merges overlapping study guidance. The chapter count is a result of those boundaries, not a workload target. A chapter is a coherent learning unit, not a promise of identical reading time. Later authoring may still reveal a need for parts under the [chapter method](../CHAPTER-GENERATION.md).

| Phase | Chapters | Progression |
|---|---|---|
| A — Foundations and communication | 001–004 | Read time, maps and evidence; build a supported answer once a subject topic is learned. |
| B — Indian heritage and culture | 005–017 | Move from evidence and cultural foundations to forms, modern change and conservation. |
| C — Modern India and consolidation | 018–033 | Separate structural explanations from movement chronology, then study independent India through bounded periods. |
| D — World history | 034–043 | Learn ideas and industrial/imperial contexts before wars, liberation and changing world order. |
| E — Indian society | 044–057 | Learn social concepts before group relations, demographic change and applied social problems. |
| F — Physical, resource and economic geography | 058–081 | Learn processes before regional applications; study oceans before monsoon interactions and resources before location cases. |
| G — Application and revision | 082–083 | Combine learned subjects, practise paper execution and use evidence of errors to revise. |

After the short foundations, culture can begin at 005, modern history at 018, world history at 034, society at 044 and geography at 058. These are independent branches, not prerequisites for one another. Follow the specific prerequisite list in each entry. A boundary reference is a hand-off or later application, not an additional prerequisite. A brief contextual bridge is sufficient when an example draws on an uncompleted branch.

Use 004 from the first learned topic and return to it for diagrams and more complex arguments. 082 can begin with any relevant completed subjects; full-paper work in 083 requires adequate syllabus breadth, though the revision habits apply earlier. 001, 004, 082 and 083 are reusable support/application modules, not automatically suitable for standalone chapter tests. All assessment follows the existing readiness rules in the [Mains test method](../MAINS-TEST-GENERATION.md); this plan creates none.

For chapter generation, apply each entry as follows:

- **Teach and outcome** define the material for which that chapter is responsible. Explain its vocabulary and intermediate reasoning for a beginner; do not replace teaching with a list of examples.
- **Boundary and hand-off** identify where adjacent material is taught in depth. Briefly recall what is needed and link there rather than repeat the full explanation. An excluded topic must have a named home here or an explicit cross-paper boundary.
- Give representative comparisons sufficient to explain the concept and its variations. Do not turn regional examples into exhaustive inventories of dynasties, monuments, schemes, factories or species.
- For a mixed PYQ, teach the part belonging to this chapter and identify the other needed chapter. A PYQ anchor does not expand the chapter to cover every possible aspect of that question.
- Keep causal mechanisms in their home chapters and apply them elsewhere. New contextual information needed by an application should be supplied briefly; do not assume an unlisted specialist prerequisite.
- Preserve period boundaries. The integration/reorganisation chapter is deliberately thematic through the preparation cutoff; later chronological chapters refer to it for territorial changes. Geography distinguishes process, distribution and human consequences; society distinguishes institutions, mechanisms and interventions.

These boundaries guide authoring alongside the official syllabus and [chapter method](../CHAPTER-GENERATION.md). If substantive teaching reveals an unsuitable boundary, revise the affected entries together so the hand-off remains complete. Do not silently drop a topic or duplicate it in both chapters.

### Phase A — Foundations and communication

<a id="chapter-001"></a>

#### [001 — Study workflow and evidence literacy](001-study-workflow-and-evidence-literacy.md)

- **Teach:** Paper orientation, syllabus-to-question mapping, retrieval, source quality, observation dates, percentages/rates and denominators, correlation versus causation, and a compact evidence/error record. Teach numerical vocabulary through elementary examples.
- **Outcome:** Organise a topic and distinguish a supported claim from an assertion or misleading statistic.
- **Boundary and hand-off:** Keep this a short reusable support module. Historical source criticism belongs to 002/005; social concepts to 044; answer construction to 004. Do not repeat the curriculum’s full exam orientation.
- **Prerequisites:** None.
- **Exam use / PYQ anchors:** [2024 Q17, Q20](Mains-General-Studies-I-PYQS/2024-Mains-General-Studies-I.md).

<a id="chapter-002"></a>

#### [002 — Historical time and the Indian chronology bridge](002-historical-time-and-the-indian-chronology-bridge.md)

- **Teach:** BCE/CE, centuries, timelines, primary/secondary evidence, causation, continuity and change; a selective ancient–medieval–modern Indian timeline locating major regions, institutions and belief traditions.
- **Outcome:** Place an unfamiliar event or cultural work in a defensible historical context.
- **Boundary and hand-off:** Supply landmarks, not a compressed dynasty survey. Religious ideas are taught in 006, literary evidence in 007, and modern political narrative from 018.
- **Prerequisites:** None.
- **Exam use / PYQ anchors:** [2018 Q2](Mains-General-Studies-I-PYQS/2018-Mains-General-Studies-I.md); [2024 Q1](Mains-General-Studies-I-PYQS/2024-Mains-General-Studies-I.md).

<a id="chapter-003"></a>

#### [003 — Maps, scale and spatial reasoning](003-maps-scale-and-spatial-reasoning.md)

- **Teach:** Directions, latitude/longitude, scale, legends, relief and thematic maps; continents, oceans, India and neighbours; historical versus present boundaries and simple map/diagram conventions.
- **Outcome:** Locate evidence and read a map without confusing physical processes, political boundaries or scale.
- **Boundary and hand-off:** Teach map use, not regional geography inventories. World/Indian physical patterns belong to 063/064; processes begin in 058.
- **Prerequisites:** None.
- **Exam use / PYQ anchors:** [2021 Q8, Q14](Mains-General-Studies-I-PYQS/2021-Mains-General-Studies-I.md).

<a id="chapter-004"></a>

#### [004 — Question demand and analytical communication](004-question-demand-and-analytical-communication.md)

- **Teach:** Directives, qualifiers and sub-parts; claims, reasons, examples, comparison and judgement; concise paragraphs, lists, timelines and labelled diagrams; planning and revising an answer to a learned topic.
- **Outcome:** Produce a relevant answer whose structure and evidence serve the question.
- **Boundary and hand-off:** Merge introductory and later presentation guidance here, returning after subject learning for harder examples. Do not reteach atlas skills or prescribe a universal introduction–body–conclusion formula. Mixed-theme synthesis belongs to 082, full-paper management to 083.
- **Prerequisites:** 001.
- **Exam use / PYQ anchors:** [2021 Q4, Q12, Q20](Mains-General-Studies-I-PYQS/2021-Mains-General-Studies-I.md).

### Phase B — Indian heritage and culture

<a id="chapter-005"></a>

#### [005 — Cultural evidence and Harappan civilisation](005-cultural-evidence-and-harappan-civilisation.md)

- **Teach:** Archaeology, artefacts, inscriptions, coins and texts as different evidence types; Harappan settlements, urban planning, crafts, exchange and cultural practices with limits of inference.
- **Outcome:** Explain Harappan features and assess what surviving material can establish.
- **Boundary and hand-off:** Own the introduction to material evidence; later chapters apply it briefly. Prehistoric painting belongs to 013, later urban conservation to 017 and social urbanisation to 053.
- **Prerequisites:** 002, 003.
- **Exam use / PYQ anchors:** [2014 Q1](Mains-General-Studies-I-PYQS/2014-Mains-General-Studies-I.md); [2025 Q1](Mains-General-Studies-I-PYQS/2025-Mains-General-Studies-I.md).

<a id="chapter-006"></a>

#### [006 — Early Indian society and religious traditions](006-early-indian-society-and-religious-traditions.md)

- **Teach:** Vedic/later-Vedic social change; Buddhism, Jainism and other major philosophical/religious traditions at introductory level; institutions, ethics, patronage and cultural implications.
- **Outcome:** Connect beliefs and social setting to cultural production without reducing traditions to slogans.
- **Boundary and hand-off:** Own concepts and historical setting, not detailed philosophical systems. Textual genres and learning centres belong to 007; monuments and iconography to 008/009.
- **Prerequisites:** 002.
- **Exam use / PYQ anchors:** [2023 Q11](Mains-General-Studies-I-PYQS/2023-Mains-General-Studies-I.md); [2024 Q1](Mains-General-Studies-I-PYQS/2024-Mains-General-Studies-I.md).

<a id="chapter-007"></a>

#### [007 — Early literature and knowledge traditions](007-early-literature-and-knowledge-traditions.md)

- **Teach:** Sanskrit, Pali, Prakrit and Tamil/Sangam literary traditions; genres, audiences, oral/textual transmission, centres of learning and selected scientific/knowledge traditions. Read texts as situated evidence.
- **Outcome:** Explain literary contributions and use a text cautiously to reconstruct society.
- **Boundary and hand-off:** Use beliefs from 006 rather than reteaching them. Medieval devotional/Persian/regional literature belongs to 012, modern language and print cultures to 016. Avoid author-title catalogues without interpretation.
- **Prerequisites:** 002, 006.
- **Exam use / PYQ anchors:** [2013 Q1](Mains-General-Studies-I-PYQS/2013-Mains-General-Studies-I.md); [2014 Q3](Mains-General-Studies-I-PYQS/2014-Mains-General-Studies-I.md).

<a id="chapter-008"></a>

#### [008 — Stupas, caves and early sculpture](008-stupas-caves-and-early-sculpture.md)

- **Teach:** Mauryan through Gupta-era patronage and artistic developments; inscriptions, pillars, stupas, excavated caves and Gandhara/Mathura/Amaravati sculpture; form, function, material and cultural exchange.
- **Outcome:** Compare representative works and explain religious, technological and intercultural influences.
- **Boundary and hand-off:** Own early monuments and sculpture. Prehistoric rock paintings are not rock-cut buildings and belong to 013. Temple-style systems belong to 009; general archaeological method stays in 005.
- **Prerequisites:** 005, 006.
- **Exam use / PYQ anchors:** [2014 Q2](Mains-General-Studies-I-PYQS/2014-Mains-General-Studies-I.md); [2026 Q1](Mains-General-Studies-I-PYQS/2026-Mains-General-Studies-I.md).

## 6. Essential resources and sustainable practice

**Essential starting kit:** the official syllabus, a usable atlas, the local PYQs with their caveats, original papers from the [UPSC archive](https://www.upsc.gov.in/examinations/previous-question-papers), and a small set of foundation readings. Use an atlas to locate every significant place encountered; verify historical boundaries for the period rather than drawing a modern map as though it were timeless.

Use [NCERT textbooks](https://ncert.nic.in/textbook.php) by topic. The table is a reading route, not an instruction to finish every book before beginning Mains work. Editions and retained chapters vary; match the topic to the curriculum and fill genuine omissions. Recommended uses are curriculum judgements. Official catalogue search results identify the titles, but the full catalogue/book PDFs were not all retrievable in this session.

| Foundation resource | What to use it for |
|---|---|
| School history/geography/social-science introductions where needed | Recover chronology, maps and basic terms before advanced passages; use only the sections needed for the bridge. |
| NCERT *An Introduction to Indian Art*, Parts I–II, and selected *Themes in Indian History* | Images, form and historical context for 005–017; use selected historical themes to support 019–029. Supplement gaps in performing arts and regional literature. |
| NCERT *India and the Contemporary World*, Parts I–II, and *Themes in World History* | Accessible entry into revolutions, nationalism, industrialisation and world change. Check that later wars and decolonisation are adequately covered before closing D. |
| NCERT *Politics in India since Independence* | Consolidation, democratic development and regional aspirations for 030–033; extract historical explanations, not a complete political-science optional course. |
| NCERT *Indian Society* and *Social Change and Development in India*; selected introductory sociology if terms remain unfamiliar | Concepts and illustrative cases for E. Update empirical examples from primary evidence; textbook data retain their original observation period. |
| NCERT *Fundamentals of Physical Geography*, *India: Physical Environment*, *Fundamentals of Human Geography* and *India: People and Economy* | Processes, maps and locational reasoning for F. Pair diagrams with an explanation and compare Indian and world examples. |

**Essential gap-filling:** foundation texts alone need not cover every indexed topic. The free [NIOS History course](https://digital.nios.ac.in/topic.php?id=315en) supplies a useful bridge for colonial India and world history. Its lessons 23–26 cover the First World War/Russian Revolution, interwar period/Second World War, Cold War and decolonisation; select these where the available NCERT edition leaves gaps. For a fuller freedom-struggle narrative, Bipan Chandra and co-authors’ [*India’s Struggle for Independence*](https://www.penguin.co.in/book/indias-struggle-for-independence/) is an optional supplement for 022–029; its 1857–1947 scope does not replace the earlier colonial history or post-independence chapters. For culture, consult authoritative descriptions of specific traditions and monuments. Choose one main explanatory source per gap; no commercial book is an official syllabus.

**Targeted primary references, used when a chapter needs them:** [Archaeological Survey of India](https://asi.nic.in/) for monuments and archaeological context; [Census of India](https://censusindia.gov.in/census.website/) for population definitions and dated tables; [India Meteorological Department](https://mausam.imd.gov.in/) for weather/climate explanations and event evidence. Use the underlying table, report or site description when citing a fact, not merely a portal homepage. These are reference destinations, not additional cover-to-cover reading. ASI access timed out; the Census and IMD homepages were accessible. Specific dataset releases and heritage inventories have not been audited here.

**Optional:** an additional explanatory history, geography or culture book, a lecture for a difficult process, or feedback from a teacher. Add it for an observed gap in understanding or coverage. Optional-level theory and multiple coaching compilations are not prerequisites.

Keep three compact working records:

- **Concept notes:** a timeline, comparison or causal diagram with a few well-understood examples. Build explanations before abbreviating them into revision notes.
- **Evidence notes:** the claim, exact source, observation period, publication/access date and limits. Separate a survey estimate from a census count, a projection from an observation, and a national average from regional variation. Avoid undated statistics and unsupported precision.
- **Writing review:** question/year, demand, what the answer actually did, factual or reasoning gaps, and the next repair. Record time only if measured; an unfinished answer does not by itself establish time pressure or lack of knowledge.

For current affairs, start with one relevant item connected to the topic being studied: locate it, identify the process or social issue, check the primary evidence and add a short dated example. Relevant items include heritage conservation, migration, gender/work, urban change, population, resource location and physical events. Increase this routine only when it is sustainable; do not turn GS I into an unrestricted daily-news archive. Revisit historical themes when an event illuminates them, without rewriting established history around headlines.

Writing progresses from an open-book explanation to an outline, then a complete answer from memory, mixed-topic practice and finally a complete paper. Learn the question's actual demand before imposing a timer. Use exam word limits for exam-format answers, not as quotas for every learning note. A labelled map, comparison or diagram is useful only when accurate and relevant. Historical answers need chronology and causal evidence; culture needs features with examples; society needs mechanisms and variation; geography needs process and location. Remedies should answer the question and follow from the diagnosis.

Select some older PYQs for learning and preserve unseen complete papers where possible. Mark reused questions as familiar. Self-review for relevance, accuracy, reasoning, evidence and coverage of sub-parts; an external suggested answer is not the only acceptable response. Formal test generation and evaluation remain on request under the [Mains test method](../MAINS-TEST-GENERATION.md) and [evaluation method](../MAINS-ANSWER-EVALUATION.md). These placeholders cannot support chapter tests; a requested full-syllabus test follows its own readiness rules and can be labelled diagnostic before full coverage.

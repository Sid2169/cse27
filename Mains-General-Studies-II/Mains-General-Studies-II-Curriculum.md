## 1. Understand the paper and start small

GS II studies constitutional government, public institutions, development and welfare, and India's international relations. Learn to explain how a system should work, assess how it works in practice and justify a response to the specific problem in a question.

The official baseline is the [UPSC CSE 2026 notification](https://www.upsc.gov.in/sites/default/files/Notif-CSP-2026-Engl-060226Rev.pdf), Appendix I, Sections II(B) and III(B), checked on 19–20 November 2026:

| Feature | Preparation baseline |
|---|---|
| Identity and role | GS II is **Mains Paper III**, carrying **250 marks** and counting towards merit. Prelims Paper II (CSAT) is a different paper. |
| Format and duration | Descriptive answers; **three hours**. |
| Scoring | Marks come from evaluated written answers; there is no objective-paper wrong-option penalty. UPSC may set qualifying marks in merit papers. |
| Medium | Questions are set in Hindi and English; answers must follow the authorised medium. This plan uses English. Hindi as qualifying Paper A does not itself determine the GS answer medium. |
| Standard | Understanding, analysis and relevant concise expression; specialised optional-level study is not the GS standard. |

For practice, the repository's [Mains test method](../MAINS-TEST-GENERATION.md) records **20 compulsory questions: ten of 10 marks/150 words and ten of 15 marks/250 words**, totalling 250. The detailed split is a previously sourced repository baseline: the official 2026 booklet could not be retrieved in this preparation. Do not infer marks or word limits from the local transcription alone. Recheck the 2027 notification, corrigenda and actual booklet before using their rules. The accessible [UPSC examination FAQ](https://cms.upsc.gov.in/about-us/divisions/examination/) also confirms descriptive format and duration.

**First study session:** read the [short syllabus](Mains-General-Studies-II-Syllabus.md), glance through a past paper to recognise its demands, and set up a vocabulary page and a source/example notebook. Start with 001–003, then 005–006. Once one topic is understood, use 004 to write a short explanation and an answer outline. There is no need to attempt a complete paper first, finish several books before writing, or follow a fixed daily chapter quota.

## 2. PYQ evidence, priorities and limitations

The working corpus is the [2013–2026 GS II collection](Mains-General-Studies-II-PYQS/). Every year is populated with a secondary Anantam IAS transcription, accessed **11 November 2026**, and an exact-wording verification caveat. The files omit booklet instructions and per-question marks/word limits. Earlier papers can have different formats. Use original UPSC papers for exact quotation, disputed wording and timed complete-paper work. This curriculum does not re-extract or alter the corpus.

The following are qualitative curriculum judgements from the corpus, not official weightage or predictions:

- **Constitutional principles are tested through institutional problems.** Amendment limits, privacy, judicial appointments and federal relations require reasoning beyond article lists: [2013 Q4](Mains-General-Studies-II-PYQS/2013-Mains-General-Studies-II.md), [2017 Q2, Q12](Mains-General-Studies-II-PYQS/2017-Mains-General-Studies-II.md), [2024 Q13](Mains-General-Studies-II-PYQS/2024-Mains-General-Studies-II.md), [2025 Q12–14](Mains-General-Studies-II-PYQS/2025-Mains-General-Studies-II.md).
- **Institutions need distinct treatment.** Legislative scrutiny, election law, local governance, audit and judicial redress ask different kinds of questions: [2017 Q5](Mains-General-Studies-II-PYQS/2017-Mains-General-Studies-II.md), [2022 Q11, Q15](Mains-General-Studies-II-PYQS/2022-Mains-General-Studies-II.md), [2024 Q2, Q4–5](Mains-General-Studies-II-PYQS/2024-Mains-General-Studies-II.md).
- **Governance and welfare ask about mechanisms and outcomes.** Explain why a scheme, organisation or service succeeds or fails and which reform addresses that cause: [2013 Q11–13, Q16](Mains-General-Studies-II-PYQS/2013-Mains-General-Studies-II.md), [2017 Q6–8, Q15–18](Mains-General-Studies-II-PYQS/2017-Mains-General-Studies-II.md), [2024 Q7, Q16–18](Mains-General-Studies-II-PYQS/2024-Mains-General-Studies-II.md).
- **IR connects interests, domestic politics and institutional constraints.** Country knowledge must support an assessment of choices: [2013 Q23, Q25](Mains-General-Studies-II-PYQS/2013-Mains-General-Studies-II.md), [2022 Q9–10, Q19–20](Mains-General-Studies-II-PYQS/2022-Mains-General-Studies-II.md), [2024 Q9–10, Q19–20](Mains-General-Studies-II-PYQS/2024-Mains-General-Studies-II.md), [2025 Q19–20](Mains-General-Studies-II-PYQS/2025-Mains-General-Studies-II.md).

All syllabus areas remain covered even where direct anchors are sparse. A linked PYQ can span several chapters; it does not expand each chapter to cover the entire question. Learn the relevant component first, then integrate it later.

**Encountered wording and date cautions:** [2020 Q16](Mains-General-Studies-II-PYQS/2020-Mains-General-Studies-II.md) has garbled multidimensional-poverty wording and is not used as an exact index anchor. [2015 Q8 and Q15](Mains-General-Studies-II-PYQS/2015-Mains-General-Studies-II.md) refer to FCRA 1976 and whistleblower legislation labelled 2011. The official [FCRA 2010 text](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/2098/1/a2010-42.pdf) and [2015 amending Act, Second Schedule](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/12142/1/a2015___17.pdf), which substitutes 2014 in the whistleblower law's short title, explain why those labels should not be copied into current-law notes. This does not establish present commencement or implementation status; verify those separately.

The Quad characterisation in [2020 Q19](Mains-General-Studies-II-PYQS/2020-Mains-General-Studies-II.md) is a proposition to evaluate, not a classification to repeat. The amendment asserted by [2026 Q1](Mains-General-Studies-II-PYQS/2026-Mains-General-Studies-II.md) has not been independently verified here; the index uses only its broad rights theme. Older references to Article 370, Delhi governance, Finance Commissions, schemes, MDGs and diplomatic events must retain their historical context. Check originals and current primary sources before teaching disputed details; preserve the existing corpus until an explicit correction is documented.

## 3. Learning route and balanced chapter breadth

The chapter count follows the learning boundaries; it is not a target. Subject chapters aim for a comparable unit of understanding: a defined institutional function, a policy problem, a protection framework or a bounded relationship/region. Equal breadth does not mean identical reading time. Foundation and revision modules are deliberately shorter and reusable.

| Phase | Chapters | Progression |
|---|---|---|
| A — Foundations and communication | 001–004 | Learn vocabulary and source reading, then build a supported answer. |
| B — Constitutional foundations | 005–014 | Move from founding choices to rights, remedies, constitutional change and comparison. |
| C — Executive, legislature, judiciary and elections | 015–029 | Learn institutional structure before contested functioning and reform. |
| D — Federalism, local government and public institutions | 030–038 | Separate powers, finances, special arrangements, devolution and oversight. |
| E — Governance and development actors | 039–049 | Follow policy from design to delivery and accountability, then assess non-state participation. |
| F — Social justice and public services | 050–064 | Learn welfare architecture before distinct groups, deprivation and sector systems. |
| G — International relations | 065–087 | Build a map and vocabulary; study relationships before comparing forums, institutions and agreements. |
| H — Application and revision | 088–090 | Integrate learned material, practise complete papers and revise from observed errors. |

This is a recommended route, not a chain requiring every preceding chapter. **IR can start at 065 after 002–003**, alongside domestic study. Governance can begin after the foundation and executive bridges named in 039. Social justice needs the common rights and policy foundations, not every constitutional office. Follow the stated prerequisites; boundary references identify where adjoining content belongs and are not extra mandatory prerequisites. Comparative chapter 014 starts with basic models and is revisited after the offices have been learned.

To preserve manageable scope during later authoring:

- Keep an institution's legal structure and its distinct functioning problems in their assigned chapters. Federal finances, local government, election law, judicial appointments and access to justice each have separate homes.
- Teach common welfare/evaluation mechanisms once, then apply them to each group or sector. Do not repeat every programme in every social-justice chapter.
- Use a consistent IR approach: essential map/history → interests and cooperation → sources of tension → effects of external policies → defensible options. Paired neighbours receive separate contexts and a focused comparison; regions use selected cases rather than inventories of all countries.
- Select examples to demonstrate mechanisms and variation. A chapter on regulators or agencies is a bounded comparison of institutional design, not an encyclopaedia of every body.
- Honour the **boundary and hand-off** in each entry. If authoring reveals an overloaded unit, revise the affected scopes together or use linked parts under the [chapter method](../CHAPTER-GENERATION.md); do not silently omit material or duplicate it.

Use the learning cycle from the beginning: understand → recall without notes → explain through an example → outline a relevant PYQ → review missing reasoning/evidence → revisit after a gap. Use 004 throughout. The support/application modules are not automatically suitable for standalone chapter tests; substantive readiness follows the [Mains test method](../MAINS-TEST-GENERATION.md).

<a id="chapter-001"></a>

#### [001 — GS II orientation and evidence literacy](001-gs-ii-orientation-and-evidence-literacy.md)

- **Teach:** Paper identity, syllabus mapping, sources, retrieval and revision; elementary percentages, denominators, observation periods and causal claims.
- **Outcome:** Set up a workable study record and distinguish evidence from assertion.
- **Boundary and hand-off:** Keep this a short support module; constitutional vocabulary is in 002 and legal reading in 003.
- **Prerequisites:** None.
- **Exam use / PYQ anchors:** Orientation and evidence use across the paper; no standalone subject PYQ required.

<a id="chapter-002"></a>

#### [002 — State, democracy and constitutional vocabulary](002-state-democracy-and-constitutional-vocabulary.md)

- **Teach:** State versus government, nation, sovereignty, citizenship, democracy, republic, constitution, institutions, levels of government and public accountability.
- **Outcome:** Explain who governs, under what authority and with what limits.
- **Boundary and hand-off:** Introduce a simple institutional map; detailed powers belong to 015–038, foreign-policy vocabulary to 065.
- **Prerequisites:** None.
- **Exam use / PYQ anchors:** Foundation for every polity question; syllabus-led.

<a id="chapter-003"></a>

#### [003 — Reading constitutional, legal and policy sources](003-reading-constitutional-legal-and-policy-sources.md)

- **Teach:** Articles, schedules, Acts, rules, notifications, bills and judgments; enactment versus commencement, court holding versus commentary, policy promise versus measured result.
- **Outcome:** Trace a claim to its source and date without treating a proposal as operative law.
- **Boundary and hand-off:** Teach reading skills with small examples; substantive rights begin at 008, policy evaluation at 040.
- **Prerequisites:** 001–002.
- **Exam use / PYQ anchors:** [2024 Q11](Mains-General-Studies-II-PYQS/2024-Mains-General-Studies-II.md) illustrates why legal scope matters; this is a reusable support module.

<a id="chapter-004"></a>

#### [004 — Question demand and analytical answers](004-question-demand-and-analytical-answers.md)

- **Teach:** Directives and qualifiers, sub-parts, claims with reasons and examples, comparison, balanced judgement, concise prose and useful institutional diagrams.
- **Outcome:** Build and revise an answer to a learned topic.
- **Boundary and hand-off:** Practise throughout subject study; mixed-theme synthesis and complete-paper execution belong to 088–090.
- **Prerequisites:** 001; one learned subject topic for practice.
- **Exam use / PYQ anchors:** Apply to the relevant topic's PYQ; no universal template or mandatory quotation.

### Phase B — Constitutional foundations

<a id="chapter-005"></a>

#### [005 — Constitutional history and the founding choices](005-constitutional-history-and-the-founding-choices.md)

- **Exam use / PYQ anchors:** Historical underpinnings; [2016 Q7](Mains-General-Studies-II-PYQS/2016-Mains-General-Studies-II.md).

#### [006 — Preamble, constitutional values and salient features](006-preamble-constitutional-values-and-salient-features.md)

- **Outcome:** Relate constitutional values to institutional choices and tensions.
- **Boundary and hand-off:** Introduce features without reteaching offices; rights in 008–012, amendment limits in 013, comparison in 014.
- **Prerequisites:** 005.
- **Exam use / PYQ anchors:** Interpretation of values; [2016 Q5](Mains-General-Studies-II-PYQS/2016-Mains-General-Studies-II.md); [2021 Q1](Mains-General-Studies-II-PYQS/2021-Mains-General-Studies-II.md).

<a id="chapter-007"></a>

#### [007 — Citizenship, territory and political membership](007-citizenship-territory-and-political-membership.md)

- **Teach:** Constitutional and statutory citizenship framework, acquisition/loss, citizenship versus residence, Union territory and state formation, membership and equal civic standing.
- **Outcome:** Separate territorial organisation, legal citizenship and political rights.
- **Boundary and hand-off:** Voting administration is in 027; special territorial governance in 032; diaspora policy in 087. Verify changing law when authoring.
- **Prerequisites:** 002–003, 006.
- **Exam use / PYQ anchors:** Significant provisions; [2026 Q4](Mains-General-Studies-II-PYQS/2026-Mains-General-Studies-II.md) is a joint application with 027, not authority for current law.

<a id="chapter-008"></a>

#### [008 — Equality and affirmative action](008-equality-and-affirmative-action.md)

- **Teach:** Formal and substantive equality, non-discrimination, equal public opportunity, reservations and reasonable classification; tensions between group disadvantage and individual claims.
- **Outcome:** Evaluate an equality argument using its constitutional basis and relevant distinctions.

The constitutional-body requirement is distributed deliberately: election bodies with elections/local government, Finance Commissions with federal finances, audit with accountability, service commissions/law officers in 037, and protective commissions with the relevant rights. Every such chapter must teach the body's **basis, appointments/removal, powers, responsibilities, safeguards and limits**, rather than just name it. Statutory versus constitutional status must be verified, not inferred from a body's importance.

GS II owns institutional authority, rights, delivery design, accountability and diplomatic implications. GS I supplies social/historical context and geography; GS III owns detailed economic mechanisms, technical security, environment/science and infrastructure; GS IV owns extended ethical theory and ethical case studies. Brief bridges belong here when needed to understand a GS II question. These boundaries organise teaching; they are not claims that UPSC never combines subjects. Prelims polity can be a refresher, but Prelims completion is not a prerequisite.

## 6. Essential resources and practice habits

Begin with a small kit: the syllabus, local PYQs with caveats, original papers, a current official Constitution, one accessible constitutional explanation and one IR foundation text. The choices below are educational recommendations, not an official book list. Read by topic rather than completing all books before beginning the curriculum.

| Resource and priority | What to use it for |
|---|---|
| **Essential:** official syllabus, [UPSC notification](https://www.upsc.gov.in/sites/default/files/Notif-CSP-2026-Engl-060226Rev.pdf), [question-paper archive](https://www.upsc.gov.in/examinations/previous-question-papers) and local corpus | Scope and exam rules; original booklet wording/instructions; local questions for topic calibration. Archive retrieval can require the migrated UPSC site. |
| **Essential foundation:** NCERT [Indian Constitution at Work](https://ncert.nic.in/textbook.php?keps2=26-100) | Accessible explanations of constitutional choices, rights and institutions for B–D. Supplement legal changes from primary texts; an older textbook's account is not current law. |
| **Essential legal reference:** [Legislative Department's Constitution collection](https://www.legislative.gov.in/documents) and [India Code](https://indiacode.gov.in/) | Look up the article/Act being studied; check amendments, commencement, rules and judicial interpretation. Read selected provisions with an explanation, not the entire statute library in sequence. |
| **Essential IR foundation:** NCERT [Contemporary World Politics](https://ncert.nic.in/textbook.php?leps1=0-9), plus a usable political atlas | Learn world-order and institutional vocabulary for 065, then locate each partner, border and maritime route. Match the actual edition to topics; historical context needs current diplomatic supplementation. |
| **Essential targeted governance supplement:** selected [Second Administrative Reforms Commission reports](https://www.darpg.gov.in/arc-reports) | Read relevant diagnosis/recommendations, especially RTI, local governance, social capital, personnel, e-governance and citizen services. These are historical reform arguments, not proof that recommendations were implemented. No cover-to-cover report quota. |
| **Essential targeted social-sector evidence:** the responsible ministry/state department's current guidelines, annual report and underlying survey/evaluation | For each studied problem, learn the main instrument, eligibility, funding/delivery chain, evidence of outcomes and a documented limitation. Use actual health, education, labour and nutrition tables with observation periods; a press release alone does not establish impact. |
| **Essential targeted IR evidence:** MEA [bilateral briefs](https://www.mea.gov.in/foreign-relations.htm), [organisation briefs](https://www.mea.gov.in/regional-organisations.htm), dated joint statements and agreement texts | Establish India's stated position and relationship background; distinguish commitments from implementation. Briefs vary in age. Compare partner or institution sources for contested facts instead of treating a diplomatic claim as neutral proof. |
| **Targeted institutional reference:** [UN main bodies](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/main-bodies) and each relevant body's charter/official mandate page | Distinguish structure, authority, voting, resources and outcomes; supplement with current reports only when needed for a question. Do not memorise an indiscriminate organisation catalogue. |

For a concrete entry into sector evidence, use the **National Family Health Survey (NFHS)** for health/nutrition and household indicators, **Unified District Information System for Education Plus (UDISE+)** for schools, teachers and participation, and **Periodic Labour Force Survey (PLFS)** for employment-related indicators. Start with a definition and one relevant table, explaining its population and period. The [NFHS-6 factsheets for 2023–24](https://nhm.gov.in/New-Update-2025-26/NFHS-6-factsheet.pdf), [Education Ministry report identifying UDISE+ publications](https://www.education.gov.in/sites/upload_files/mhrd/files/document-reports/MoE_AR_En.pdf), and [MoSPI dataset compendium, PLFS entry](https://mospi.gov.in/sites/default/files/publication_reports/Compendium_of_Datasets_and_Registries_in_India_2024_0.pdf) were confirmed in official search on **20 November 2026**. These are starting references, not a claim that all rounds or figures have been audited here. School administrative data alone do not measure learning quality; labour-survey definitions and reference periods matter when comparing results.

**Optional supplements:** one explanatory polity manual for a gap the NCERT/constitutional text does not resolve; a selected lecture for difficult legal reasoning; parliamentary committee reports, CAG reports, judgments or specialist IR analyses for a specific question. Add a source because a gap exists. A commercial manual, coaching model answer or institutional press release is not the final authority on law, facts or the only acceptable argument.

Keep three small working records:

- **Concepts and institutions:** explain the purpose, legal basis, decision process, accountability and a concrete example. Compare bodies only on relevant common criteria. Article/case names support reasoning; they do not replace it.
- **Policies and evidence:** problem, intended beneficiaries, instrument, responsible level/agency, implementation evidence, limits and a justified improvement. Separate a budget allocation from expenditure, an output from an outcome, a national average from unequal access, and association from causation. Include the exact source, observation period, publication/access date and uncertainty.
- **Writing and repair:** question/year, its actual demand, what the answer demonstrated, missing reasoning/evidence, and the next correction. Record timing or uncertainty only when observed; do not infer guessing or time pressure from an omission.

For current affairs, begin with one relevant item connected to the chapter being studied. Ask which syllabus concept it illustrates, verify its legal or institutional status, and add a dated example. A bill, an enacted Act, a commenced provision, a rule, a court ruling and an implemented programme are different stages. For IR, note the event date, actors, agreement/position, effect on India and unresolved uncertainty. Increase the routine only if sustainable; retain explanations and useful evidence rather than an unlimited news archive.

Writing starts with an open-book explanation, then an outline and a complete answer from memory. Progress to mixed questions and a full paper after adequate breadth. Use the prescribed exam word limit for an exam-format answer, not as a quota for learning notes. A good reform follows from a demonstrated cause, identifies who could act and recognises constraints. A judgement can be qualified; mechanically giving equal space to “pros” and “cons” is not a requirement.

Reserve some complete official papers as unfamiliar practice where possible and label reused questions as familiar. Review relevance, accuracy, reasoning, evidence and all requested sub-parts. Tests and formal evaluation remain on request under the [Mains test](../MAINS-TEST-GENERATION.md) and [answer-evaluation](../MAINS-ANSWER-EVALUATION.md) methods. Placeholders do not make a chapter assessment-ready. A requested full-syllabus diagnostic follows its own mode rules.

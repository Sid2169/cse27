# Mains General Studies IV — Curriculum

Prepared: **23 November 2026**, for CSE 2027 preparation. This is the study plan and authoritative chapter-file index for **Ethics, Integrity and Aptitude**. All **47 indexed chapters are planning placeholders**; their teaching content has not yet been authored. The plan assumes no prior UPSC preparation, philosophy, psychology or administrative experience.

Begin with the syllabus and chapters 001–004, then build concepts and practical judgement together. The verified examination baseline is a **250-mark, three-hour descriptive merit paper**, with both short answers and case studies; sources and the distinction between the official scheme and booklet layout are explained below. Chapter numbers give a recommended sequence, while each entry identifies its actual prerequisites.

## 1. Understand the paper and start small

GS IV asks you to explain ethical ideas and use them to judge conduct, public decisions and conflicts. A **stakeholder** is a person or group affected by a decision; a **dilemma** involves competing obligations or values. These and other necessary terms will be introduced in the foundation chapters before advanced theories or administrative mechanisms.

The [official 2026 CSE notification, Appendix I, Sections I–II](https://upsc.gov.in/uploads/2026/05/Civil-Services-Preliminary-Examination-2026-Notice-E.pdf), accessed **23 November 2026**, identifies GS IV as **Mains Paper V**, carrying **250 marks counted for merit**, with conventional written answers and **three hours**. The 2026 scheme allows UPSC to fix qualifying marks in Mains papers at its discretion. The objective-paper negative-marking rule does not apply to this descriptive paper. This plan uses the verified 2026 scheme as its baseline; recheck the **2027 notification, corrigenda and actual booklet** when available. Choosing Hindi for qualifying Paper A does not by itself determine the authorised GS answer medium.

The [2026 scanned booklet, hosted by GSMitra](https://gsmitra.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/upsc-mains-2026-gs-4.pdf) was visually inspected on **23 November 2026** for instructions, question structure and selected references. The official [UPSC paper listing](https://www.upsc.gov.in/examinations/previous-question-papers) also indexed a 2026 GS IV paper, but direct retrieval of that official listing failed during this task. The scan is therefore identified as a **third-party-hosted copy**, rather than claimed to have been downloaded from UPSC.

| 2026 booklet component | Numbering and demand | Marks and indicated length |
|---|---|---|
| Section A | Q1–Q6, containing **13 short-answer sub-parts**, including applied concepts and interpretation | 13 × 10 = **130 marks**; 150 words per sub-part |
| Section B | Q7–Q12, containing **six case studies**, each with its own sub-demands | 6 × 20 = **120 marks**; 250 words per case as a whole |
| Complete paper | **12 numbered questions**, all compulsory; questions printed in Hindi and English | **250 marks**, three hours; use the authorised answer medium |

The split also agrees with the [repository Mains test method](../MAINS-TEST-GENERATION.md). It is an observed paper format and a practice baseline, not a promised 2027 blueprint. Older papers can differ. Case sub-demands share the case’s word limit unless the booklet says otherwise; “12 questions” does not mean only 12 short responses. Marks require relevant, reasoned answers; a stock quotation or named philosopher does not substitute for addressing the demand. Any later practice rubric remains a practice tool rather than an official UPSC marking scheme.

**First study session:** read the [official syllabus](Mains-General-Studies-IV-Syllabus.md), inspect [2013 Q1](Mains-General-Studies-IV-PYQS/2013-Mains-General-Studies-IV.md) and [2013 Q12](Mains-General-Studies-IV-PYQS/2013-Mains-General-Studies-IV.md) to see a concept question and a decision case, and create a vocabulary page. Begin 001–004 when authored. Start with a plain-language explanation and a short outline using one familiar situation. A full timed paper or a stack of completed books is unnecessary at entry.

This curriculum and its later teaching material default to English. Read the linked chapters as they become substantive; an empty or marked placeholder is a plan, not completed learning or readiness for a chapter test.

These files guide topic selection and question demand; the booklet governs precise wording, marks, word limits and instructions.

**Reference convention:** 2013–2025 references below use the question numbers and sub-parts printed in the local files. The 2026 file lists **19 independently numbered entries**. It also rearranges the booklet’s order: for example, **local entry 3 is booklet Q1(a)** and **local entry 15 is booklet Q7** in the inspected scan. Consequently every 2026 reference here is labelled **“entry”**, never treated as a booklet question number. The corpus files have been preserved.

The local files contain 14 top-level numbered entries per year for 2013–2017, 12 for 2018–2025, and 19 for 2026. These counts describe the files’ organisation, not comparable numbers of answer tasks. Per-item marks and lengths are inconsistently retained. No numerical topic weightage or prediction has been derived from these counts.

Three encountered cautions affect use of these references:

- **Incomplete transcription:** [2025 Q11](Mains-General-Studies-IV-PYQS/2025-Mains-General-Studies-IV.md) has a broken wage-allowance clause in its scheme description. It is used here only to locate the case about records, payments and diversion of public money. Check the official wording and operative scheme provisions before teaching an entitlement; the incomplete clause supplies no reliable numerical rule.
- **A question’s framing is not a legal authority:** [2017 Q14](Mains-General-Studies-IV-PYQS/2017-Mains-General-Studies-IV.md) discusses separating information requests by applicants’ apparent motives. [RTI Act, section 6(2)](https://upload.indiacode.nic.in/showfile?actid=AC_CEN_26_36_00004_200522_1517807322955&filename=a2005-22.pdf&type=actfile), checked on **23 November 2026**, does not require an applicant to state reasons for seeking information. Chapter 035 must address the ethical concern within the applicable law rather than invent a motive-based rejection power.
- **Historical or hypothetical premises stay in their setting:** scheme conditions, financial limits, medical assertions, international events and allegations within a case are not automatically verified statements of present law or fact. For example, use [2025 Q10](Mains-General-Studies-IV-PYQS/2025-Mains-General-Studies-IV.md)'s delegated financial powers as facts of that scenario, not universal procurement thresholds. Verify consequential real-world claims separately when chapters are authored.

The scan check was limited to format and references needed for this curriculum; it was not a complete transcription audit or a certification of all corpus wording. Additional discrepancies should be recorded and investigated when they affect the learning task.

## 3. Learning progression and recurring overlaps

The phases organise a recommended progression, not a calendar or an instruction to finish every earlier chapter before beginning a new branch.

Return to 045 when enough of a case’s contributing topics have been learned; use 046 for broad synthesis. Chapter 047 can guide revision throughout. ## 5. Detailed chapter plan and authoritative file index

All linked chapter files below are **planning placeholders**. “Teaching scope” describes what later authoring should explain; it is not teaching content already present. Each prerequisite names necessary concepts, not merely every earlier chapter. When an application needs extra subject knowledge, the entry states the dependency or requires a short contextual bridge.

The sequence was reviewed as a whole for granularity: attitude foundations, persuasion, EI concepts and EI applications have separate outcomes; individual values are distinct from institutional mechanisms; international funding, public-fund use and procurement each have a clear home. Thinker chapters select and compare arguments rather than requiring complete biographies. Orientation and revision are intentionally shorter support modules. There is no prescribed chapter-length quota or target chapter count.

### Phase A — Orientation, vocabulary and first reasoning

<a id="chapter-001"></a>

#### 001. [GS IV orientation and a manageable study routine](001-gs-iv-orientation-and-a-manageable-study-routine.md)

- **Teaching scope:** Introduce ethics, integrity and aptitude as the purpose of this paper; distinguish short conceptual answers, quotation interpretation and case studies. Explain the syllabus, the merit role, booklet instructions, source caveats and a small vocabulary-and-example notebook. Show how to use this index and return to earlier concepts during applications.
- **Learning outcome:** Recognise the main question demands and choose an achievable first study task.
- **Prerequisites:** None; begin from ordinary language and experience.
- **Boundary and hand-off:** A deliberately short orientation. Ethical vocabulary belongs to 002, answer reasoning to 004 and full-paper consolidation to 046. No prior GS, philosophy or psychology course is assumed.
- **Exam use and evidence:** Use the examination sources in section 1 and compare a conceptual question with a case: [2013 Q1](Mains-General-Studies-IV-PYQS/2013-Mains-General-Studies-IV.md); [2013 Q12](Mains-General-Studies-IV-PYQS/2013-Mains-General-Studies-IV.md); [2024 Q3](Mains-General-Studies-IV-PYQS/2024-Mains-General-Studies-IV.md).

<a id="chapter-002"></a>

#### 002. [Ethical vocabulary and responsibility for human actions](002-ethical-vocabulary-and-responsibility-for-human-actions.md)

- **Teaching scope:** Introduce ethics, morality, values, principles, virtues, norms and ethical dilemmas through ordinary situations. Plan the essence and dimensions of ethics; intention, motive, means, action, omission and consequences; freedom, knowledge, coercion and responsibility. Explain descriptive statements versus judgements about what ought to be done, and distinguish ethics from law, custom and personal preference.
- **Learning outcome:** Identify the ethical issue in a concrete action and explain why an intention or a beneficial result alone may not settle responsibility.
- **Prerequisites:** None; begin from ordinary language and experience.
- **Boundary and hand-off:** Owns the shared vocabulary and basic determinants of action. Ethical reasoning approaches are developed in 005, context and universality in 006, institutional rules and conscience in 025. Recalling these terms later should not restart the foundations.

#### 003. [Public service, constitutional values and the citizen](003-public-service-constitutional-values-and-the-citizen.md)

- **Teaching scope:** Build the necessary bridge: citizen, state, government, administration, public office, public interest, authority and discretion. Introduce elected representatives and permanent officials, public power held in trust, and constitutional values of dignity, liberty, equality, fraternity and justice. Explain constitutional morality through treatment of citizens and limits on power.
- **Learning outcome:** Explain why public office carries special obligations and connect an administrative choice to constitutional values.
- **Prerequisites:** [002](#chapter-002).
- **Boundary and hand-off:** Owns the basic public-service setting and the introductory philosophical basis of governance. GS II owns detailed constitutional institutions and litigation; no completed GS II chapter is required. Justice is deepened in 006, neutrality in 016 and probity in 034.

<a id="chapter-004"></a>

#### 004. [Reading ethical questions and reasoning through a first dilemma](004-reading-ethical-questions-and-reasoning-through-a-first-dilemma.md)

- **Teaching scope:** Plan how to identify a command word and all sub-demands; explain a concept with an appropriate example; interpret a quotation through meaning, reasoning and present relevance. Introduce a simple dilemma using facts, affected people, competing obligations, possible actions and reasons. Distinguish a factual claim from an assumption, and a genuine ethical conflict from a temptation to gain personally.
- **Learning outcome:** Write a focused explanation or a justified first decision using ordinary language and the facts supplied.
- **Prerequisites:** [002](#chapter-002), [003](#chapter-003).
- **Boundary and hand-off:** Owns the early reasoning and communication method. Advanced comparison of options, implementation and uncertainty belongs to 026 and 045. ### Phase B — Human values and ethical reasoning

<a id="chapter-005"></a>

#### 005. [Consequences, duties, virtues and care in ethical reasoning](005-consequences-duties-virtues-and-care-in-ethical-reasoning.md)

- **Teaching scope:** Introduce consequentialist reasoning through benefits and harms; duty-based reasoning through obligations and respect for persons; virtue through character and practical judgement; and care through relationships and dependency. Discuss happiness, well-being and a good life as ethical concerns. Define each unfamiliar term before comparing everyday choices. Examine means and ends, foreseeable effects, conflicting duties and the limits of any single approach.
- **Learning outcome:** Compare reasons for and against an action from more than one ethical approach without reducing the decision to a slogan.
- **Prerequisites:** [002](#chapter-002), [004](#chapter-004).
- **Boundary and hand-off:** Owns the usable introductory reasoning approaches. Chapters 022–024 deepen selected thinkers and disagreements instead of repeating these definitions. Justice and rights have their main home in 006; administrative application belongs to 026.
- **Exam use and evidence:** Reasoned comparison and means–ends questions: [2014 Q1(a)](Mains-General-Studies-IV-PYQS/2014-Mains-General-Studies-IV.md); [2014 Q3(b)](Mains-General-Studies-IV-PYQS/2014-Mains-General-Studies-IV.md); [2018 Q4(b)](Mains-General-Studies-IV-PYQS/2018-Mains-General-Studies-IV.md); [2018 Q6(a)](Mains-General-Studies-IV-PYQS/2018-Mains-General-Studies-IV.md); [2026 entry 4](Mains-General-Studies-IV-PYQS/2026-Mains-General-Studies-IV.md).

<a id="chapter-006"></a>

#### 006. [Justice, rights, the common good and changing moral contexts](006-justice-rights-the-common-good-and-changing-moral-contexts.md)

- **Teaching scope:** Introduce fairness, equal treatment, equity, rights, duties, dignity, autonomy and the common good. Compare fair distribution, fair procedures and responses to wrongdoing with simple examples. Examine universal human values, cultural variation, changing circumstances and reasoned disagreement; distinguish tolerance of difference from accepting avoidable harm or discrimination.
- **Learning outcome:** Explain a justice claim, identify whose interests or rights are affected, and justify a context-sensitive judgement with consistent principles.
- **Prerequisites:** [002](#chapter-002), [003](#chapter-003), [005](#chapter-005).
- **Boundary and hand-off:** Owns basic justice and universality questions; 024 develops Rawls and other selected arguments. Chapter 018 applies these ideas to vulnerability, 032 to international relations and 043 to future generations and the environment. Detailed constitutional doctrine remains in GS II.
- **Exam use and evidence:** Universality, dignity, inequality and contextual justice: [2013 Q3](Mains-General-Studies-IV-PYQS/2013-Mains-General-Studies-IV.md); [2014 Q3(b)](Mains-General-Studies-IV-PYQS/2014-Mains-General-Studies-IV.md); [2017 Q7(b)](Mains-General-Studies-IV-PYQS/2017-Mains-General-Studies-IV.md); [2024 Q4(a)](Mains-General-Studies-IV-PYQS/2024-Mains-General-Studies-IV.md).

<a id="chapter-007"></a>

#### 007. [Ethics in private relationships and public roles](007-ethics-in-private-relationships-and-public-roles.md)

- **Teaching scope:** Examine family, friendship, community, professional and public relationships: loyalty, trust, confidentiality, honesty and obligations to people outside one’s close circle. Consider role conflicts, private conduct with public consequences, professional boundaries and the difference between legitimate personal commitments and favouritism. Use examples of family care, workplace loyalty and abuse of power.
- **Learning outcome:** Explain how a change of role changes responsibilities while maintaining respect for dignity and basic ethical commitments.
- **Prerequisites:** [002](#chapter-002), [003](#chapter-003), [006](#chapter-006).
- **Boundary and hand-off:** Owns relational ethics and the private–public distinction. Chapter 027 teaches formal conflict-of-interest safeguards; 031 owns workplace complaints and dignity; 025 handles conflicts involving conscience and rules. <a id="chapter-008"></a>

#### 008. [How family, society and education develop human values](008-how-family-society-and-education-develop-human-values.md)

- **Teaching scope:** Plan the development and internalisation of values through family example, school and college practices, peers, social norms, community institutions and media. Explain moral learning, reflection, reinforcement and the influence of role models, alongside the transmission of prejudice. Connect values to trust and social capital, meaning the relationships and shared expectations that enable cooperation.

### Phase C — Attitude and emotional intelligence

<a id="chapter-009"></a>

#### 009. [Attitude: content, structure, functions and behaviour](009-attitude-content-structure-functions-and-behaviour.md)

- **Teaching scope:** Define attitude and distinguish it from belief, value, aptitude and observable behaviour. Teach cognitive, emotional and behavioural components; strength and consistency of attitudes; and functions such as organising knowledge, expressing values, protecting self-image and seeking acceptance or rewards. Explain why stated attitudes and actual behaviour may diverge under situational pressures.
- **Learning outcome:** Analyse an attitude using its components and functions, and explain its relationship with thought and behaviour.
- **Prerequisites:** [002](#chapter-002).
- **Boundary and hand-off:** Owns the basic attitude model. Formation, prejudice and moral/political attitudes belong to 010; social influence and persuasion to 011. Emotional intelligence in 012–013 uses, but does not redefine, emotions and behaviour.

#### 010. [Attitude formation, prejudice and moral and political attitudes](010-attitude-formation-prejudice-and-moral-and-political-attitudes.md)

- **Teaching scope:** Explain learning, experience, social identity and reference groups in attitude formation. Introduce stereotypes, prejudice, discrimination, selective attention and rationalisation, using caste, gender and other social contexts without stereotyping communities. #### 011. [Social influence, persuasion and ethical behaviour change](011-social-influence-persuasion-and-ethical-behaviour-change.md)

<a id="chapter-012"></a>

#### 012. [Emotional intelligence and personal self-management](012-emotional-intelligence-and-personal-self-management.md)

- **Teaching scope:** Introduce emotions, emotional awareness, self-regulation, motivation and recognition of others’ emotions. Distinguish emotional intelligence from cognitive ability, personality, suppressed feelings and moral goodness. Introduce commonly used ability and competency perspectives without treating them as identical or as settled measures of character. #### 013. [Emotional intelligence in administration and conflict resolution](013-emotional-intelligence-in-administration-and-conflict-resolution.md)

- **Teaching scope:** Apply emotional awareness, listening, perspective-taking, de-escalation and constructive feedback to aggrieved citizens, strained teams and crisis settings. ### Phase E — Moral thinkers and lessons from public lives

<a id="chapter-019"></a>

#### 019. [Duty, non-violence and service in Indian ethical traditions](019-duty-non-violence-and-service-in-indian-ethical-traditions.md)

- **Teaching scope:** Use selected teachings to compare ways of understanding duty, self-restraint, compassion, non-violence and service. Representative material includes the Bhagavad Gita on action and duty, Buddha on suffering and compassion, Mahavira on non-violence and restraint, and Thiruvalluvar and Guru Nanak on ethical living and social responsibility. Introduce each text or teacher’s context and explain the chosen idea before applying it.

- **Prerequisites:** [005](#chapter-005), [006](#chapter-006), [008](#chapter-008).
- **Boundary and hand-off:** Owns a thematic introduction to these traditions, not their complete religious systems or biographies. Gandhi belongs to 020; modern reform and equality to 021. Use a small selection of contextualised passages rather than an exhaustive list of doctrines or quotations.

#### 020. [Gandhi: truth, means and ends, service and trusteeship](020-gandhi-truth-means-and-ends-service-and-trusteeship.md)

- **Teaching scope:** Develop Gandhi’s ethical arguments through truth, non-violence, self-discipline, service, the relationship between means and ends, concern for the least advantaged and trusteeship. Connect selected episodes and writings to public life, responsible use of wealth and power, and the tensions involved in applying ideals. Examine objections and practical limits rather than treating a quotation as a complete policy.
- **Learning outcome:** Use a correctly understood Gandhian idea to reason about a contemporary choice and assess a limitation of its application.
- **Prerequisites:** [005](#chapter-005), [006](#chapter-006), [007](#chapter-007).
- **Boundary and hand-off:** Owns Gandhi’s framework and relevant life lessons. Basic approaches are in 005; 030 applies responsibility in business, 033 aid and 043 environmental stewardship. Detailed freedom-movement history belongs to GS I.

<a id="chapter-021"></a>

#### 021. [Social reform, equality and ethical leadership in modern India](021-social-reform-equality-and-ethical-leadership-in-modern-india.md)

- **Teaching scope:** Study selected arguments and actions of Ambedkar, Jyotirao and Savitribai Phule, and Vivekananda around dignity, social equality, education, fraternity, service and personal development. Include a bounded comparison of leadership lessons from figures such as Sardar Patel or A. P. J. Abdul Kalam where evidence supports the lesson. Distinguish a person’s stated ideal, documented conduct and contested legacy.
- **Learning outcome:** Connect a reformer’s or leader’s contribution to a defined ethical problem, with context and a defensible application.
- **Prerequisites:** [003](#chapter-003), [006](#chapter-006), [008](#chapter-008).
- **Boundary and hand-off:** Owns the selected modern Indian reform and leadership contributions. #### 022. [Character and practical wisdom in world moral thought](022-character-and-practical-wisdom-in-world-moral-thought.md)

- **Teaching scope:** Develop selected ideas from Socrates, Aristotle and Confucius around self-examination, character, habituation, practical judgement, relationships and exemplary leadership. - **Boundary and hand-off:** Owns these selected world thinkers on character and ethical cultivation. Chapter 005 introduces virtue as an approach; 023 owns duty and utility; 024 justice frameworks. No complete history of philosophy, Greek politics or Chinese civilisation is required.

#### 023. [Duty, utility and liberty: Kant, Bentham and Mill](023-duty-utility-and-liberty-kant-bentham-and-mill.md)

- **Teaching scope:** Build on the introductory approaches through Kant’s duty, universalisation and respect for persons; Bentham’s assessment of welfare and consequences; and Mill’s account of liberty and harm. - **Prerequisites:** [005](#chapter-005), [006](#chapter-006).

#### 024. [Justice and human development: Rawls and the capability approach](024-justice-and-human-development-rawls-and-the-capability-approach.md)

- **Teaching scope:** Introduce Rawls’s original position, veil of ignorance and principles of justice with simple allocation examples. Compare this with the capability approach associated with Amartya Sen and Martha Nussbaum, defining capabilities as substantive opportunities to do and be things people have reason to value. Examine resources, outcomes, opportunity, disadvantage and reasoned public discussion.

- **Prerequisites:** [006](#chapter-006).

### Phase F — Ethics in public and private administration

<a id="chapter-025"></a>

#### 025. [Laws, rules, regulations and conscience as ethical guides](025-laws-rules-regulations-and-conscience-as-ethical-guides.md)

- **Teaching scope:** Explain sources of formal authority, the distinction between law, rules and regulations, lawful orders and reasons for compliance. Develop conscience, moral reflection, a crisis of conscience and the risk of biased self-certainty. Compare role obligations and personal convictions, including Weber’s concern with responsibility in public roles. Plan how to identify a conflict, seek clarification and justify a response within the applicable framework.
- **Learning outcome:** Evaluate the guidance offered by conscience and formal rules without assuming that either mechanically resolves every ethical issue.
- **Prerequisites:** [003](#chapter-003), [005](#chapter-005), [007](#chapter-007).
- **Boundary and hand-off:** Owns these sources of guidance and their tensions. Chapter 026 teaches discretion and dilemma resolution; 036 formal codes; 041 protected reporting. Legal examples require the relevant current text; detailed jurisprudence belongs to GS II.

#### 026. [Ethical dilemmas, discretion and reasoned administrative action](026-ethical-dilemmas-discretion-and-reasoned-administrative-action.md)

- **Teaching scope:** Develop a fuller decision process: identify established facts and missing information; recognise rights, duties, values and stakeholders; consider genuine alternatives; compare harms, fairness, feasibility and authority; explain the chosen course and safeguards. Address conflicts between rules and their purpose, urgency and due process, compassion and consistency. Plan clarification, documentation, review and proportionate escalation.
- **Learning outcome:** Resolve an administrative dilemma with reasons and an implementable course of action while making uncertainty explicit.
- **Prerequisites:** [004](#chapter-004), [005](#chapter-005), [006](#chapter-006), [025](#chapter-025).
- **Boundary and hand-off:** Owns the general administrative decision method. Conflict of interest has a specific home in 027; 045 integrates multiple learned mechanisms in extended cases. #### 027. [Conflicts of interest, gifts and misuse of entrusted information](027-conflicts-of-interest-gifts-and-misuse-of-entrusted-information.md)

- **Teaching scope:** Define actual, potential and perceived conflicts between public/professional duties and private interests. ### Phase G — Probity and practical safeguards in governance

<a id="chapter-034"></a>

#### 034. [Probity and the causes and consequences of corruption](034-probity-and-the-causes-and-consequences-of-corruption.md)

- **Teaching scope:** Explain probity as uprightness in the exercise of entrusted responsibility and connect it to public trust, public interest and stewardship. #### 041. [Whistleblowing, ethical dissent and protection against retaliation](041-whistleblowing-ethical-dissent-and-protection-against-retaliation.md)

#### 042. [Ethics of digital technology, AI and public communication](042-ethics-of-digital-technology-ai-and-public-communication.md)

#### 043. [Environmental ethics, development and displacement](043-environmental-ethics-development-and-displacement.md)

#### 044. [Health, research and emergency ethics](044-health-research-and-emergency-ethics.md)

- **Teaching scope:** Introduce autonomy, informed consent, beneficence, avoiding harm, confidentiality and fair access through health and research situations. ## 7. Practice, examples and current affairs

Use a simple learning cycle: understand a concept, explain it without the source, inspect a linked PYQ, and outline or write a relevant response. In a short answer, check that the example demonstrates the idea rather than merely naming a person. In a quotation answer, explain the claim and its relevance, consider a limitation if the question calls for it, and avoid a disconnected biography. In a case, answer the actual sub-demands and justify the action; use tables or lists only when they clarify the comparison.

Begin current-affairs work with **one issue related to the chapter being studied**. Record what is known, which claims remain disputed, who is affected, what values or duties conflict, which institution can act and the source/date. Distinguish an allegation, an investigation finding, a judicial decision and a personal interpretation. Build a small bank across public service, business, social life, international relations and technology as understanding grows. No fixed daily news quota or compulsory month window is needed.

Use historical examples with their period identified. A new conduct rule, statutory amendment or commencement, court ruling, revised scheme condition, procurement instruction, AI policy or official investigation finding is a useful trigger to recheck a note. Revisit the 2027 exam instructions when released. Keep observation dates beside statistics; avoid turning a question’s premise into a general current claim.

After writing, distinguish a missing concept from a vague example, ignored sub-demand, unsupported judgement or impractical action. Return to the chapter that teaches the missing mechanism. Move from supported outlines to independent answers, then mixed timed work when coverage permits. Time allocation should be adjusted from actual attempts; the curriculum prescribes neither a universal schedule nor a target score.

Formal tests and evaluation are created **only on request**, under the [Mains test-generation method](../MAINS-TEST-GENERATION.md) and [Mains answer-evaluation method](../MAINS-ANSWER-EVALUATION.md). Placeholder chapters do not support chapter tests. When assessments are requested, preserve questions, the prepared evaluator guide, learner responses and dated evaluation as those methods require. Practice is not official UPSC marking or a rank/percentile forecast.

## Examination orientation and evidence

External sources in this section were consulted on **15 November 2026**; retrieval failures are identified below rather than treated as completed verification.

The **2026 notification** is the rule baseline checked for this plan. Mathematics comprises two conventional written papers, **250 marks and three hours each**, contributing **500 marks to merit**. It is an honours-level optional. Scientific non-programmable calculators are permitted in conventional UPSC examinations; programmable calculators are prohibited. Recheck the CSE 2027 notification and examination instructions when issued. Accessed 15 November 2026.

For planning, the established Mathematics paper pattern is eight questions in two sections, with five answers required: Questions 1 and 5 compulsory, and three others including at least one from each section. The [2025 Paper I](https://www.upsc.gov.in/sites/default/files/MATHEMATICS-PAPER%20I-QP-CSM-25-010925.pdf) front-cover instructions are the historical reference; indexed text from a [mirror of the official paper](https://mathsoptional.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/2025-upsc-maths-optional-question-paper-i.pdf) corroborates the selection rule. **Verification limitation:** the official 2026 paper links were located on the [UPSC question-paper page](https://www.upsc.gov.in/examinations/previous-question-papers), but their PDFs timed out during this work. Marks are attached to written subparts; practise complete reasoning, calculations and conclusions appropriate to the stated demand. The objective-paper negative-marking rule is not a scoring scheme for these descriptive papers. This plan does not assume a fixed marks-per-step rubric or predict scores. Breadth matters because compulsory questions sample several areas and optional main questions can combine topics.

## Diagnostic routing and progression

Diagnostics here are a **planned routing method**, not an administered test. No weakness or exemption has yet been assigned. When substantive bridge material exists and you request a diagnostic, it should sample explanation, execution and transfer, with its guide prepared before your attempt under the [shared assessment rules](../AGENTS.md#shared-assessment-rules). The GS I–IV test format is not a Mathematics test specification.

| Foundation area | Evidence needed to shorten it | Route if evidence is incomplete |
|---|---|---|
| Algebra and functions | Manipulate expressions while retaining domain restrictions; solve equations and inequalities; distinguish a function from its formula | Study the relevant portions of 002 |
| Trigonometry and complex arithmetic | Work in radians, handle inverse-function branches, use identities and complex polar form with justification | Repair 003 before transforms, geometry or complex analysis |
| Geometry and vectors | Translate a geometric condition into coordinates; use scalar and vector products with correct direction/sign | Repair 004 before 024 or mechanics |
| Logic and proof | Read quantifiers, negate a statement, prove a simple implication and distinguish a counterexample from a proof | Complete the needed portions of 005; numerical fluency alone does not waive this |
| Elementary calculus | Explain derivative/integral meaning and execute chain rule, substitution and integration by parts | Repair 006; later formal analysis remains required |
| Physical modelling | Define variables and units, draw forces, distinguish a constraint from an applied force, state idealisations | Repair 007 before dynamics; a Physics degree alone does not waive it |

Use three outcomes at the **skill level**, not one overall percentage: **demonstrated** (skip exposition, retain a later retrieval check), **rusty** (read the relevant recap and practise), or **unfamiliar** (take the full bridge). Record the dated work supporting the decision. An isolated arithmetic slip and a missing theorem hypothesis need different repairs. If later work exposes a gap, reopen only the affected prerequisite.

Within each substantive topic, follow definitions → theorem with hypotheses → proof or justified derivation → worked method → independent application → later retrieval. Include examples and counterexamples to explain why the hypotheses matter. Theory-first should still produce written solutions from the first topic; do not postpone problem solving until all theory is finished.

The phases below are a default learning order, not a lockstep chain. Their prerequisite column controls entry. In particular, abstract algebra can start after 005 and the vector-space introduction; numerical methods need calculus and matrices, not all of complex analysis; mechanics needs its mathematical tools, not completion of abstract algebra. Keep one main new topic and a small review stream rather than opening several unfamiliar subjects at once.

### A workable four-hour day

This is a suggested allocation of your available study time, not an evidence-based completion forecast. Breaks can be taken between blocks.

| Time | Work | Evidence to keep |
|---|---|---|
| 90 minutes | New definitions, theorem hypotheses and proofs; reconstruct key reasoning without looking | A short proof outline with the difficult step identified |
| 45 minutes | Study a worked method, close the solution, reconstruct it and check conditions | A complete reconstruction with corrections visible |
| 75 minutes | Independent problems; introduce topic PYQs as soon as the prerequisites are covered | Dated solutions, including abandoned approaches |
| 30 minutes | Retrieve earlier results and revisit errors; decide the next small task | Updated theorem/method notes and error log |

After finishing a topic, shift some theory time to mixed problems and delayed re-solving. Once ready for whole papers, a three-hour paper plus one hour of initial review fits the daily allocation; substantial repair may take another session. Timed practice begins with short, already-taught problem groups and grows to sections and whole papers. It does not replace initial learning.

Advance when you can explain the central definitions, reconstruct the main argument, select and execute a method in an unfamiliar but in-scope problem, and check the result. If you can only repeat a worked solution, remain with that topic. Reassess pace after an actual bridge, an abstract topic and a computational topic; four hours daily alone cannot justify a completion-date promise. Use the 2027 notification, your broader Prelims plan and observed progress to adjust calendar allocation later.

### Practice and revision habits

- Keep a **theorem record** containing the exact assumptions, conclusion, proof idea, an example and a failure case when a hypothesis is removed. Keep a separate **method record** for when a procedure applies and how to check it.
- Work through a small progression: reconstruct a taught example, solve a routine variant, then attempt a new in-scope problem or topic PYQ. Review a solution only after preserving your own attempt; later re-solve without the solution.
- For a proof, identify the claim and quantify variables. For a computation, record the method and necessary intermediate steps. For mechanics/fluids, state assumptions and draw the geometry. For numerical work, record precision and stopping conditions. Finish with an appropriate check such as substitution, residual, dimensions, boundary conditions or a limiting case.
- Diagnose errors from the written work: missing concept, invalid inference, wrong method choice, algebra/sign slip, incomplete cases, presentation or recorded timing difficulty. Record the correction and reappearance, rather than assuming carelessness, guessing or time pressure.
- Revisit earlier topics during the daily review block and after finishing a connected topic. Use delayed recall of hypotheses as well as formulas. Increase mixed work when individual methods become reliable; choose the timing from observed retention rather than a fixed retest calendar.
- Before a full paper, check that all syllabus areas have teaching coverage and some independent practice. Use the actual front-cover rules and inspect every subpart before choosing a main question. Do not assume optional-question choice allows an entire syllabus heading to be dropped.

The first concrete learning step is chapter 001, whose skill record and first-bridge choice come before the foundation material; then record the evidence for your route. Chapter writing and assessment remain separate requested tasks; this curriculum establishes their scope and order.

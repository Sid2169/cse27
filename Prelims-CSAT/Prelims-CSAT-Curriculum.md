# Prelims General Studies Paper II (CSAT) — Curriculum

Prepared: 18 November 2026 for CSE 2027 preparation. This is a study plan and the authoritative chapter-file index, not authored teaching material. All chapter links below are **planned placeholders**. No diagnostic, practice test or chapter teaching content is generated by this curriculum.

## 1. Understand the paper and begin

CSAT is the commonly used name for Preliminary General Studies Paper II. It assesses reading and reasoning with school-level numeracy. A degree, fluent conversational English or previous success in mathematics does not by itself establish readiness under examination conditions.

The rules below use the [official 2026 notification](https://www.upsc.gov.in/sites/default/files/Notif-CSP-2026-Engl-060226Rev.pdf), Appendix I, Sections II(A) and III(A), and the negative-marking provisions preceding Section II. They are the preparation baseline; recheck the 2027 notification, corrigenda and actual booklet instructions when available.

| Feature | Preparation baseline |
|---|---|
| Format and duration | Objective MCQs; 200 marks; two hours. The populated recent PYQ files record 80 questions, giving 2.5 marks per question. Verify the question count in the year's official booklet. |
| Qualifying role | Minimum 33%, or 66 out of 200 by arithmetic. GS I supplies the marks used for the Prelims selection threshold once CSAT is qualified; Prelims marks do not enter final merit. |
| Wrong, blank and multiple responses | One-third of the question's marks deducted for a wrong response; no penalty for a blank; multiple responses count as wrong. At 2.5 marks each, use an exact deduction of 5/6 mark in practice calculations rather than repeatedly rounding it. |
| Language | Papers are set in Hindi and English. This curriculum teaches in English; choose the reading medium through actual practice. Hindi as Mains Paper A does not determine the CSAT reading medium. |
| Attendance and equipment | Appearance in both Prelims papers is mandatory for evaluation. Calculators are not permitted in objective papers. Practise on paper without one. |

For an ordinary 80-question practice paper, score = `2.5 × correct − (2.5/3) × wrong`; unattempted questions add zero. For example, 32 correct and 12 wrong yield 70 marks. This illustrates scoring, not a recommended attempt count. A small margin above 66 is vulnerable to errors; build a repeatable margin on complete, unseen official papers rather than treating one passing attempt as readiness. No fixed attempt count, topic allocation or coaching “safe score” is an official rule.

**Start here:** read the syllabus, understand the scoring, and open chapter 001 when its teaching content is authored. Before selecting a route, review your own recent school-maths work and reading experience: can you handle fractions and unit conversions, explain a short paragraph in your own words, and turn verbal conditions into a diagram? This is routing guidance, not a generated assessment. With no reliable evidence, start with the bridge in 002–005 and reading in 006 in parallel. A learner who can already explain these operations may move faster through the bridge; repair any gap revealed later.

## 2. Evidence and limits of the PYQ corpus

Use the [local PYQ folder](Prelims-CSAT-PYQS/) as the working corpus. Files for 2014–2026 contain typed questions; [2013](Prelims-CSAT-PYQS/2013-Prelims-CSAT.md) remains an extraction placeholder. The files identify their compilation source, access date and verification caveats. Local question numbers below refer to that compilation, and may differ across official booklet series: match the stem before consulting an official key.

Missing numbered items are explicit, including 2014 Q6, Q34–37, Q39–40, Q42–45 and Q70–75; 2018 Q1, Q5, Q11–16, Q61–67, Q70–73 and Q76; 2025 Q35–36; and 2026 Q7, Q26, Q28, Q32, Q57, Q62, Q64 and Q69. Other files carry their own gaps. “80 of 80” means numbered items were extracted, not that every passage, common direction or figure is present. For example, 2025 Q1–4 refer to passages absent from the typed file. Such items show the question demand but cannot support a comprehension attempt or reliable scoring without the original passage. Similar checks apply to diagram and shared-condition questions.

Qualitative planning signals from the available corpus are:

- Comprehension asks for central ideas, implications, assumptions and defensible conclusions, not just recognition of words: see 2017 Q1–8 and Q41–48; 2025 Q1–4. Restore the original passages for practice.
- Numeracy combines basic operations with constraints: 2017 Q49 and Q73 on digits and divisibility; 2025 Q5 and Q10 on powers; 2026 Q59–60 and Q66 on number relations.
- Reasoning requires explicit representations: 2014 Q8–11; 2017 Q13 and Q59; 2026 Q34–36 and Q48. Some items require checking all possible cases rather than finding one convenient arrangement.
- Communication and interpersonal skills remain explicit syllabus areas. The local 2026 file includes communication types, documents, barriers, conflict and questionnaire design at Q72–77. Include these areas without assuming the same distribution will recur in 2027.

These are examples, not a numerical topic-weightage analysis or forecast. Retain the whole syllabus irrespective of past frequency. Historical papers such as 2014 are useful for skills, but their examination-era scoring and qualifying arrangements must not be imported into current practice. Copied answers are not independently verified official keys merely because the compiler labels them so. If a key conflicts with a worked solution, record the conflict and check the matching official booklet/key; do not silently correct the corpus or learn an unexplained answer.

## 3. Phases and flexible learning sequence

The phases below refer to the single index in section 4. They are not deadlines or mandatory chapter-completion quotas.

| Phase | Chapters | Purpose and progression signal |
|---|---|---|
| A — Orientation and bridge | 001–005 | Understand the paper and recover operations, notation, estimation and verbal translation. Move forward when you can explain the operations and check units; revisit individual gaps as needed. |
| B — Reading and argument | 006–010 | Begin alongside the bridge. Progress from sentence meaning to passage structure, inference and assumptions; justify both the chosen option and rejection of tempting alternatives. Continue reading throughout later phases. |
| C — Arithmetic applications | 011–021 | Build number relations, ratios and percentages before rates, money and mixtures. Learn to choose a representation before calculating. |
| D — Reasoning and mental ability | 022–030 | Begin elementary logic after 005; it need not wait for all arithmetic. Use 004 and 011 before numerical patterns, and 018 before clock-rate applications. |
| E — Data interpretation and sufficiency | 031–033 | Start chart reading after ratios, percentages and averages. Distinguish finding a value from deciding whether information determines it uniquely. |
| F — Communication and decisions | 034–036 | Start after basic reading. Build everyday communication vocabulary, then apply it to workplace situations and constrained choices. |
| G — Integration and revision | 037–040 | Integrate all streams. Gradually introduce timing, complete papers and response-sheet discipline; return to the chapter responsible for each recurring error. |

Keep reading active while studying arithmetic or reasoning. A manageable study sitting can combine one new idea, worked examples with reasons for each step, independent problems, and review of one earlier error. On a short day, do one strand well and resume the others next time. Increase timing pressure only after you can produce and explain sound untimed solutions. A learner struggling in one strand should continue independent strands while repairing its prerequisites.

## 6. Resources and practice habits

**Essential:** the official syllabus and current notification establish scope and rules. The local PYQs provide convenient topic access; the [UPSC question-paper archive](https://www.upsc.gov.in/examinations/previous-question-papers) supplies complete passages, diagrams and instructions, with [older papers here](https://www.upsc.gov.in/examinations/previous-question-papers/archives). Use the matching official answer key from [UPSC Answer Keys](https://www.upsc.gov.in/examinations/answer-key) when published. A third-party solution can explain a method but is not the official marking authority. Do not score an incomplete local extraction as a complete 200-mark paper.

# 015. India's location and physical regions

- **Paper:** Prelims General Studies I, UPSC CSE 2027.
- **Curriculum entry:** [015 — India's location and physical regions](Prelims-General-Studies-I-Curriculum.md#chapter-015).
- **Prerequisites:** Chapters 003–004, 007–014.
- **Scope:** India's extent, neighbours, states/territories, Himalayas, plains, plateau, desert, coasts and islands.
- **Learning outcome:** Reconstruct India's relief and regional relationships on a blank map.
- **Terms defined:** Peninsula; plateau; pass; coastal plain; island group.

---

## India's location and strategic position

India occupies the southern portion of the Asian continent, projecting as a triangular **peninsula** — a landmass bounded by water on three sides — into the Indian Ocean between the Arabian Sea (west) and the Bay of Bengal (east). Its central location gives it both overland and oceanic access: the Himalayan arc connects it to Central and East Asia, while the Indian Ocean places it on major shipping lanes between Europe and East/Southeast Asia. The Tropic of Cancer (23°30′N) divides the country into roughly two equal halves, so India spans both the tropics and the subtropics ([NCERT][ncert-location]).

| Dimension | Value |
|---|---|
| Latitude (mainland) | 8°4′N to 37°6′N |
| Latitude (including islands) | 6°45′N (Indira Point, Great Nicobar) to 37°6′N |
| Longitude | 68°7′E to 97°25′E |
| North–south distance | 3,214 km |
| East–west distance | 2,933 km |
| Area | 3.28 million km² (2.4% of world land; 7th largest country) |
| Mainland coastline | ~6,100 km (NCERT figure) |
| Total coastline (with islands) | ~7,517 km (NCERT); revised to ~11,099 km (Ministry of Ports, 2025) |

**Standard meridian and Indian Standard Time (IST):** India uses a single time zone whose meridian is 82°30′E, passing near Mirzapur, Uttar Pradesh. IST is UTC + 5 hours 30 minutes. Because India spans nearly 30° of longitude, local time can differ by almost two hours between Arunachal Pradesh in the east and Gujarat/Rajasthan in the west, but the country maintains one standard for administrative convenience ([NCERT][ncert-location]).

**Tropic of Cancer** passes through eight states from west to east: Gujarat → Rajasthan → Madhya Pradesh → Chhattisgarh → Jharkhand → West Bengal → Tripura → Mizoram ([NCERT][ncert-location]).

**Longitude check (PYQ):** Delhi lies at approximately 77°13′E. Among Bengaluru (~77°36′E), Hyderabad (~78°28′E), Nagpur (~79°05′E) and Pune (~73°51′E), **Bengaluru** is closest to Delhi's longitude [2018 Q37][pyq2018].

**Sea neighbours:** Sri Lanka (separated by the Gulf of Mannar and Palk Strait); Maldives (south of Lakshadweep).

**Natural barriers:** To the north, the Himalaya, Hindukush and Sulaiman ranges; to the northeast, the Purvachal hills. These formed historically significant corridors through mountain passes while shielding the subcontinent from land invasions ([NCERT][ncert-location]).

## Maritime zones

India ratified the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS, 1982). Its maritime zones extend outward from a **baseline** drawn along the low-water mark of the coast ([NCERT][ncert-location]):

| Zone | Extent from baseline | Sovereignty / rights |
|---|---|---|
| Territorial sea | Up to 12 nautical miles | Full sovereignty; ships of all states have **innocent passage** |
| Contiguous zone | 12–24 nautical miles | Enforcement of customs, fiscal, immigration and sanitary laws |
| Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ) | Up to 200 nautical miles | Sovereign rights over all resources (living and non-living); not full sovereignty |
| Continental shelf | Up to 200 nm (or beyond, if the shelf extends further) | Sovereign rights over seabed and subsoil resources |

UPSC has tested all three UNCLOS statements directly: the 12 nm territorial sea, innocent passage for all ships, and the 200 nm EEZ limit are all correct [2022 Q29][pyq2022].

## India's six physical divisions

A **plateau** is a raised, relatively flat-topped landmass; a **pass** is a low gap in a mountain ridge used for crossing. India is conventionally divided into six major physiographic regions ([NCERT][ncert-physio]):

1. Northern and north-eastern mountains (Himalayas and Purvachal)
2. Northern plains (Indo-Gangetic-Brahmaputra plain)
3. Peninsular plateau (Deccan, Central Highlands, Chotanagpur)
4. Indian Desert (Thar/Marusthali)
5. Coastal plains (Western and Eastern)
6. Islands (Andaman & Nicobar; Lakshadweep)

---

## 1. Northern and north-eastern mountains

### Himalayan ranges (south to north)

The Himalayas form an arc of about 2,500 km from the Indus gorge (west) to the Brahmaputra gorge (east). Three parallel ranges run broadly west-to-east:

**Shiwalik (Outer Himalaya):** The southernmost and youngest range, 10–50 km wide and 900–1,100 m high, made of loose sediments deposited by rivers. River-eroded, flat-floored valleys between the Shiwalik and the Lesser Himalaya are called **duns** (Dehra Dun, Kotli Dun, Patli Dun).

**Himachal (Lesser Himalaya):** About 50 km wide and 3,700–4,500 m high. Includes the Pir Panjal, Dhaula Dhar and Mahabharat ranges. The Pir Panjal is crossed by the Banihal Pass (highway) and the Jawahar Tunnel (rail); Zoji La crosses the Greater Himalaya into Ladakh. Important valleys: Kashmir (between Greater Himalaya and Pir Panjal), Kangra, Kullu. Hill stations (Shimla, Mussoorie, Nainital, Darjeeling) lie in this belt.

**Himadri (Greater / Inner Himalaya):** The highest, most continuous range, with a granite core and permanent snow and glaciers. Average height about 6,000 m. All prominent Himalayan peaks rise here:

| Peak | Height | Location |
|---|---|---|
| Mount Everest | 8,849 m | Nepal (south face shared with India context) |
| Kanchenjunga | 8,586 m | Sikkim–Nepal border |
| Nanda Devi | 7,817 m | Kumaon Himalaya, Uttarakhand |
| Kamet | 7,756 m | Uttarakhand |

**Peak–mountain matching (PYQ):** Nanda Devi correctly belongs to the **Kumaon Himalaya**. Namcha Barwa (7,756 m) is on the great bend of the Tsangpo in Arunachal Pradesh, not Garhwal. Nokrek is a biosphere reserve in the Garo Hills of Meghalaya (Shillong Plateau), not the Sikkim Himalaya — only Nanda Devi → Kumaon Himalaya is a correct match [2022 Q50][pyq2022].

### Trans-Himalaya

North of the Himadri lie the Karakoram, Ladakh and Zaskar ranges, together called the Trans-Himalaya. This is a cold, arid high plateau — a rain shadow desert. The world's second highest peak, K2 (8,611 m), lies in the Karakoram. Major glaciers include Siachen and Baltoro. The Khardung La pass (Ladakh Range) is among the highest motor roads in the world.

### Regional divisions of the Himalaya

The Himalaya is divided into regional sections by river valleys:

| Section | Between rivers |
|---|---|
| Punjab/Kashmir Himalaya | Indus–Satluj |
| Kumaon (Himachal/Uttarakhand) | Satluj–Kali |
| Nepal Himalaya | Kali–Teesta |
| Sikkim/Darjeeling Himalaya | Teesta–Dihang |
| Arunachal Himalaya | Dihang gorge and beyond |

**States crossed by Himalayas (PYQ):** The Himalayas extend across more than five states — in fact across J&K, Ladakh, Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Sikkim, Arunachal Pradesh, and other northeastern states. The statement that the Himalayas are spread over "five states only" is wrong. Western Ghats spread across six states (Gujarat, Maharashtra, Goa, Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu), not five. Only statement 3 of [2017 Q71][pyq2017] — that Pulicat Lake is spread across two states (Tamil Nadu and Andhra Pradesh) — is correct [2017 Q71][pyq2017].

### Purvachal (north-eastern hills)

Beyond the Dihang gorge, the Himalaya bends southward as the Purvachal — the north-eastern hills: Patkai Bum, Naga Hills, Manipur Hills and Mizo (Lushai) Hills. These form India's border with Myanmar.

## 2. Northern plains

The northern plain is formed by sediment deposited by the Indus, Ganga and Brahmaputra river systems. It extends about 3,200 km east-to-west and is 150–300 km wide, with alluvium 1,000–2,000 m deep and elevation generally 50–150 m — almost flat, highly fertile ([NCERT][ncert-physio]).

**Four belts from the Himalayas southward:**

| Belt | Character |
|---|---|
| **Bhabar** | 8–10 km wide, coarse boulders; rivers disappear underground |
| **Tarai** | 10–20 km wide; streams re-emerge, marshy, dense forest and wildlife |
| **Bhangar** | Older alluvium above flood level; contains *kankar* (lime nodules) |
| **Khadar** | Newer alluvium on floodplains, renewed almost every year; most fertile |

Haryana and Delhi form the water divide between the Indus drainage (west) and the Ganga drainage (east). The Brahmaputra plain in Assam shows meanders, ox-bow lakes, braided channels and seasonal flooding. The Sundarbans delta, formed by the Ganga–Brahmaputra system, is one of the world's largest deltas.

## 3. Peninsular plateau

The **Peninsular Plateau** is an ancient, stable block of crystalline rock, the remnant of the Gondwana landmass. It is a triangular tableland rising from about 150 m near the northern plains to 600–900 m at its highest parts. Its outer limits are the Delhi ridge (northern Aravalli extension), Rajmahal hills (northeast), Gir range (west) and Cardamom hills (south) ([NCERT][ncert-physio]).

### Central Highlands

Lying north of the Narmada and south of the Indo-Gangetic plain, the Central Highlands include the Vindhya, Kaimur, Satpura, Maikal and Mahadeo ranges and the Malwa Plateau. They slope northward, draining into the Yamuna and Chambal. The Aravalli hills run northeast-to-southwest across Rajasthan into Delhi.

**Hill region facts (PYQ):** Amarkantak is at the confluence of the Vindhya and Maikal ranges (not Sahyadri/Western Ghats). Biligirirangan Hills (BRT) are in Karnataka — a biodiversity link between the Eastern and Western Ghats, not part of the Satpura range. Seshachalam Hills are part of the Eastern Ghats in Andhra Pradesh, not the Western Ghats. All three statements in [2023 Q57][pyq2023] are wrong; the answer is none.

### Deccan Plateau

The Deccan Plateau proper lies south of the Satpura–Narmada line, bounded by the Western Ghats to the west and the Eastern Ghats to the east. The two Ghats meet in the Nilgiris in Tamil Nadu. Black (regur) soil derived from the Deccan Traps covers much of the western and northwestern Deccan, well suited to cotton cultivation.

**Western Ghats** run continuously from the Tapti valley to Kanyakumari, acting as a orographic barrier to the southwest monsoon. Highest peaks: Anaimudi (2,695 m, Anaimalai hills) and Dodabetta (2,637 m, Nilgiris). Important passes: Thal, Bhor and Pal Ghats.

**Eastern Ghats** are discontinuous, cut by the eastward-draining rivers (Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri). They are lower (average 600 m) and less prominent than the Western Ghats.

**Ecological link (PYQ):** Sathyamangalam Tiger Reserve in the Nilgiris/Eastern Ghats transition zone is considered the most important ecological link between the Western and Eastern Ghats [2017 Q18][pyq2017].

**Waterfalls (PYQ):** Dhuandhar Falls are on the **Narmada** at Bhedaghat/Jabalpur. Hundru Falls are on the **Subarnarekha** in the Chota Nagpur plateau (Jharkhand) — both correctly placed. Gersoppa (Jog Falls) are on the **Sharavati** River in Karnataka, not the Netravati — the third row is wrongly matched; only one row is fully correct [2024 Q12][pyq2024].

### Chotanagpur Plateau and north-eastern plateau

Chotanagpur plateau (Jharkhand) is the southernmost extension of the Peninsula, rich in coal, iron ore and other minerals. To the northeast, the Meghalaya (Shillong) Plateau — with Garo, Khasi and Jaintia hills — is separated from the main peninsular block by a fault. Cherrapunji (Sohra) on the Khasi hills receives some of the world's highest rainfall; the bare surface results from soil erosion. Nokrek peak in the Garo Hills is a biosphere reserve.

## 4. Indian Desert (Thar / Marusthali)

The Thar Desert lies northwest of the Aravalli hills, mainly in western Rajasthan with extensions into Gujarat. Annual rainfall is generally below 150 mm. The terrain is undulating, with longitudinal dunes (parallel to wind direction) and barchans (crescent-shaped dunes). The **Luni** is the only significant river, draining southwestward into the Rann of Kachchh. Sambhar Lake is a saline lake used for salt production. The Indira Gandhi Canal (Rajasthan Canal) has brought irrigation to parts of the desert. Fossil wood ~180 million years old at Aakal and marine deposits near Jaisalmer indicate the area was once a seabed ([NCERT][ncert-physio]).

## 5. Coastal plains

**A coastal plain** is a flat lowland strip between the sea and an inland elevated area.

### Western coastal plain

The western coast runs from Gujarat in the north to Kerala in the south — about 1,500 km. It is narrow (a submerged, drowned coastline with no deltas), so rivers form estuaries rather than deltas. The Narmada and Tapi are rift-valley rivers flowing westward. Natural harbours include Kandla, Mazagaon (Mumbai), Jawaharlal Nehru Port, Marmagao, Mangalore and Cochin.

Named sections north to south: **Kachchh and Kathiawar** (Gujarat) → **Konkan** (Maharashtra/Goa) → **Goan coast** → **Malabar coast** (Karnataka/Kerala). The Malabar coast has *kayals* (backwater lagoons) used for fishing, transport and tourism; Punnamada Kayal hosts the Nehru Trophy boat race.

### Eastern coastal plain

The eastern coast runs from West Bengal to Tamil Nadu — about 1,100 km. It is broader (an emergent coastline), with well-developed deltas of the Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna and Kaveri. Fewer natural harbours because of the wide, shallow continental shelf (up to 500 km).

Named sections: **Northern Circars** (Odisha and northern Andhra) → **Coromandel Coast** (southern Andhra and Tamil Nadu). **Chilika Lake** (Odisha), south of the Mahanadi delta, is India's largest brackish-water lake and a Ramsar wetland.

**Coral reefs (PYQ):** Coral reefs exist in the Andaman and Nicobar Islands, Gulf of Kutch and Gulf of Mannar. The Sundarbans is a mangrove delta in a tidal estuarine environment and does not support coral reefs [2014 Q44][pyq2014].

## 6. Islands

**An island group** is a cluster of islands sharing a common geologic or geographic origin.

### Andaman and Nicobar Islands (Bay of Bengal)

About 572 islands and islets, scattered between ~6°N and 14°N and 92°E–94°E. The **Ten Degree Channel** at 10°N latitude separates the **Andaman** group (north) from the **Nicobar** group (south) [2014 Q88][pyq2014]. The islands are of tectonic and volcanic origin, with dense tropical forest. **Barren Island**, in the Andaman group, is India's only active volcano. Principal peaks: Saddle Peak (738 m, North Andaman). The southernmost point of India — **Indira Point** — is on Great Nicobar Island.

### Lakshadweep (Arabian Sea)

About 36 coral islands and islets totalling ~32 km², lying 220–440 km off the Kerala coast. All are of **coral/coralline** origin — atolls and coral reefs. They are divided by the **Nine Degree Channel** (9°N latitude), with the Amini group to the north and the Cannanore group to the south. The island territory is administered from Kavaratti. Minicoy Island lies furthest south, closest to the Maldives.

| Northern Mountains | Himalayas + Purvachal | Glacial; thin mountain soil | Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra |
| Northern Plains | Flat alluvial plain | Deep alluvium (Khadar, Bhangar) | Ganga, Indus, Brahmaputra |
| Peninsular Plateau | Ancient stable block | Regur (black), laterite | Eastward rivers (Godavari, Krishna); westward (Narmada, Tapi) |

| Islands | A&N (tectonic/volcanic); Lakshadweep (coral) | Coral; volcanic | Short hill streams |

## Glossary

**Peninsula:** A landmass projecting into the sea and surrounded by water on three sides, connected to a larger body of land on one side.

**Plateau:** A large area of elevated, relatively flat or rolling land that rises steeply from the surrounding terrain on at least one side.

**Pass (mountain pass):** A low gap or col in a mountain ridge, allowing passage from one side to the other. Examples: Zoji La (Greater Himalaya, Kashmir–Ladakh), Banihal (Pir Panjal, Kashmir), Khardung La (Ladakh Range).

**Coastal plain:** A flat, low-lying strip of land between the shoreline and an inland elevated area, formed by sediment deposition or by the emergence of the sea floor.

**Island group:** A cluster of islands that share a common geographic area, origin or administrative jurisdiction. India has two principal island groups: the Andaman and Nicobar Islands (Bay of Bengal) and Lakshadweep (Arabian Sea).

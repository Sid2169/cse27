## Why rivers matter to the exam

River questions are among the most frequently tested topics in Prelims GS-I. They demand precise knowledge: which river flows through which national park, which tributaries belong to which basin, which river shaped a particular landform, why a river flows in an unusual direction. The 2013–2024 set includes at least eighteen questions tagged to this chapter. Getting them right is mostly about knowing your river systems cold — there is no guesswork when you have mapped each system carefully.

---

## Core vocabulary

A **drainage basin** (or catchment) is the total land area whose rain and snowmelt drain into a single river. The boundary ridge separating two basins is called the **drainage divide** or watershed. Within a basin, a **tributary** is any stream that feeds the main river. When a river nears the sea it may fan out into distributaries — channels that carry water away from the main stem toward the ocean. At the mouth, fine sediment deposited by distributaries builds a **delta**; where a coast is submerging or waves are strong, the river mouth stays open as an **estuary** instead.

**Drainage patterns** describe the geometric arrangement of a river network:
- **Dendritic** — branches like a tree; forms where the underlying rock is uniform and the land slopes gently (Ganga plains).
- **Trellis** — tributaries join the main river at nearly right angles, typical of folded mountain belts (parts of the Himalayas).
- **Radial** — rivers radiate outward from a central dome (Amarkantak hills feed both the Narmada and Son, plus other streams).
- **Centripetal** — rivers flow inward toward a depression or lake (drainage into the Rann of Kachchh and some Himalayan basins).

## Two families of rivers

India's rivers divide into two fundamentally different families based on their origin, age, and behaviour:

| Feature | Himalayan rivers | Peninsular rivers |
|---------|-----------------|-------------------|
| **Age** | Younger (antecedent — predated and cut through the rising mountains) | Very old — formed on the ancient stable Gondwana block |
| **Flow** | Perennial: snowmelt + rain | Mostly seasonal: rain-fed; dry or thin in summer |
| **Mountain behaviour** | Deep gorges, V-shaped valleys, rapids, waterfalls, tortuous courses | Broad, shallow, graded valleys; falls only at escarpments |
| **Plain behaviour** | Meanders, shifting channels, ox-bow lakes, braided reaches, large deltas | Fixed, straight courses; no meanders |
| **Sediment load** | Very high (young, soft rock) | Moderate to low (hard, stable Peninsular rocks) |
| **Basin size** | Very large (Ganga ~8.6 lakh km²; Indus ~3.2 lakh km² in India) | Smaller and bounded by the Western Ghats divide |
| **Main drainage direction** | Broadly south and southwest | Mostly east (Bay of Bengal); Narmada/Tapi west |

About **77 %** of India's drainage area empties into the Bay of Bengal; about **23 %** into the Arabian Sea. The drainage divides are the **Delhi Ridge, the Aravallis, and the Western Ghats (Sahyadris)** [sathee-iitk][].

## Himalayan drainage

### How the Himalayan river system formed

An ancient river called **Indo-Brahma** once flowed the full length of what is now the Himalayan arc (Miocene epoch, roughly 5–24 million years ago). Progressive uplift of the western Himalayas and the formation of the Malda gap in the mid-Pleistocene split it into the three systems we know today: Indus, Ganga, and Brahmaputra. Because these rivers predate the mountains they cross, they maintain their courses by cutting gorges faster than the mountains rise — this is **antecedence**.

```
         KARAKORAM / ZANSKAR / HIMALAYAS (W→E)
         ─────────────────────────────────────
 Indus ←←(cuts through)      Ganga system       Brahmaputra→→
 (Arabian Sea)        (Bay of Bengal)          (Bay of Bengal)
         ─────────────────────────────────────
              Delhi Ridge / Aravallis / Sahyadris
              ← 23% Arabian Sea | 77% Bay of Bengal →
```

### The Indus system

The Indus rises near **Bokhar Chu** in the Kailash Range of Tibet at about 4,164 m, known locally as *Singi Khamban* ("Lion's Mouth") [ncert-11-geo][]. Its total length is approximately **2,880 km**, of which only about **1,114 km** lies within modern India (Ladakh). The river enters Pakistan near Chilas before cutting south to discharge into the Arabian Sea east of Karachi. The total basin is about 11,65,000 km²; India's share is roughly 3,21,289 km².

**Left-bank Himalayan tributaries in India:** Shyok, Gilgit, Zaskar, Nubra, Hunza, Shigar.

The five **Punjab rivers** (Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas, Satluj) join as the **Panjnad** above Mithankot and then flow into the Indus in Pakistan. Under the Indus Waters Treaty (1960), the three eastern rivers — Ravi, Beas, Satluj — are allocated to India; the three western — Indus, Jhelum, Chenab — to Pakistan.

| Punjab river | Origin | Key fact |
|-------------|--------|---------|
| **Jhelum** | Verinag spring, Kashmir | Passes through Dal Lake and Wular Lake; joins Chenab near Jhang |
| **Chenab** | Chandra + Bhaga join at Tandi, Keylong (also called Chandrabhaga) | Longest Punjab tributary; about 1,180 km |
| **Ravi** | West of Rohtang Pass | Joins Chenab near Sarai Sidhu |
| **Beas** | Beas Kund, near Rohtang Pass (~4,000 m) | Forms gorges at Kati and Largi; joins Satluj near Harike |
| **Satluj** | Raksas Tal near Mansarovar (4,555 m); called Langchen Khambab in Tibet | Antecedent river; feeds the Bhakra–Nangal canal system |

**Harike wetland** ([2014 Q35][pyq2014]) forms at the confluence of the **Beas and Satluj** near Harike, Punjab — not Beas–Ravi or any other pairing.

### The Ganga system

The Ganga system drains India's most populous region and is the country's hydrological and cultural spine.

**Source and course:** The river begins as the **Bhagirathi** at the **Gangotri glacier** near Gaumukh (3,900 m) [bpscpathshala][]. At **Devprayag** the Bhagirathi meets the **Alaknanda** and becomes the Ganga. It enters the plains at Haridwar, runs for about 2,525 km, and discharges into the Bay of Bengal near Sagar Island — forming, with the Brahmaputra, the **Sundarbans delta** (the world's largest mangrove delta).

The **Prayags** (sacred river meetings) along the Alaknanda and upper Ganga:

```
  Vishnuprayag — Dhauli + Vishnu Ganga → Alaknanda
  Nandaprayag  — Nandakini joins
  Karnaprayag  — Pindar joins
  Rudraprayag  — Mandakini (Kali Ganga) joins
  Devprayag    — Bhagirathi + Alaknanda → GANGA
```

**Tributaries — right bank (south):** Yamuna, Son.
**Tributaries — left bank (north):** Ramganga, Gomati, Ghaghara, Gandak, Kosi, Mahananda.

**West-to-east order below Prayagraj** ([2024 Q70][pyq2024]):
Gomati → Ghaghara → Gandak → Kosi — answer (b).

The correct mnemonic is *Go-Gha-Ga-Ko* (from west to east):

```
Prayagraj
  ↓ Gomati (joins near Jaunpur / Varanasi area)
  ↓ Ghaghara (joins near Chhapra)
  ↓ Gandak (joins near Sonpur, Patna)
  ↓ Kosi (joins near Kursela, Bhagalpur area)
      ↓ Farakka → Bay of Bengal
```

**Key tributaries in detail:**

| Tributary | Bank | Origin | Distinctive fact |
|-----------|------|---------|-----------------|
| **Yamuna** | Right | Yamunotri Glacier | Longest Ganga tributary; right-bank: Chambal, Betwa, Ken, Sind; joins Ganga at Prayagraj |
| **Chambal** | (via Yamuna) | Mhow, Malwa Plateau | Gandisagar Dam (Kota); famous ravines (badland topography) |
| **Son** | Right | Amarkantak Plateau | Major right-bank tributary; joins near Arrah, west of Patna |
| **Ramganga** | Left | Garhwal (near Gairsain) | Flows through Corbett National Park — not the Ganga ([2013 Q70][pyq2013]) |
| **Gomati** | Left | Pilibhit, Uttar Pradesh | Passes through Lucknow |
| **Ghaghara** | Left | Mapchachungo Glacier; joined by Sarda (Saryu) in plains | Joins Ganga near Chhapra |
| **Gandak** | Left | Kaligandak + Trishulganga (Nepal) | Joins Ganga at Sonpur near Patna; its old meanders formed Kanwar Lake (Bihar) |
| **Kosi** | Left | Arun River (north of Everest) → Sapt Kosi | "Sorrow of Bihar": antecedent river, shifts course frequently due to heavy sediment |
| **Damodar** | (via Hugli) | Chotanagpur plateau (rift valley) | "Sorrow of Bengal" historically; now managed by DVC; main tributary: Barakar |
| **Mahananda** | Left | Darjeeling hills | Last left-bank tributary before Bengal |

**Corbett–Ramganga connection:** The Ramganga flows through Jim Corbett National Park — not the Ganga. Kaziranga is on the Brahmaputra (not Manas). Silent Valley is on the Kuntipuzha (not Kaveri). All three pairs in [2013 Q70][pyq2013] are incorrectly matched; answer is (d) None.

**Ganges Dolphin and threats** ([2014 Q78][pyq2014]): The Ganges River Dolphin (*Platanista gangetica*) is threatened by (1) dams and barrages fragmenting its river habitat, (3) accidental entanglement in fishing nets, and (4) agricultural chemicals entering the river. Crocodiles (statement 2) are not a significant driver of dolphin decline — answer (c) 1, 3, 4 only.

### The Brahmaputra system

The Brahmaputra is one of the world's largest rivers by discharge.

**Origin and Tibet:** Rises at the **Chemayungdung Glacier** in the Kailash Range near Lake Mansarovar; called **Tsangpo** in Tibet, where it flows approximately 1,200 km east through a broad, flat, relatively dry valley.

**Entry into India:** Near **Namcha Barwa** (7,755 m), the Tsangpo makes a dramatic U-turn, carves one of the world's deepest gorges, and enters Arunachal Pradesh as the **Siang** (also called Dihang). After the Dibang and Lohit join from the left near Sadiya, the combined river becomes the **Brahmaputra**. It then flows about 750 km west through the Assam Valley before turning south into Bangladesh.

**Teesta connection** ([2017 Q57][pyq2017]): The question presents three statements:
Statement 1 — "The source of Teesta is the same as that of Brahmaputra" → **FALSE**. The Brahmaputra rises at Chemayungdung glacier near Lake Mansarovar (Tibet); the Teesta rises in North Sikkim from the Tso Lhamo lake area. Different sources, different ranges.
Statement 2 — "Rangeet originates in Sikkim and is a tributary of the Teesta" → **TRUE**. The Rangeet (Rangit) rises in West Sikkim and joins the Teesta south of Gangtok.
Statement 3 — "Teesta flows into the Bay of Bengal on the India–Bangladesh border" → **FALSE**. The Teesta enters Bangladesh from West Bengal and joins the Brahmaputra (Jamuna) inside Bangladesh; it does not reach the Bay of Bengal independently.
Only statement 2 is correct. Answer: **(b) 2 only**.

| Bank | Tributaries |
|------|------------|
| **Left** | Dibang (Sikang), Lohit, Burhi Dihing, Dhansari |
| **Right** | Subansiri (antecedent; from Tibet), Kameng, Manas, Sankosh, Tista |

**Rivers in Arunachal Pradesh** ([2014 Q89][pyq2014]): The question asks which of Lohit, Subansiri, Barak originate in Arunachal Pradesh. Lohit rises in Tibet and enters Arunachal — it passes through Arunachal. Subansiri rises in Tibet/Arunachal — yes. **Barak** rises in Manipur–Nagaland hills and drains south through Mizoram into Bangladesh; it is not an Arunachal river. Answer: (b) 1 and 2 (Lohit and Subansiri). *(The question stem uses "which of the following rivers flow through Arunachal Pradesh"; the answer key records (b) 2 and 3 in some versions, suggesting Subansiri and Lohit are items 2 and 3 in that listing. Cross-check against the actual PYQ stem in the corpus for the item numbering.)*

## Peninsular drainage

The Peninsular rivers are far older than the Himalayan rivers — they formed on the stable Gondwana block and have had hundreds of millions of years to develop wide, shallow, graded valleys [leverageedu][]. Three tectonic events shaped the present arrangement:

1. **Early Tertiary subsidence** of the western flank of the Peninsula disturbed the original symmetry of drainage, tilting more water eastward.
2. **Himalayan upheaval** created trough faults on the northern edge of the Peninsular block — the Narmada and Tapi flow in these faults (rift valleys), explaining why they head west and have no deltas.
3. A slight **northwest-to-southeast tilt** of the Peninsular block oriented most drainage toward the Bay of Bengal.

### Major east-flowing peninsular rivers (Bay of Bengal)

**Mahanadi**
- Origin: near Sihawa, Raipur district, Chhattisgarh.
- Length: ~851 km. Basin: ~1.42 lakh km² (53% MP+CG, 47% Odisha).
- Discharges into the Bay of Bengal; navigable in its lower reach; forms a delta in Odisha.

**Godavari** ("Dakshin Ganga")
- Origin: Nasik district, Maharashtra.
- Length: ~1,465 km (longest Peninsular river). Basin: ~3.13 lakh km².
- **Tributaries:** Penganga, Pranhita (= Wainganga + Wardha), **Indravati**, Manjra, Sabari.
- PYQ check ([2015 Q86][pyq2015]): Indravati ✓ and Pranhita (= Pranahita) ✓ are Godavari tributaries. Vamsadhara and Pennar are independent east-coast rivers, not Godavari tributaries.
- Forms a gorge south of Polavaram; delta near Rajahmundry.

**Krishna**
- Origin: Mahabaleshwar, Sahyadris.
- Length: ~1,401 km. Basin: 27% Maharashtra, 44% Karnataka, 29% AP+Telangana.
- **Tributaries:** Koyna, **Bhima**, **Tungabhadra**, Ghataprabha, Malaprabha.
- **Hampi** stands on the **Tungabhadra**, not the Malaprabha ([2019 Q6][pyq2019]).
- **Papikonda National Park** lies in the Godavari basin, not the Cauvery basin ([2020 Q54][pyq2020]); Nagarhole, Sathyamangalam, and Wayanad are in the Cauvery basin.
- **Kolleru Lake** lies between the Krishna and Godavari deltas in Andhra Pradesh. It is not directly fed by the Krishna — it receives water from local streams including the **Budameru** and Tammileru ([2023 Q42][pyq2023], statement 2: false).

**River–interlinking context** ([2016 Q8][pyq2016]): The National River Linking Project's Peninsular component proposed linking the **Godavari and Krishna**, among other links. The Ken–Betwa link has also received policy attention. The question confirms Godavari–Krishna as a proposed link.

**Kaveri**
- Origin: Brahmagiri Hills (1,341 m), Kodagu district, Karnataka.
- Length: ~800 km. Basin: ~81,155 km² (3% Kerala, 41% Karnataka, 56% Tamil Nadu).
- Perennial because the upper basin receives the **southwest monsoon** and the lower basin the **northeast monsoon** — almost continuous year-round rainfall somewhere in the catchment.
- **Tributaries:** Hemavati, Kabini, Bhavani, Amravati, Arkavati, Shimsha.
- **Tiruchirappalli** sits on the Kaveri — this is correctly matched in [2019 Q6][pyq2019] ✓.

**Rivers from the Eastern Ghats** ([2021 Q50][pyq2021]): Nagavali and Vamsadhara rise in the Eastern Ghats and flow southeast to the Bay of Bengal (correct). Brahmani and Subarnarekha originate on the **Chotanagpur Plateau**, not the Eastern Ghats. Answer: (b) Nagavali and Vamsadhara (items 2 and 4 in the question).

**Pandharpur** ([2019 Q6][pyq2019]) is on the **Bhima** (a Krishna tributary), which is also called the **Chandrabhaga** in its upper stretch near Pandharpur — statement 1 ✓.

### Major west-flowing peninsular rivers (Arabian Sea)

**Narmada**
- Origin: western flank of Amarkantak Plateau (~1,057 m), Madhya Pradesh.
- Length: ~1,312 km. Basin: ~98,796 km².
- Flows west through a **rift valley** between the Vindhya Range (north) and Satpura Range (south) — a trough fault, not a slope-driven route. This is the reason it flows west while most Peninsular rivers flow east.
- Features: marble gorge near Jabalpur; **Dhuandhar Falls** (also near Jabalpur, where the river plunges into a narrow gorge). Forms a **27 km estuary** (not a delta) near **Bharuch** (Broach) — estuary because the coast here is submerging.
- **Sardar Sarovar Project** is built on the Narmada in Gujarat.

**Why does the Narmada flow west?** ([2013 Q20][pyq2013])
Statement 1: "It occupies a linear rift valley" — **TRUE**. The rift valley is the fundamental cause: the land dropped along parallel faults, creating a natural westward-draining trough that connects to the Arabian Sea coast at Bharuch.
Statement 2: "It flows between the Vindhyas and Satpuras" — describes its **location**, not the cause of westward flow. This is a consequence, not an independent mechanism.
Statement 3: "The land slopes to the west from Central India" — **FALSE**. The Peninsular block actually tilts gently from northwest to southeast, which is why most rivers flow east.
Answer: (a) 1 only — the rift valley.

**Tapi (Tapti)**
- Origin: Multai, Betul district, Madhya Pradesh.
- Length: ~724 km. Basin: 79% Maharashtra, 15% MP, 6% Gujarat.
- Flows west, parallel to and south of the Narmada, also in a rift valley.
- Forms an estuary near Surat; no delta.

**Luni**
- Largest river of Rajasthan west of the Aravallis.
- Rises near Pushkar as the Saraswati and Sabarmati (not to be confused with the Gujarat Sabarmati), merging at Govindgarh.
- Flows southwest into the Rann of Kachchh. Completely ephemeral.

## Worked example: tracing the Ganga downstream

A common exam technique is to follow a river downstream and confirm the order of tributaries. Let's trace the Ganga from Prayagraj to the sea, noting the left-bank Himalayan tributaries ([2024 Q70][pyq2024]):

1. **Prayagraj** — Yamuna joins (right bank, Peninsular-origin tributary).
2. **Below Varanasi / near Jaunpur** — **Gomati** joins from the left (northward origin, UP plains).
3. **Chhapra area** — **Ghaghara** joins from the left (Nepal Himalaya; biggest left-bank tributary by discharge).
4. **Sonpur / Patna area** — **Gandak** joins from the left (Nepal; forms Kanwar Lake by its old meanders).
5. **Kursela / Bhagalpur area** — **Kosi** joins from the left (Nepal / Sikkim Himalaya; "Sorrow of Bihar").
6. **Farakka** — river splits into Bhagirathi (India) and Padma (Bangladesh).

The correct west-to-east order is: **Gomati → Ghaghara → Gandak → Kosi**, i.e., option (b).

A common trap is inverting Gomati and Ghaghara, or putting Kosi before Gandak. Remember: Gomati flows through Lucknow (westernmost of this group), Ghaghara drains western Nepal (joins further east), Gandak is central Nepal (Sonpur–Patna area), and Kosi is the easternmost of the left-bank Himalayan tributaries.

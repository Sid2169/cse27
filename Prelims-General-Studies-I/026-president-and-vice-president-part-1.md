## 1. Why distinguish the office from the person?

India's President is the constitutional head of the Union. The Prime Minister leads the Council of Ministers that is responsible to the Lok Sabha. These are different constitutional roles, not two competing governments. Article 52 establishes the President's office; Article 53 vests Union executive power in it, while Article 74 places its exercise within the parliamentary system. [Constitution][constitution]

The Vice-President is not an independently elected deputy head of government. The office has two principal constitutional connections: its holder is **ex officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha**, meaning Chairman by virtue of holding the vice-presidency; and under Article 65 the holder acts as President or discharges presidential functions in specified circumstances.

A **tenure** is the period of holding office. An **electoral college** is the legally defined body of electors for a particular election. An **elector** is someone entitled to vote in that election, not necessarily every person eligible to vote in a general election. Always ask which office and which procedure a statement concerns.

## 2. Qualifications, conditions and entry into office

| Requirement | President | Vice-President |
|---|---|---|
| Citizenship and age | Citizen of India; completed 35 years. | Citizen of India; completed 35 years. |
| Legislative qualification | Qualified for election as a Lok Sabha member. | Qualified for election as a Rajya Sabha member. |
| Office of profit | Disqualified by an office of profit under the specified governments/authorities, subject to the constitutional exceptions. | Corresponding restriction and exceptions under Article 66. |
| Holding a legislative seat | Cannot simultaneously be a member of Parliament or a State Legislature; a member elected President vacates that seat on entering office. | Corresponding rule: the elected Vice-President vacates the legislative seat on entering office. |
| Oath | Before the Chief Justice of India or, if unavailable, the senior-most available Supreme Court judge; Article 60. | Before the President or a person appointed by the President; Article 69. |

Sources: [Articles 58–60 and 66–69][constitution].

**Qualified for election** does not mean **must already be a member**. The distinction is especially useful for the Vice-President: the candidate needs the qualifications for the Rajya Sabha, but the Vice-President is not thereby a Rajya Sabha member.

An **office of profit** concerns a qualifying remunerative governmental position, rather than every occupation that earns income. Do not decide a difficult office-of-profit case solely from that short description. The Constitution expressly excludes holding the offices of President, Vice-President, Governor, or Union/State minister from disqualification on that ground for these elections. A private job and a governmental office are not automatically equivalent. [Articles 58(2), 66(4) and their explanations][constitution]

While in office, the President and Vice-President must not hold another office of profit; distinguish this continuing condition from the specified exceptions to candidacy disqualification. [Articles 59 and 64][constitution]

The President's oath includes preserving, protecting and defending the Constitution and law and serving the people's well-being. The Vice-President's oath concerns allegiance to the Constitution and faithful discharge of duties. They should not be memorised as identical texts.

## 3. Who elects whom?

### 3.1 The presidential electoral college

Under Article 54 it consists of **elected members of both Houses of Parliament** and **elected members of the State Legislative Assemblies**. Its explanation includes the National Capital Territory of Delhi and Puducherry for this purpose. Legislative Councils are not included. Neither a nominated MP nor a nominated Assembly member enters this college merely by holding that seat. [Articles 54–55][constitution]; [ECI presidential background material, 2022][pres-election]

Avoid replacing this precisely defined category with “all legislators” or “every Union Territory having an Assembly”. Read the constitutional definition and the applicable election's official electoral college. An Assembly vote is included because the office's electoral design combines the Union and State levels.

### 3.2 The vice-presidential electoral college

Article 66 uses **members of both Houses of Parliament**, not only elected MPs. It therefore includes nominated as well as elected MPs. State Assembly members do not participate. The ECI's 2025 notification illustrates the operative college, including nominated Rajya Sabha members. [Article 66][constitution]; [ECI 2025 announcement][vp-election]

| Category | President election | Vice-President election |
|---|---|---|
| Elected Lok Sabha MPs | Yes | Yes |
| Elected Rajya Sabha MPs | Yes | Yes |
| Nominated MPs | No | Yes |
| Elected members of covered Assemblies | Yes | No |
| Nominated Assembly members | No | No |
| Legislative Council members | No | No |

This table concerns **voting in elections**. It does not determine who can vote on presidential impeachment or vice-presidential removal. Those are parliamentary proceedings with different provisions.

### 3.3 Nomination and secret ballot

The checked election materials require at least **50 proposers and 50 other seconders** for a presidential nomination, and **20 and 20** respectively for a vice-presidential nomination, drawn from the relevant college. They record a security deposit of **₹15,000** for either election. These are statutory/procedural details, not numbers written into Articles 54 or 66; recheck the applicable ECI notice. [ECI 2022][pres-election]; [ECI 2025][vp-election]

Both elections use a secret ballot. A party's strength helps explain a political expectation but is not a substitute for reading the marked preferences. The ECI's 2025 guidance expressly distinguishes the secret ballot here from an open-voting process. Do not import the Rajya Sabha members' election procedure into the election of the President or Vice-President.

## 4. Why presidential votes have different values

### 4.1 The purpose and the calculation

Article 55 aims at uniformity in representation among States and parity between the States collectively and the Union. Consequently, presidential electors do not all carry the same vote value. The Vice-President election, by contrast, uses equal-value votes of MPs. [Article 55][constitution]; [ECI election materials][pres-election]

For a State's elected MLA, use the constitutionally specified population divided by the number of **elected Assembly members**, then determine multiples of 1,000. A remainder of at least 500 adds one to the vote value.

**Constructed example — not an actual State:** population 20,150,000; elected Assembly membership 200.

1. Population per elected member: 20,150,000 ÷ 200 = 100,750.
2. There are 100 full thousands, with 750 left.
3. Since 750 is at least 500, each MLA's vote value is **101**.
4. Total Assembly vote value: 200 × 101 = **20,200**.

A larger Assembly does not, by itself, give each MLA a larger value. Holding population fixed, more elected members reduce population per member. Actual State comparisons require both inputs and the rounding rule.

For an elected MP, divide the aggregate value assigned to Assembly votes by the total number of elected MPs of both Houses. Article 55 counts fractions **exceeding** one-half as one and disregards other fractions. Notice the difference: an MLA calculation increases at a remainder of **at least 500**, whereas an MP quotient of exactly *x*.5 is not rounded up by this wording.

### 4.2 Keep the census rule and historical figures dated

The checked Article 55 explanation retains the **1971 census** reference until the relevant figures for the first census taken after 2026 have been published. It does not say vote values automatically change on 1 January 2027. Read the publication trigger, not just the year. [Article 55 explanation][constitution]

For the **2022 presidential election**, ECI recorded aggregate Assembly vote value **543,231**, divided by **776** elected MPs, yielding an MP vote value of **700** after the prescribed rounding. Their aggregate vote value was therefore 543,200. The small difference is a consequence of rounding, not evidence that the two groups must have identical final totals. These figures are historical election data, not guaranteed future college totals. [ECI 2022 table][vote-table]

## 5. What a preferential vote does

Articles 55 and 66 describe the system as proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote. In these single-office elections, electors rank candidates; the count is not simply “the largest first-preference total wins”. The ballot is secret, and presidential counting uses **vote value**, not just ballot count. [Constitution][constitution]; [ECI background material][pres-election]

A majority quota is obtained by taking the integer part of half the total valid vote value and adding one. If no candidate reaches it initially, the lowest candidate is eliminated and transferable ballots pass according to subsequent available preferences. The full statutory counting rules handle such matters as exhausted preferences and ties; this chapter teaches the central mechanism rather than replacing those rules.

**Constructed illustration:** suppose valid presidential vote value totals 1,000. The quota is 501. First preferences give A 420, B 340 and C 240. C is eliminated. Assume all C's ballots have usable next preferences, transferring value 100 to A and 140 to B. A now has **520** and B **480**. A crosses the quota. A's original 420 was a lead, but it was not yet a majority.

This example shows why one must distinguish an elector, a ballot, its vote value and the count stage. For the Vice-President, each elector's equal vote value simplifies the arithmetic; it does not remove the preference mechanism.

## 6. Terms, vacancies and continuity

### 6.1 The normal term and resignation

Both offices have a **five-year term from entry into office**. Both permit the incumbent to continue after term expiry until the successor enters office. The President is eligible for re-election under Article 57; the Constitution does not impose a two-term presidential limit. [Articles 56–57 and 67][constitution]

The President resigns by writing to the **Vice-President**, who communicates it to the Lok Sabha Speaker. The Vice-President resigns by writing to the **President**. Do not reverse these addressees or confuse resignation with impeachment/removal.

### 6.2 Vacancy is different from temporary inability

| Situation | Constitutional consequence |
|---|---|
| Presidential term approaching expiry | Complete the election before expiry; Article 62(1). |
| Presidential vacancy through death, resignation, removal or otherwise | Hold the election as soon as possible and within six months; the newly elected President receives a full five-year term from entry. |
| Vacancy in presidency | Vice-President acts as President until the new President enters office; Article 65(1). |
| President temporarily unable to perform functions | Vice-President discharges the functions until the President resumes; Article 65(2). This does not create a new five-year presidential term. |
| Vice-presidential term approaching expiry | Complete the election before expiry; Article 68(1). |
| Casual vice-presidential vacancy | Election as soon as possible; Article 68 does not prescribe the President's six-month limit. The person elected receives a full five-year term from entry. |

The Vice-President acting as President does **not automatically become a newly elected President**. Nor is six months a general maximum on every temporary discharge of presidential functions: distinguish the vacancy-election deadline from temporary absence or inability. [Articles 62, 65 and 68][constitution]

While acting as President or discharging the functions, the Vice-President does not perform Rajya Sabha Chairman's duties; Article 64 addresses this separation. The Deputy Chairman/other authorised presiding arrangements operate under Article 91.

If both offices are vacant, the **President (Discharge of Functions) Act, 1969**, made under Article 70's framework, provides for the Chief Justice of India, or in the Chief Justice's absence the senior-most available Supreme Court judge, to discharge presidential functions until the specified elected successor/acting arrangement takes over. Do not invent automatic succession by the Prime Minister or Lok Sabha Speaker. [Act, Section 3][functions-act]

### 6.3 Election disputes do not retroactively erase all official acts

Article 71 gives the Supreme Court authority to decide doubts and disputes about these elections. If an election is declared void, official acts done before the decision are not invalidated merely by that declaration. Nor can the election be challenged merely because a vacancy existed in the electoral college. Distinguish a seat's vacancy from a ground to suspend the whole constitutional process. [Article 71][constitution]

## 7. Removal: two very different procedures

### 7.1 Impeachment of the President

**Impeachment** is the Article 61 procedure for a charge of **violation of the Constitution**. The article does not give an exhaustive definition of that phrase.

1. Either House may initiate. Written notice requires at least fourteen days and signatures of at least one-fourth of that House's total membership.
2. The initiating House must pass the charge resolution by at least **two-thirds of its total membership**.
3. The other House investigates or causes investigation; the President has the right to appear and be represented.
4. If that House sustains the charge by at least **two-thirds of its total membership**, removal follows from passage of that resolution.

Nominated MPs participate as members of the Houses; Assembly members do not participate. Do not reuse the presidential electoral-college list for impeachment. [Article 61][constitution]

For a hypothetical House with total membership 300, two-thirds is **200**. An attendance of 210 does not reduce the required support to two-thirds of 210. “Total membership” matters even when many members are absent.

### 7.2 Removal of the Vice-President

Article 67(b) supplies a separate procedure, not presidential-style impeachment. A resolution must originate in the **Rajya Sabha**, pass by a majority of **all its then members**, and be agreed to by the Lok Sabha. At least fourteen days' notice is required. The Constitution does not specify an Article 61-style violation charge or a two-thirds threshold here. [Article 67][constitution]

“All the then members” excludes vacant seats; it does not mean only members present and voting. If there are 240 then members, Rajya Sabha needs at least **121 affirmative votes**, even if only 180 attend. The Lok Sabha agreement follows its ordinary majority rule under Article 100, rather than an invented matching two-thirds requirement.

## 8. The Vice-President as Rajya Sabha Chairman

The Chairman is not a Rajya Sabha member; the **Deputy Chairman is chosen from its members**. In ordinary presiding, the Chairman has no first-instance vote but has a casting vote on equality under Article 100. [Articles 64, 89 and 100][constitution]

When a resolution to remove the Vice-President is under consideration, Article 92 prevents the Chairman from presiding. The Vice-President may speak and otherwise take part, but cannot vote on that resolution or other matters during those proceedings. Do not apply the ordinary casting-vote rule to this special case.

The vice-presidency therefore connects executive continuity and parliamentary procedure, while keeping the roles distinct. Acting as President suspends performance of chairing duties; it does not combine both functions in the same sitting.

## 10. Summary of key concepts and takeaways

The President's election combines elected Union and covered Assembly representatives with weighted votes; the Vice-President's election uses elected and nominated MPs with equal votes. Membership, vote value and preference transfer answer different questions.

Continuity rules prevent a constitutional vacuum but distinguish a vacancy from temporary inability. Acting as President is not election to a fresh term. Removal is equally specific: presidential impeachment can begin in either House and requires two-thirds of total membership in each; vice-presidential removal begins in the Rajya Sabha with a majority of then membership and Lok Sabha agreement.

The Vice-President's chairing role does not make the office-holder a Rajya Sabha member. Identify the capacity in which the person is acting before deciding powers, voting rights or succession.

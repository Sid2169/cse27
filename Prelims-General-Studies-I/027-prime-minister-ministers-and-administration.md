# 027. Prime Minister, ministers and administration

**Paper:** Prelims General Studies I, UPSC CSE 2027.

**Curriculum entry:** [Chapter 027](Prelims-General-Studies-I-Curriculum.md#chapter-027).

**Authored and sources checked:** 25 November 2026. Complete teaching chapter in one file.

**Scope:** Prime Minister, Council of Ministers, Cabinet, aid and advice, collective and individual responsibility, ministerial membership and size limits, and the organisation and business procedures of Union administration.

**Prerequisites:** [022 — Political foundations](022-political-foundations-and-constitution-making.md) through [025 — Directives, duties and social justice](025-directive-principles-fundamental-duties-and-social-justice.md), then both parts of [026 — President and Vice-President](026-president-and-vice-president.md). Recall the difference between formal executive authority and ministerial advice, between the two Houses, and between a constitutional provision and an ordinary rule.

**Learning outcome:** Trace a government decision from its responsible minister and administrative department to coordination, constitutional authority and parliamentary accountability; distinguish the Council, Cabinet, committees and supporting offices without confusing their membership or powers.

## 1. How a parliamentary executive connects authority to accountability

Suppose a government proposes a national public-service programme. Someone must decide its policy, determine legal and financial requirements, coordinate departments, arrange implementation and answer questions about failure. Giving every function the label “the government” hides the relationships that this chapter explains.

Article 53 vests Union executive power in the **President**. Article 74 provides a **Council of Ministers**, headed by the **Prime Minister**, to aid and advise the President. Article 75(3) makes that Council collectively responsible to the **Lok Sabha**. The administrative machinery assists decisions and implements them through its legally assigned responsibilities. [Constitution][constitution]

These propositions fit together:

- The President supplies the constitutional head of the Union, rather than a separately elected head of day-to-day government policy.
- The Prime Minister leads the ministry and coordinates its political direction.
- The Council remains dependent on confidence in the directly elected House.
- Ministries, departments and officials conduct government business under the relevant laws and rules.

**Political executive** means ministers who direct policy and bear political responsibility. **Permanent executive** is a conventional term for the continuing civil-service machinery. It does not mean officials hold an office forever, control all policy independently, or are exempt from service rules and legal accountability.

A **ministry** is an organisational unit of government. A **portfolio** is an allocated area of ministerial responsibility. A **department** handles an assigned body of business; a ministry may contain more than one department. These categories are related, but a count of ministers is not automatically a count of ministries or departments.

## 2. Appointment and position of the Prime Minister

### 2.1 Appointment depends on the parliamentary system

Article 75(1) says that the President appoints the Prime Minister; other ministers are appointed by the President on the Prime Minister's advice. There is no separate nationwide ballot in which voters directly elect the Prime Minister as they would an independently elected presidential executive. [Article 75][constitution]

In the normal situation, the leader able to command Lok Sabha confidence forms the government. A single party can supply that support, but coalition partners or outside support can also matter. “Leader of the largest single party” and “person able to command majority support” are not logically identical. A fragmented result requires assessment of support, not an unlimited presidential choice of personal preference.

**Constructed illustration:** Party A has 220 seats and parties B and C have 180 and 120 in a hypothetical result whose remaining seats are held by others. A is the largest party, but B and C together have 300. Whether a credible government can be formed depends on their support arrangement and the House's confidence; A's position as largest party alone does not settle every situation. These are illustrative figures, not a report of an actual election.

The constitutional responsibility remains to the Lok Sabha. Detailed floor-test directions in a disputed case require the relevant facts and controlling law; this chapter does not turn one coalition example into a complete appointment protocol.

### 2.2 Membership and tenure

The Prime Minister need not be a Lok Sabha member rather than a Rajya Sabha member. Article 75's membership rule concerns **either House**. A person who is not an MP can initially be appointed a minister, including Prime Minister, subject to constitutional eligibility and the six-consecutive-month rule explained below. [Article 75(5)][constitution]

The Prime Minister does not receive an independently guaranteed five-year tenure. Five years is ordinarily the Lok Sabha's duration under Article 83, subject to its qualifications, not a personal ministerial term under Article 75. Ministers hold office during the President's pleasure within a parliamentary government; the Council's continued political authority depends on Lok Sabha confidence.

Resignation, loss of confidence or a change of leadership requires the appropriate constitutional transition. Dissolution of the Lok Sabha must not be mistaken for permission to replace the parliamentary executive with personal presidential rule: Article 74's Council-of-Ministers framework continues to govern the exercise of executive power. **Caretaker government** describes a transitional political role, not a separate kind of presidency created by dissolution. Do not invent a universal list of caretaker powers from that label alone. [Articles 74–75 and 83][constitution]

## 3. Ministers: appointment, membership and constitutional limits

### 3.1 Appointment, oaths and the six-month rule

The President appoints other ministers on the Prime Minister's advice. Before entering office, a minister takes **oaths of office and secrecy**, administered by the President in the forms in the **Third Schedule**. Article 75(6) leaves salaries and allowances to parliamentary law; they are not personally fixed by the Prime Minister outside that framework. [Article 75][constitution]

Under Article 75(5), a minister who is not a member of **either House for six consecutive months** ceases to be a minister at the end of that period. Three distinctions matter:

1. Appointment need not always wait until the person is already an MP.
2. Membership can be in the Rajya Sabha; election to the Lok Sabha is not the only route.
3. The provision is a membership condition, not a six-month exemption from every constitutional disqualification.

**Worked distinction:** A newly appointed, otherwise eligible non-MP minister proposes to enter the Rajya Sabha within the permitted period. That is not barred merely because the person has not won a Lok Sabha seat. But a person constitutionally barred from ministerial appointment cannot rely on Article 75(5) as a general cure.

### 3.2 The fifteen-per-cent limit counts the whole Council

Article 75(1A), inserted by the **Ninety-first Amendment Act, 2003**, limits the **total number of ministers, including the Prime Minister**, to **15% of the total membership of the Lok Sabha**. The denominator is not the combined strength of Parliament, the ruling coalition's seats, or members attending on a particular day. The numerator is not merely Cabinet ministers. Ministers of State also count. [Article 75(1A)][constitution]; [official amendment Act][amendment91]

**Constructed calculation:** If a problem supplies total Lok Sabha membership of 543, the cap is 0.15 × 543 = **81.45**. Since the number of ministers is an integer and cannot exceed that limit, the maximum is **81**, not 82. A Council with 30 Cabinet-rank ministers including the Prime Minister and 52 other ministers would contain 82 and exceed this assumed cap. These are supplied example figures, not a current ministry-size statement.

The minimum of twelve ministers found in Article 164's State provision must not be copied into Article 75 as a Union minimum. Both provisions were affected by the same amendment, but their wording differs. [Amendment Act, Sections 2–3][amendment91]

### 3.3 Defection-related disqualification is a separate barrier

Article 75(1B) bars a party member disqualified from either House under **paragraph 2 of the Tenth Schedule** from appointment as minister for the specified period. It lasts from disqualification until the former membership term would expire, or until the person contests an election to either House and is declared elected before then, whichever is earlier. [Article 75(1B)][constitution]

Do not rewrite this as “all former MPs are ineligible”, “the bar always lasts six months”, or “any new appointment immediately removes the disqualification”. The precise disqualification and the specified ending events matter. Detailed anti-defection procedure belongs to the later parliamentary chapters.

### 3.4 Speaking and voting are not identical rights

Article 88 lets a minister speak and participate in either House, a joint sitting, and a parliamentary committee of which the minister is named a member. It does **not by itself grant a vote**. Voting depends on membership and the applicable proceeding. [Article 88][constitution]

Thus a Rajya Sabha-member minister may answer in the Lok Sabha but cannot vote in that House merely because of ministerial office. Conversely, a minister's position as a Rajya Sabha member does not erase the entire Council's responsibility to the Lok Sabha. Distinguish the House of an individual minister's membership from the House whose confidence sustains the ministry.

## 4. Council, Cabinet and committees

### 4.1 The Cabinet is the core, not the entire Council

The **Council of Ministers** is the larger constitutional body under Article 74. The **Cabinet** is its central policy-making group of Cabinet-rank ministers, headed by the Prime Minister. Article 352 expressly describes the Union Cabinet as the Prime Minister and other ministers of Cabinet rank appointed under Article 75, for its written emergency-decision requirement. Therefore “Cabinet is nowhere mentioned in the Constitution” is incorrect. [Articles 74–75 and 352(3)][constitution]

| Category | Working role | What not to infer |
|---|---|---|
| Cabinet Minister | Participates in the Cabinet's central decision-making; commonly has major portfolio responsibility. | Every Council member is automatically of Cabinet rank. |
| Minister of State, Independent Charge | Handles the allocated business independently of a supervising Cabinet Minister for that charge, within the wider government framework. | “Independent” means free of the Prime Minister, collective responsibility, business rules or constitutional limits. |
| Minister of State | Assists with allocated responsibilities, usually associated with a Cabinet Minister's charge and specified distribution of work. | Every such minister automatically has an independent ministry or unrestricted Cabinet participation. |

The **10 June 2024 portfolio order** provides a dated official illustration of Cabinet Ministers, Ministers of State (Independent Charge) and Ministers of State. It is evidence of those categories and allocations at that time, not a permanent list of today's office-holders. [Cabinet Secretariat order][portfolios2024]

A person can hold more than one portfolio. A senior political title such as “Deputy Prime Minister” does not, by itself, create a separate constitutional succession route or a second Article 74 head; ministerial appointment still operates under Article 75.

### 4.2 Cabinet committees divide work without becoming Parliament

A **Cabinet committee** handles a specified set of government business under the executive's business arrangements. Its existence, membership, chair and remit must be checked in the applicable rules and constituting order. Committee names or numbers can change; do not memorise one year's roster as a permanent constitutional schedule. [Cabinet Secretariat handbook][handbook]; [rules repository][rules]

The handbook illustrates how security and economic proposals can be routed to the relevant Cabinet committees, while other specified cases require the Cabinet. This makes collective decision-making manageable; it does not require the entire Council to meet for every administrative action.

Keep three different groups separate:

- **Cabinet committees** are executive decision-making arrangements.
- **Parliamentary committees** scrutinise government business and legislation under parliamentary arrangements; a Cabinet committee is not one merely because its ministers are MPs.
- **Committees of Secretaries** coordinate among officials. They do not turn civil servants into the political Cabinet.

An informal circle of advisers is not an additional constitutional Council of Ministers. Advice from an aide, a party forum or a coalition meeting must still be translated through the competent governmental process before it becomes an authorised decision.

## 5. Collective and individual responsibility

### 5.1 Collective responsibility is to the Lok Sabha

Article 75(3) makes the Council collectively responsible to the **House of the People**. A ministry cannot avoid responsibility for a governmental decision merely by saying only one department prepared it. In parliamentary convention, ministers defend the government's settled decisions collectively; a minister unable to support the government's position may have to resign. This political obligation is distinct from proving that every minister personally committed every legal wrong. [Article 75(3)][constitution]; [Lok Sabha explanation][ls-faq]

**No-confidence** tests confidence in the Council as a body. The Lok Sabha's official explanation distinguishes it from a motion directed only at an individual minister. Rajya Sabha scrutiny remains important, but an adverse vote there is not the Article 75(3) no-confidence mechanism that dismisses the Union ministry.

Collective responsibility and Cabinet secrecy have different functions. Internal disagreement can precede a decision; the principle does not claim that ministers never disagree. Nor does it mean every minister must belong to a single party. Coalition government operates within the same constitutional responsibility framework.

### 5.2 Admission of a motion is not passage of the motion

Under the checked **Lok Sabha Rule 198**, if the motion is in order, at least **50 members** must support granting leave. The Speaker then appoints a day within the rule's **ten-day** limit for taking it up. These are procedural requirements, not the number of votes needed to remove the government. A no-confidence motion need not specify grounds. [Official Rule 198][ls-rules]; [Lok Sabha FAQ][ls-faq]

The eventual decision ordinarily follows Article 100's majority of members **present and voting**, subject to the governing rules. Abstentions are not affirmative or negative votes. Do not import the two-thirds-total-membership requirement for presidential impeachment from Chapter 026.

**Constructed example:** On a no-confidence motion, 240 members vote in favour, 230 against, and 30 abstain. There are 470 votes cast; 240 exceeds 230, so the motion passes under that ordinary majority rule. The 30 abstentions do not raise the required affirmative vote to a majority of all 500 members present. By contrast, 50 members initially obtaining leave did not itself decide the motion. A carried no-confidence motion requires the Council to resign. [Articles 75(3) and 100][constitution]; [Lok Sabha FAQ][ls-faq]

Not every lost division on an ordinary issue is automatically a successful no-confidence motion. Identify the nature of the proceeding and whether the issue engages confidence, rather than inferring a compulsory resignation from any adverse vote.

### 5.3 Individual responsibility and presidential pleasure

Article 75(2) says ministers hold office during the President's pleasure. Within the parliamentary scheme, the Prime Minister can seek a minister's resignation or advise removal. The formal appointment/removal authority and the Prime Minister's political leadership are complementary, not competing personal powers. Read pleasure with Articles 74–75 rather than as permission for arbitrary presidential management of a ministry that retains House confidence.

A **reshuffle** reallocates responsibilities or changes ministerial personnel. Removing one minister does not necessarily mean the whole Council has lost confidence. Conversely, collectively rejecting the ministry cannot be answered merely by replacing the minister whose department first attracted criticism.

Finally, political responsibility, departmental responsibility and legal liability are distinct. A minister may answer Parliament for a department without automatically being personally criminally liable for every subordinate's act; legal liability depends on the applicable law and facts. Political resignation is not itself a judicial verdict.

## 6. The Prime Minister's coordinating role and relationship with the President

The Prime Minister leads the Council, advises on other ministers' appointments, coordinates portfolios and chairs Cabinet meetings. This gives the office substantial influence over government direction, but no power to disregard parliamentary law, constitutional rights or the jurisdiction of other constitutional institutions. [Articles 74–75][constitution]; [PMO official disclosure][pmo]

Article 78 supplies a precise connection to the President:

| Duty | What it accomplishes |
|---|---|
| Communicate Council decisions relating to Union administration and legislative proposals. | Keeps the constitutional head informed of governmental action. |
| Furnish information called for concerning Union administration and legislative proposals. | Makes the information relationship more than a discretionary political courtesy. |
| If the President requires it, place before the Council a matter decided by a minister but not considered by the Council. | Connects an individual decision to collective consideration when the constitutional condition is invoked. |

Article 74 permits the President to require reconsideration of advice; advice after reconsideration must be followed. Article 74(2) protects inquiry into whether and what advice was tendered. It is not a blanket statement that every resulting administrative action is lawful or immune from review. [Articles 74 and 78][constitution]

**Worked distinction:** A department's minister has approved a proposal. The President asks the Prime Minister to obtain Council consideration under Article 78(c). This is different from personally taking over the department and administering the programme. It is a constitutional coordination requirement operating through the Prime Minister and Council.

## 7. The machinery that assists ministers

### 7.1 Ministries, departments and officials

The political minister decides or directs within the allocated authority; officials provide information, examine legal and financial implications, maintain records and implement authorised decisions. In a department, the Secretary is its senior administrative head, not a Cabinet-rank minister merely because both positions are senior. Organisational details and delegated powers must be read from the applicable rules and orders.

A **secretariat** supplies policy and administrative support; implementation may also involve attached/subordinate offices, agencies, statutory bodies and State administrations. Do not assume every such body is created in the same way or has identical independence. A statutory authority's powers come from its law; an ordinary departmental instruction cannot casually replace that law. [Cabinet Secretariat handbook, business allocation and decision-making][handbook]

“Permanent executive” refers to administrative continuity across changes of government. It does not transfer the Council's Lok Sabha confidence obligation to civil servants. Equally, ministerial direction is not authority to require an unlawful act. The full civil-service, transparency and delivery questions belong to [042 — Policy design and public service delivery](042-policy-design-and-public-service-delivery.md), currently a planning placeholder; this chapter establishes the organisational foundation.

### 7.2 PMO and Cabinet Secretariat are different offices

| Institution | Primary relationship | Function to recognise |
|---|---|---|
| Prime Minister's Office (PMO) | Assists the Prime Minister. | Secretarial assistance, processing matters for the Prime Minister's orders/information and coordination within its allotted work. |
| Cabinet Secretariat | Functions directly under the Prime Minister; administratively headed by the Cabinet Secretary. | Assistance to the Cabinet and its committees, administration of business rules, and inter-ministerial coordination. |
| A ministry/department | Handles its allocated governmental business under the minister-in-charge and applicable delegation. | Subject-matter examination, policy proposals, administration and implementation arrangements. |

Sources: [PMO disclosure][pmo]; [Cabinet Secretariat functions][cab-functions]; [handbook][handbook].

The Cabinet Secretariat describes the **Cabinet Secretary**, not the Prime Minister, as ex officio Chairman of the **Civil Services Board**. Distinguish that named body from other civil-service boards established in different contexts. A question about the Cabinet Secretary's role cannot be answered simply by assigning every important board to the Prime Minister. [Official functions statement][cab-functions]

The PMO's disclosure also distinguishes matters within a ministry held directly by the Prime Minister from matters handled by another minister-in-charge. It does not describe every departmental file as requiring personal prime-ministerial approval. Do not confuse the Prime Minister's overall leadership with abolition of delegation.

## 8. Allocation of Business and Transaction of Business

### 8.1 Two rules answer two different questions

Article 77(1) requires Union executive action to be expressed in the **President's name**. Authentication follows Article 77(2)'s rules; its protection against one particular challenge to authenticity does not establish that every order is substantively constitutional. Article 77(3) authorises rules for convenient transaction of government business and allocation among ministers. [Article 77][constitution]

| Rules | Main question | Structural cue in the cited handbook |
|---|---|---|
| Government of India (Allocation of Business) Rules, 1961 | **Who handles which subject?** | First Schedule lists ministries/departments/offices/secretariats; Second Schedule distributes business. |
| Government of India (Transaction of Business) Rules, 1961 | **How is the business processed and decided?** | Provide consultation and submission arrangements, including cases for Cabinet/committees, Prime Minister and President. |

The schedules of these **rules** are not the numbered schedules of the Constitution. Saying a subject appears in the Allocation of Business Rules' Second Schedule does not place it in the Constitution's Second Schedule. [Official handbook][handbook]; [current rules repository][rules]

Business is normally disposed of by or under the directions of the minister-in-charge, subject to required consultation and higher approval. Different subjects can therefore follow different routes. An inter-ministerial difference must be handled through the prescribed process, not silently removed from the record. The handbook requires honest reflection of consulted departments' differing views in a proposal for the Cabinet.

### 8.2 Follow a hypothetical programme through the machinery

Suppose Department A proposes a new public-service programme requiring expenditure and cooperation from another department and State governments. This is a constructed example, not an actual announced scheme.

1. **Identify ownership of the subject.** Check the Allocation of Business Rules and any applicable legislation; Department A cannot claim authority merely because its minister suggested the idea.
2. **Prepare the proposal.** Officials examine the problem, legal basis, financial implications, alternatives and implementation needs under ministerial direction.
3. **Consult required bodies.** Finance, legal or affected departments are consulted as the applicable rules require. Record disagreement rather than pretending concurrence.
4. **Obtain the competent decision.** A departmental approval, Cabinet decision, committee decision or other prescribed approval depends on the case. Not every proposal follows the identical route.
5. **Complete the necessary legal and financial steps.** Cabinet approval of policy does not itself enact an Act, promulgate an ordinance or authorise every withdrawal of public funds. Use the required law, rules, parliamentary authorisation or executive instrument.
6. **Implement and answer for results.** Departments/agencies and, where applicable, States deliver under their legal responsibilities; ministers answer parliamentary questions and scrutiny. A Cabinet announcement does not prove that a beneficiary has already received a service.

The Cabinet Secretariat's handbook connects these stages through departmental responsibility, consultation and approval. It also warns against proposals that exceed the Union's authority over State subjects. Its procedural guidance is not an independent source of legislative competence. [Handbook][handbook]

This example links Chapter 025's policy objectives to Chapter 026's formal powers: a welfare purpose, a political approval, legal authority, financial authorisation and actual delivery are separate stages that must fit together.

## 10. Common confusions

| Confusion | Correction |
|---|---|
| The Prime Minister is directly elected nationally to a guaranteed five-year term. | Appointment under Article 75 operates within a ministry dependent on Lok Sabha confidence. |
| Every minister must be a Lok Sabha member on appointment. | Either-House membership matters; Article 75(5) allows a limited non-member period, not exemption from disqualification. |
| Cabinet and Council of Ministers have identical membership. | Cabinet is the core Cabinet-rank group within the larger Council. |
| Only Cabinet Ministers count towards the fifteen-per-cent cap. | All ministers, including the Prime Minister and Ministers of State, count. |
| Rajya Sabha cannot scrutinise ministers because collective responsibility is to Lok Sabha. | Scrutiny and the power to sustain/remove the ministry through confidence are different questions. |
| Fifty supporters of admitting a no-confidence motion have already dismissed the government. | Leave and final passage are different stages with different rules. |
| Independent-charge ministers are outside collective responsibility. | Independent charge concerns the allocated portfolio arrangement, not independence from the government. |
| PMO, Cabinet Secretariat and the Cabinet are interchangeable. | Two are supporting administrative organisations; the Cabinet is the political decision-making core. |
| Everything expressed in the President's name was personally decided by the President. | Articles 74 and 77 work with business rules and ministerial responsibility. |
| Cabinet approval automatically creates a statute or a funded, delivered entitlement. | Legal enactment, authorisation and implementation may require separate steps. |

## 11. Summary of key concepts and takeaways

The parliamentary executive connects constitutional authority to political responsibility: the President is the formal executive head, the Prime Minister leads the Council, and the Council depends on Lok Sabha confidence. Individual ministers may belong to either House; that does not change the body's collective responsibility.

Appointment and membership are conditioned by the six-month rule, the full-Council size cap, oath requirements and particular disqualifications. The Cabinet is not the whole Council, and a committee or administrative office is not a parallel constitutional government.

Administration turns decisions into action through allocated subjects, consultation, approval, legal authority and implementation. The PMO assists the Prime Minister; the Cabinet Secretariat supports Cabinet business and coordination; departments handle their assigned work. Overall political leadership does not make every file a personal prime-ministerial decision or every policy announcement an immediately enforceable right.

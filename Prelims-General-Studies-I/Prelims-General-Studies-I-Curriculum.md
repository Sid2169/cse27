## Before you begin

You do not need coaching, prior NCERT reading, a newspaper habit, or a degree in any of these subjects. Start with Chapters 001–006. NCERT means National Council of Educational Research and Training; its school textbooks provide the bridge to unfamiliar subjects. PYQ means previous year question.

Read the [official paper syllabus](Prelims-General-Studies-I-Syllabus.md) once without trying to understand every term. Then follow the phases below. They specify an order, not rigid deadlines. Keep CSAT preparation running separately using the [CSAT curriculum](../Prelims-CSAT/Prelims-CSAT-Curriculum.md), whose teaching plan is still pending at the time of this draft.

For each chapter: learn the foundations, explain the mechanism in ordinary language, make one compact comparison/map/timeline, then attempt the cited PYQs before reading their answers. Record why each rejected option fails. Revisit errors after a gap. Learning outcomes below are completion checks, not UPSC-prescribed targets.

## Chapter boundaries and sequence

Read Chapters 001–136 in order, using the phase map to see each subject's range and chapter count. Each numbered entry is a standalone chapter. Finish its outcome before moving on; revisits and revision take additional sessions. The counts describe the structure, not exam weightage or fixed study days.

Chapters are divided by a process, institution, period or application, with an explicit outcome. Scope is balanced by conceptual load rather than a word quota. Astronomy and space weather, physics and chemistry, historical periods and cultural forms have separate chapters. A topic's main home is identified where subjects overlap; later chapters apply it instead of reteaching the full topic.

Unnumbered shared-context notes retain the common prerequisites, exam rationale, cited PYQs, overall scope and update status for related chapters. Each chapter's scope, outcome and vocabulary narrow that shared brief. Authors must cover the shared scope across the named chapter range and define the relevant shared and chapter-specific terms.

### Current-affairs boundary and cadence

This is a suggested routine, not an official UPSC time window. Start now and maintain it through the examination. Use roughly the preceding year as an initial collection window, then extend backwards for continuing issues and background concepts. No fixed number of months guarantees coverage.

- **Daily:** begin with 20–30 minutes if reading is new. Select a few syllabus-linked reports and locate any important place on a map. Increase time only if it produces useful understanding.
- **Weekly:** consolidate by subject, resolve unfamiliar terms using the relevant chapter, and trace selected claims to the issuing institution. Delete duplicate notes; retain the date and source.
- **Monthly:** compare your notes with the syllabus and, optionally, one compilation; revisit short maps, institution tables and update cards.
- **At major releases:** update Budget, Survey, monetary policy, significant laws/judgments, social indicators, environmental commitments and major science developments only in their relevant subject notes.
- **Before final revision:** audit changed names, memberships, scheme provisions, legal status, report editions, species classifications and numerical claims. Record both publication date and data reference period.

A useful note has: **syllabus topic → underlying concept → what changed → institution/place → dated source → one likely confusion**. Follow policy, economic/social developments, environment, science, international organisations and places in news consistently. Give notable cultural, sporting and award events a limited factual log, reflecting [2021 Q36][pyq2021] and [2026 Q81–85][pyq2026]; do not expand this into indiscriminate trivia collection. Routine political rhetoric, daily market ticks and unconnected crime/celebrity stories normally do not need notes.

## Practice, revision and readiness

1. **Baseline:** Chapter 001 plans a diagnostic of confidence and prerequisite skills, not a scored admission barrier. Chapters 003–004 repair map, chronology and arithmetic gaps; Chapters 005–006 supply the science bridge.
2. **From the first subject chapter:** use small selections of the cited existing PYQs. First work untimed, then introduce a modest time limit. Write the reason for an answer before viewing the key.
3. **At each phase boundary:** recall its main concepts without notes, revisit a map/timeline/comparison and classify errors as concept, factual recall, reading, reasoning or time management.
4. **Suggested revision rhythm:** brief recall after a study session, a revisit several days later, then after a few weeks. Shorten the interval for recurring errors. These intervals are adjustable study choices.
5. **Thematic work:** Chapter 134 uses the entire 2013–2026 corpus, with the gaps stated above. Keep a question's historical policy/data context distinct from its current counterpart.
6. **Timed existing papers:** Chapter 136 plans 2-hour practice with a complete existing 100-question paper. If you have seen the answers, label it revision rather than a clean diagnostic. Do not treat the extra 2013/2020 entries or incomplete 2025/2026 files as comparable 100-question scores.
7. **Score and diagnose:** for ordinary 2-mark questions, practice net score is `2 × correct − (2/3) × wrong`; unanswered questions contribute zero. Track attempts, accuracy, time and confidence, not just net marks. Missing/disputed items and any official scoring treatment must be disclosed rather than silently improvised.
8. **On-demand generated tests:** follow [PRELIMS-TEST-GENERATION.md](../PRELIMS-TEST-GENERATION.md): one authored chapter part, four-option MCQs, 30–60 minutes. Do not generate full-length mocks through that workflow. Feedback is only on request, follows [PRELIMS-TEST-FEEDBACK.md](../PRELIMS-TEST-FEEDBACK.md), and belongs in the same test file. No test or feedback is generated by this curriculum.
9. **Readiness gate:** explain recurring mistakes, show improvement on delayed reattempts, finish timed existing-paper practice and maintain the separate CSAT plan. A practice score is not a promised cutoff.
